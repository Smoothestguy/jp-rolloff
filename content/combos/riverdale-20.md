Riverdale Park was platted in 1889 as a planned railroad "villa park," its streets named for U.S. presidents and laid out in a grid around the central ellipse that once held the commuter station. The houses that filled it are old — the median home here went up in 1943, and construction started as far back as 1890 — so the work that needs a dumpster is almost never new construction. It is renovations, additions, plaster and knob-and-tube tear-out, full gut-rehabs of pre-WWII frame houses, and the flood cleanouts this riverside town knows all too well. For most of that work, the 20-yard roll-off is the size that fits: big enough for a real project, small enough to land in a driveway. It is the box we drop in Riverdale Park more than any other.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. For construction debris it includes delivery, pickup on request, a 14-day rental, and a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance — priced by material, so call for a quote. Extra days run $5/day, and weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — you call to schedule when you are ready.

The four-foot walls are the part people underestimate. They are low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but the 22-foot length swallows the volume of a serious project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes here: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but short of the 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or heavy corridor work. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret. On a standard two-car driveway, it fits with room to spare.

## Which Riverdale Park jobs suit the 20-yard

This is the size people reach for when the work is real but still contained to one home. Around Riverdale Park, a handful of jobs come up again and again:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's best-known role. The town's pre-1943 housing collects a lifetime of belongings, and clearing one of these homes between owners, or after years of accumulation, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-two-baths gut, or a run of rooms getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job in these older frame houses. The Riverdale Park Historic District — listed on the National Register in 2002 — is an eclectic mix of Queen Anne, Colonial Revival, Craftsman, Tudor Revival, American Foursquares, and bungalows, and those larger 2.5-story houses generate bigger tear-out volumes than a compact cottage. Plaster, lath, and old knob-and-tube wiring add up fast.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When a longtime family home around the historic Riversdale Mansion neighborhood changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical single-family roof tear-off on one of these older homes fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most single-layer asphalt jobs.
- **Flood and water-damage cleanouts.** This is the job that sets Riverdale Park apart. The Northeast Branch of the Anacostia River runs straight through town, and heavy storms — sometimes just three inches of rain — have repeatedly caused flash flooding that town officials have pushed the county to address. When a basement or ground floor takes on water, the soaked drywall, ruined flooring, waterlogged furniture, and dead appliances pile up quickly, and a 20-yard is usually the right size to clear it in one drop.

It helps to know that not every Riverdale Park project lands on the 20-yard. A single-room refresh or a small flood touch-up may only need a 10 or 15-yard, while a full gut-rehab of one of the larger Foursquares — taking the house down to studs — can push toward a 25 or 30. Tell us your address and the job, and we will tell you straight whether the 20 is the right call.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Lot and driveway placement.** The original town was laid out in three sections with relatively uniform lot dimensions and building setbacks, so many properties have narrow, consistent frontages and modest driveways. A 20-yard needs roughly 22 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door, so on a tighter driveway, placement is worth a quick conversation before the truck rolls. We will scout it with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**Permits.** The Town of Riverdale Park requires a permit when a roll-off sits in the public right-of-way — the street — and that typically takes one to two business days to arrange, so it is worth planning a day or two ahead if the street is your only option. Placing the box on your own driveway is typically permit-free, which is part of why we steer most residential drops there when the lot allows it. We will tell you which situation you are in when we look at your address.

**Flood-zone access.** Because the Anacostia's Northeast Branch and its floodplain cut through town — with the Northeast Branch Trail and Rhode Island Avenue Trolley Trail running about 2.5 miles along the levee — some lots near the water sit on soft or low ground with limited access. The Army Corps of Engineers reserved much of that floodplain for parkland and flood control, which trims the buildable area on nearby properties. For a heavy 20-yard on one of those lots, we plan placement carefully to keep the box on stable footing.

**Drive time.** We are just up the road in Beltsville, roughly ten minutes from most Riverdale Park addresses. Most addresses here are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run. Whether you are on a narrow historic block in the Town of Riverdale Park, near the Town Center and MARC station area, or out by the Riversdale Mansion, we know the streets and plan placement before we roll.
