## Which Riverdale Park jobs suit this size

Riverdale Park's housing stock is genuinely old: the median home was built around 1943, and dwellings here go back to 1890. That means most work in town isn't new construction. It's renovation, addition, and full gut-rehab of pre-WWII wood-frame houses. The original town was platted in 1889 as a planned railroad villa park, with presidentially named streets laid out in a grid around a central ellipse, and the Riverdale Park Historic District, listed on the National Register in 2002, holds an eclectic mix of Queen Anne, Colonial Revival, Craftsman, Tudor Revival, American Foursquares, and bungalows. The larger 2.5-story homes among them produce serious tear-out volume when you open up walls, pull plaster and lath, or strip knob-and-tube wiring. Those are exactly the projects a 25-yard is built for.

A few of the jobs where this size earns its keep around town:

- **Home additions and whole-house remodels.** When you're framing new square footage or gutting a Foursquare back to the studs, demolition debris piles up quickly. The 25-yard handles plaster, lath, old flooring, cabinetry, and framing offcuts in one container.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** The bigger historic homes here carry a lot of roof. Pulling old shingles and decking across a wide span fills volume fast, and the 2-ton allowance gives you room for the weight.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** Near the Town Center and the MARC station, the Station at Riverdale Park development brought roughly 160,000 square feet of retail and office space, a 231-unit apartment building, and 119 townhomes to the Route 1 corridor. Tenant turnovers and buildouts in that kind of space need a major-size container, and commercial accounts welcome — ask about billing.
- **Flood cleanouts.** This is Riverdale Park's distinctive driver. The Northeast Branch of the Anacostia runs straight through town, and the area is one of the most flood-prone in the DMV. Heavy storms have repeatedly caused flash flooding and pushed town officials to demand better county stormwater infrastructure. When a basement or ground floor takes on water, the cleanout is bulky and heavy: soaked drywall and insulation, ruined flooring, waterlogged furniture, appliances. The 25-yard swallows a serious flood cleanout in one drop, which is exactly what you want when you're racing to dry a house out.

If your project is closer to a single-room refresh, one of our smaller sizes will likely serve you better and cost less. The 25-yard shines when the volume is real.

## Local logistics, permits, and drive time

Placement is the main thing to plan for in Riverdale Park. The original town used relatively uniform lot dimensions and building setbacks, so many properties have narrow, consistent frontages and modest driveways, and the floodplain land along the Anacostia was reserved for parkland and flood control, which limits buildable area on some nearby lots. An 18-foot container needs a clear, reasonably flat spot. If your driveway can take it, that's the simplest option, because driveways are typically permit-free.

If the container has to sit in the public right-of-way, the Town of Riverdale Park requires a permit for that placement, and it generally takes one to two business days to process. It's worth building that lead time into your schedule so the dumpster isn't waiting on paperwork. Tell us where you're hoping to set it and we'll help you figure out whether you need the permit or can keep it on your own property.
