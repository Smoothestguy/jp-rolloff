## What fits in a 30-yard

This is the size for volume. With about nine pickup loads of capacity, the 30-yard swallows the kind of debris that would overflow a 20- or 25-yard well before the job is done. Pricing is per material — a 30-yard starts at $665 for construction debris (other materials are priced separately, and heavy aggregates like dirt, concrete, and asphalt are only offered in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard sizes). That rate includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton weight allowance; extra days are $5/day and any weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

Two things to keep in mind. First, the 6-foot walls are tall, which makes loading bulky, lightweight material easy but means heavy material adds up fast on weight — the 30-yard's strength is cubic volume, not density, so it's ideal when you've got a lot of *stuff* rather than a lot of dirt, concrete, or shingles (those heavy loads are better matched to a smaller box where you'll hit the weight cap before you run out of room). Second, that 22-foot length needs space. We need a straight, reasonably level approach to back the truck in and roll the container off, plus clearance overhead.

## Which Upper Marlboro jobs suit this size

The 30-yard earns its keep on the four jobs it was built for, and Upper Marlboro generates all four.

**Full demolition.** When a structure is coming down — an old outbuilding, a barn, a detached garage — the debris stream is large and continuous. Out in the Rural Tier around the Equestrian Center and Show Place Arena, working horse farms mean barns, paddocks, round pens, and aging outbuildings, and when one of those comes down or gets gutted, a 30-yard keeps the whole job in one container.

**Commercial site.** Upper Marlboro is the county seat, with the courthouse, County Administration Building, Board of Education, and Sheriff's headquarters all clustered in the downtown core. Commercial tenant work, office turnovers, and site cleanups in and around the county-seat district produce the steady, high-volume debris a 30-yard is sized for.

**Bulk waste haul.** The ZIP-code area around the historic core was largely built between 1970 and 1999, so much of 20772/20774 has a 1970s–80s single-family character — and whole-home cleanouts, estate clear-outs, and accumulated bulk from decades in one house fill a big box quickly. The chronic Western Branch flooding downtown is its own driver: when Water Street and the low-lying blocks take on water, flood-damage gut jobs send drywall, flooring, insulation, and ruined furnishings out by the truckload.

**Multi-family cleanout.** Town-wide, roughly 43% of homes are row/attached or townhouse stock, heavily concentrated in the newer planned communities. Turnovers and cleanouts across attached housing — and across the active builds at Westphalia Town Center, where Ryan Homes, D.R. Horton's Watermark community, Mid-Atlantic Builders, and Haverford Homes are putting up townhomes and single-family homes — generate framing scrap, packaging, and post-build debris that suits the 30-yard's volume.

## Placement, permits, and drive time

Here's where the downtown-versus-county split becomes real, especially for a container this long.

**Out in the county (most of 20772/20774).** A lot of this area is rural with large-lot, estate, and agricultural-zoned parcels — some subdivisions carry a 5-acre-per-home minimum, and tracts near Rosaryville Road and Mill Creek run into the tens of acres. Those long private driveways and big lots easily accommodate a 30-yard on private property, and private-property placement typically doesn't require a permit. If the container has to sit in the county right-of-way, though, that's a Prince George's County permit. The newer attached communities at Westphalia and Marlton are the flip side: townhouse driveways are narrow and curb space is tight, so on those jobs we'll talk through exactly where the box can land before we show up.

**Downtown in the historic core.** The Town of Upper Marlboro runs its own rules. A roll-off can sit on **private property for up to 15 days without a town permit**; you need a town permit if it stays longer than 15 days *or* if it goes in the town's right-of-way (street or sidewalk). For those details, Town Hall is at **(301) 627-6905**. The downtown streets are also part of the listed Upper Marlboro Residential Historic District and sit close to the Western Branch, so dry, firm ground and good timing matter — we don't want a heavy 30-yard parked on soft, flood-prone soil.
