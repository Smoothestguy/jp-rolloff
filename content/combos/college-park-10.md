## What fits in a 10-yard

The 10-yard is best known as the "garage cleanout" size for a reason: its low 3.5-foot walls make it easy to walk debris in over the side rather than heaving everything up and over a tall lip. At 12 x 8 x 3.5 feet it sits inside a single parking spot, so it slides onto a one-car driveway or a tight street placement without eating your whole frontage.

Think of it in pickup-truck terms — about three full loads. That is the right capacity for a single room or a focused project, and the one-ton weight allowance covers the typical mix of household junk, furniture, and light renovation debris. Heavier loads like concrete, brick, or wet plaster fill that ton fast, so if your project is mostly dense material, give us a quick call and we will tell you straight whether 10 yards is enough or whether you would be better off with a bigger box.

## Which College Park jobs suit this size

College Park's housing stock skews old and varied. Most homes were built between roughly 1940 and 1969, with Old Town and Berwyn holding houses over a century old. That mix of pre-WWII frame houses, mid-century Cape Cods, ranches, and split-levels means small, room-at-a-time projects are constant — and those are exactly what the 10-yard is built for.

- **Garage cleanout.** The signature job for this size. One car-width driveway, low walls, three truck-loads of capacity — clear out years of accumulation in a weekend without renting more box than you need.
- **Bathroom remodel.** A single bathroom's worth of old tile, vanity, tub, and fixtures fits comfortably in 10 yards. Berwyn's older bungalows and Cape Cods and the converted homes in Calvert Hills see a steady stream of these gut-and-refresh jobs.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Branches, brush, sod, and storm cleanup from the deep lots common in Old Town — where the standard parcel runs about 50 by 200 feet — drop neatly into the low 10-yard so you can pitch limbs in over the rail.
- **Estate downsize.** Clearing out a parent's home or prepping an inherited Old Town or Berwyn house for sale is rarely a 30-yard job. The 10-yard handles a measured, room-by-room downsize, and its small footprint keeps the driveway usable while the work stretches over days.

Near the UMD campus and in Calvert Hills, many of these older homes have been converted into multi-unit rentals, and the August lease-turnover cycle drives a predictable late-summer wave of move-out cleanouts and landlord turnover work. A 10-yard parked in the driveway is often all a single unit's worth of left-behind furniture and junk requires.

## Placement, permits, and drive time

Placement is the thing to plan for in College Park, more so than in the driveway-rich suburbs around it. Old Town's lots are deep but narrow, driveways tend to be single-car-width, and homes sit close to their neighbors. The 10-yard's small footprint is a real advantage here — it fits where a larger box simply won't, and its short length makes backing into a tight driveway end far easier for our driver.

Plenty of homes near campus and in North College Park have few or no driveways and lean on street parking, and several of those blocks are already permit-only for parking. Where your driveway can't take the box, street placement becomes the practical option. For a container set in the public right-of-way (the street), the **City of College Park requires a right-of-way permit, issued through Public Works.** Driveways and private lots, by the rule we work from, do not require that street permit. Permit language can change, so if you are placing on the street we are glad to talk through current requirements before we deliver — and we always recommend confirming the specifics with College Park Public Works.

As for getting to you: we dispatch from Beltsville, so a College Park delivery is a quick run straight down Route 1. That keeps our scheduling tight and lets us be flexible when a cleanout or remodel timeline shifts on you.
