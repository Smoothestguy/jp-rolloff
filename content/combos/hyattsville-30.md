## What fits in a 30-yard dumpster

The 30-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly fourteen pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material and priced by the load — heavy aggregates like dirt, concrete, and asphalt are only offered in our 10-, 15-, and 20-yard sizes — so in Hyattsville you call for a quote. Every drop **includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance**, with pickup on request — just call to schedule. Additional days run $5 per day, and any weight over the 3-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

Those tall six-foot walls, stretched across the full 22 feet of length, are what make this the maximum-capacity option. It is built for sheer volume, not for tossing a few bags over the side. The trade-off is footprint: a 30-yard needs a long, straight run-up to set down level, plus clearance for the truck to position and lift it. On an open commercial lot or a deep driveway that is no issue. On a tight historic lot, it is the first thing we check together — and sometimes the reason we will steer you toward a 25-yard instead.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is the real question here.** Hyattsville's defining challenge is its streetcar-suburb DNA. The older neighborhoods — Hyattsville Hills, Downtown, and Castle Manor — were platted before cars, on a gridiron of relatively narrow lots with rear alleys, detached garages, and a notable absence of front-yard driveways. One representative historic lot runs about 39 ft wide by 57.5 ft deep off a 15-ft alley. Unlike the auto-era subdivisions elsewhere in the DMV, you often cannot simply drop a roll-off in a wide front driveway here, and a 30-yard needs more run-up than any of our other sizes. So before we deliver, we work out the placement with you — whether the bin sits in a driveway, off the rear alley, or on the street — and we lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The permit rule.** If the 30-yard sits entirely on private property — a driveway, a back yard, or a commercial lot — you do **not** need a permit. Street or shoulder placement is different: the City of Hyattsville requires a Street Access / Right-of-Way Usage permit, with a $50 fee, before a temporary container goes on a public street, and any street-side obstruction has to be marked at night with red lanterns to warn traffic. Because so many historic-district homes lack a usable driveway, street placement — and that permit — comes up far more often here than in newer suburbs. Two things worth knowing: the City's permit and code-compliance questions go through its Office of Code Compliance at (301) 985-5032, and the rule only applies on City-maintained streets. The Hyattsville ZIP codes (20781, 20782, 20783, 20784) reach well past the city limits into unincorporated areas — parts near Chillum, Adelphi, and Mount Rainier — that fall under Prince George's County instead. We will flag which set of rules your address falls under before anything rolls off the truck.

A note on the West Hyattsville and Riverdale-border edges: lots near the Northwest Branch of the Anacostia and the trail corridor can run into soft ground and limited access, which matters with a loaded 30-yard. If your site is down near the stream valley, tell us and we will plan the set accordingly.

**Drive time.** Hyattsville is a short run from our Beltsville yard, straight down the Route 1 and Baltimore Avenue corridor, so most jobs go out inside 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run.
