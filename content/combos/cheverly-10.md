## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard is the compact end of the lineup. It measures roughly 12 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds about three pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Cheverly a 10-yard **is priced by material, so call for a quote**. Every 10-yard **includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with the 1-ton allowance** — extra days run $5/day. Pricing is per material, not a single catch-all number: weight over the 1-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton, and pickup is on request, so you call us to schedule it.

What makes the 10-yard so easy to live with is its footprint and its low walls. At 12 feet long it fits inside a single parking space, and the 4-foot sides mean you can walk right up and drop debris over the edge without a ramp or a heave. The trade-off is the weight allowance: a single ton goes fast with anything dense. That's the whole reason this size exists alongside the bigger ones — it's built for **bulky-but-light** loads from one room or one corner of the property, not for heavy demolition or a whole-house gut. For a contained cleanout or a small remodel, though, the 10-yard is the size you don't overpay for. It's the one most folks know as the "garage cleanout" size, and that name is earned.

## Which Cheverly jobs suit the 10-yard

Cheverly is a deliberately planned bedroom community — developer Robert Marshall laid it out as an "island of green," and its housing stock is dominated by detached single-family homes built largely between 1921 and the 1960s: Cape Cods, Colonial Revivals, ramblers, and mid-century ranch and split-levels. A century on, those homes are deep into their second and third round of renovations, and a lot of that work is the small, room-at-a-time kind that fits a 10-yard exactly. The jobs we drop this size for most around Cheverly:

- **Garage cleanouts.** This is the 10-yard's signature job. Decades of paint cans, broken lawn gear, old furniture, and boxes clear out of a single-family garage into one container parked right outside the door — the smallest footprint we carry, sized for a one-room clear-out without paying for space you won't use.
- **Bathroom remodels.** A single-bath gut in one of Cheverly's older houses — vanity, tub, tile, and drywall — is a textbook 10-yard load. The debris is bulky but won't blow past the one-ton allowance the way a kitchen or a roof would. (If you're working on one of the town's century-old Sears or McClure kit homes and pulling out plaster, lath, and old additions, that debris runs heavier and bulkier — for a job like that you'd likely want to step up to a 15- or 20-yard.)
- **Yard waste and trees.** Cheverly is a designated Tree City USA with a protected oak canopy and homes set on generous, tree-shaded lots. Brush, branches, hedge removal, and storm cleanup fill a 10-yard about right, and the steady canopy work here keeps this size busy.
- **Estate downsizing.** When a longtime family home in this historic community is being thinned out — clearing one room, a basement corner, or a relative's belongings — the 10-yard handles a contained sort-and-toss without the cost of a full-house container.

A quick honest word on sizing, because Cheverly's housing makes it matter. The town holds Prince George's County's largest documented collection of 1920s mail-order kit houses, and the Cheverly Historic District is on the National Register of Historic Places. Renovating one of those century-old homes — with its plaster, lath, original wood, and dated additions — tends to generate more, and heavier, debris than a single-room job, which pushes you toward a mid-size 15- or 20-yard. The 10-yard is the right call for the focused, contained, lighter work. Tell us the address and the job, and we'll say plainly whether this is the size you want or whether you'd be better served stepping up.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement.** Most 10-yards we drop in Cheverly go on the homeowner's own driveway, on these detached single-family lots — no street involved. The container needs only about 12 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, so it slots into a single car spot with room to spare. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

A couple of things set Cheverly apart from the flat, grid-platted suburbs around it, and they're worth raising before drop-off. The town was planned around its rolling, wooded contours, so driveways here often sit on a grade — we'll level the container so the door works and nothing shifts. And with that mature oak canopy and the winding, narrow streets, low-hanging limbs and truck access are a real consideration. None of it is a problem when we know in advance; just point us to the spot and flag any tight turns or overhanging branches when you call, and we'll plan the approach.

**The Cheverly permit rule.** The Town of Cheverly requires a permit **only when the dumpster is placed in the street or right-of-way.** A container on your driveway is permit-free. If the only practical spot at your address is the public street, you'll need that permit, and the turnaround runs about 24 to 48 hours — so it's worth a heads-up early. Given how these lots are laid out, the driveway is almost always the answer for a 10-yard, and we'll tell you which side of the rule your job falls on when we look at the spot.
