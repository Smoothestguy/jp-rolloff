## Where it goes, and which permit applies

Here's where Rockville is genuinely different from most towns we serve. The city is split between two extremes, and that split decides your dumpster placement more than anything else.

On one side you've got the large-lot homes of Derwood and the modest but driveway-friendly Cape Cods of Twinbrook. On these properties, a 20-yard usually drops right onto the driveway with room to spare. The City of Rockville treats private property as the default spot for a container, so a driveway placement keeps you clear of the permit question entirely. That's the easy scenario, and it covers a lot of Rockville.

On the other side are the dense, tightly planned parts of the city. King Farm, near the Shady Grove Metro, is a 430-acre New Urbanist community with narrow townhome lots, alley-loaded garages, and tight street frontage, all of which favor careful positioning and often a smaller container. Rockville Town Center, the pedestrian-oriented district downtown, and the high-density Pike District along the North Bethesda border are dominated by multifamily and apartment living rather than the private driveways found elsewhere. In these spots, fitting a 20-yard onto private property can be the real challenge.

When the container can't go on private property, you're into permit territory. The City of Rockville requires a Public Works permit to place a dumpster in the public right-of-way or easement, and a key condition is that you have to demonstrate the container cannot safely sit on private property. DPW (pw@rockvillemd.gov) makes that call, and it typically takes 2 to 3 business days, so it's worth planning ahead. Driveways and private property stay permit-free.

One more thing to check before you renovate. Rockville has three local historic districts, West End, East Rockville, and Lincoln Park, plus other listed historic properties. Lincoln Park, founded in 1891 as one of Maryland's first Black-owned home developments, and the Victorian-era West End hold some of the city's oldest homes. Exterior alterations in these districts can require a Historic Area Work Permit, with the city aiming to schedule review within about 45 days. That doesn't change which dumpster you need, but if your project touches the outside of a historic home, build that review time into your plan.
