## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**The permit question in Laurel is really a jurisdiction question.** Laurel is unusually fragmented — a single address can fall under the City of Laurel, Prince George's County, Howard County, or Anne Arundel County, and each one has its own road and right-of-way authority. In every one of them, a container that sits entirely on private property — your driveway, parking area, or a commercial site — generally does **not** need a permit. The permit only comes into play when the 30-yard has to occupy the public right-of-way: a street, sidewalk, or alley.

Where that line falls depends on your exact street. Inside the **City of Laurel**, right-of-way placement requires a permit from the Department of Public Works; the city reports a right-of-way permit running about $25 per week, with proof of liability insurance and a site plan, though you should confirm the current fee and paperwork directly with DPW. A **Prince George's County** address handles road occupancy through DPIE, **Howard County** through its Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits, and **Anne Arundel County** separately again. We deal with these splits all the time, so tell us your address and we will tell you which rule applies before anything is scheduled.

**Placement by neighborhood.** This matters more in Laurel than in most towns because of the housing mix. In the Old Town historic district, homes sit on narrow lots threaded with broad streets and rear alleys, which often pushes a container to the alley or street and into city right-of-way permit territory. In Russett and the surrounding Maryland City area — roughly 600 acres of 1980s-90s townhomes and condos near Fort Meade and the NSA — shared driveways, guest-parking spots, and HOA rules constrain where a 30-yard can go, so we scout placement with you ahead of time. South Laurel's detached driveways are the simplest, and North Laurel's newer Maple Lawn-area construction usually has room to work. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.
