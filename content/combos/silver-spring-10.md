## Placement and permits in unincorporated Montgomery County

Here's the wrinkle that makes Silver Spring different from most DMV towns. Silver Spring is unincorporated Montgomery County, so there's no city hall issuing dumpster permits — anything to do with the public roadway runs through Montgomery County's Department of Permitting Services (MCDPS).

The good news for the 10-yard is that its small footprint makes **private-driveway placement** the easy path, and a container set on your own private property generally needs no special county permit for a short-term home job. That's the route we steer most Silver Spring customers toward, and the compact 10-yard is the size most likely to actually fit a short driveway or a single car spot.

The reason that matters so much here: the near-in neighborhoods — Woodside, Four Corners, Kemp Mill, and out toward the Wheaton border — are dense pre-1960 subdivisions with narrow streets and short driveways, and many homes back onto the Sligo Creek floodplain and parkland, which constrains where a roll-off can sit and often forces driveway-only placement. The alternative of dropping the box in the street is rarely worth it for a quick home job. Any placement within a Montgomery County roadway requires a **Public Right-of-Way Permit**, those permits expire six months from issuance, and they can require traffic-control devices like cones or barricades — with a full traffic-control plan on high-volume roads or in business districts. A state or municipal roadway would need a permit from that jurisdiction instead.

That right-of-way burden is exactly why the 10-yard's tight footprint is an advantage in town: keep it on private property and you sidestep the permit entirely. A couple of local notes worth raising before we deliver:

- **Forest Glen**, home to the National Park Seminary Historic District, includes adaptive-reuse and historic-property pockets where renovation debris can carry historic-property considerations.

- Even with a county right-of-way permit, your **HOA** may have its own rules that require approval before a container is set.

We don't pull permits for you, but after years of running this area we can tell you what to expect for your specific street and help you keep the box on private property when that's the simpler path.
