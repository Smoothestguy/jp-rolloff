## What fits in a 30-yard dumpster

The 30-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 30-yard **starts at $665 for construction debris** (other materials are priced separately, and heavy aggregates like dirt, concrete, and asphalt are only offered in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard sizes). That price **includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance**, with pickup on request when you call to schedule it. Extra time runs $5 a day, and any weight over the 3-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton — so you always know how the number is built.

That extra foot of wall height over the mid-size bins, carried across 22 feet of length, is what makes this the maximum-capacity option. It is built for volume, not for tossing a few bags over the rail. The trade-off is footprint: a 30-yard needs a long, straight run-up to set down level, plus room for the truck to position and lift it. On a wide commercial lot or one of Landover's larger unincorporated parcels, that is no problem. On a tight wartime lot over in Landover Hills, it is the first thing we check — and sometimes the reason we steer you to a 25-yard instead.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**The permit rule, and why Landover is tricky.** Landover has a permit fault line running right through the same ZIP codes, so the answer depends on your exact address. Most of Landover is **unincorporated Prince George's County**. On unincorporated addresses, a container that sits entirely on private property — your driveway, lot, or a commercial yard — needs **no permit** at all. A permit is only required when the bin has to occupy the public right-of-way, meaning the county street or curb, in which case it falls under the Prince George's County Department of Permitting, Inspections and Enforcement (DPIE).

But if your address is in **Landover Hills**, the rules change. Landover Hills is a separate incorporated town, so street or right-of-way placement there requires a **town permit**, not the county process — even though it sits a few blocks from unincorporated streets that follow the county rules. We have run this area long enough to sort out which side of the line you are on before the truck rolls, so you are not left guessing.

**Placement for a 30-yard.** Because this is the biggest bin we carry, placement matters more than with the smaller sizes. On the larger unincorporated lots and the commercial sites along Route 202, there is usually room for the long run-up the 30-yard needs. Over in Landover Hills, the small WWII-era lots and narrow streets are tighter — driveway placement can be a squeeze, and street placement (with that town permit) is more often the only option. Either way, we scout the spot with you ahead of delivery and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect your driveway.

**The stadium factor.** Landover is anchored by the 200-acre FedExField/Northwest Stadium site off Ring Road, which is slated for demolition and mixed-use redevelopment after the Commanders move on. As that corridor turns over, the demolition and bulk-debris work it generates is squarely the kind of job the 30-yard is built for, and we are right next door to handle it.

**Drive time.** Landover is a short hop from our Beltsville yard, so we keep tight turnarounds here. Most Landover jobs go out inside 24 hours, and early calls can often make the same-day run.
