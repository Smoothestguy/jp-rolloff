## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures roughly 14 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall. That holds about four and a half pickup-truck loads of debris, and it comes with a 1.5-ton (3,000-pound) weight allowance. Our typical price runs $425 to $485, and that includes delivery, pickup, and the tonnage inside that allowance, so you're not guessing at the final number.

The four-foot walls matter as much as the volume. You can load this size by hand or with a wheelbarrow without heaving everything over your head, which is why it's the go-to for a one-room job where a crew is moving steadily over a few days. It's the size we describe as the "single-room reno" bin: enough room for the demo from one kitchen or two baths, not so much that you're staring at half an empty box on pickup day.

## Placement: the real question in Bladensburg

Bladensburg is dense. The town holds roughly 9,657 residents (2020 Census) on about a single square mile of land, a density near 9,000 people per square mile. That means narrow lots, short driveways, and limited curb frontage on a lot of properties. So the central placement question here isn't really "where do you want it" — it's "where will it physically fit and bear the weight."

We almost always recommend driveway placement, and the town's rules line up with that. The Town of Bladensburg requires a permit for a dumpster placed in the street or public right-of-way; driveway placement is permit-free, with same-week turnaround. On Bladensburg's short, dense in-town streets, a street-placed box would intrude on traffic anyway, so the driveway is both the easier path and the practical default.

Two local quirks shape where the bin can actually sit. First, this is flood country. Severe Anacostia floods in 1933, 1942, and 1954 led to a major flood-control project in the late 1950s, complete with levees three to eighteen feet high along both banks. Low-lying parcels near the Anacostia corridor can still sit in or near the floodplain, and soft or saturated ground affects where we can safely set a loaded dumpster. Second, the town has leaned hard into green stormwater infrastructure to protect the river — including porous Grasspave2 paving, like the roughly 11,160 square feet installed at the historic Bostwick House. Permeable and grass-paved surfaces look like driveways but can't carry the weight of a loaded roll-off, so on some properties we'll talk through an alternate spot before delivery. If your project sits over near the Port Towns cluster or the Edmonston border along the river, those ground conditions are worth a quick mention when you call.
