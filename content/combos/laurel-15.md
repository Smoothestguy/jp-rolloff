## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Which jurisdiction's permit applies to your street.** Laurel is unusually fragmented for a single city. One address can fall under the City of Laurel, Prince George's County, Howard County, or Anne Arundel County — each with its own road and right-of-way permit authority. The good news for a one-room reno is that the answer is usually simple: a 15-yard sitting entirely on your private driveway is typically permit-free in all four jurisdictions. A permit only comes into play when the container has to sit in the public right-of-way — the street, sidewalk, or alley.

When the street is the only option, the rule depends on where your address sits. The City of Laurel requires a Right-of-Way Permit through its Department of Public Works; that permit reportedly runs about $25 per week and asks for proof of liability insurance and a site plan, though we always tell folks to confirm the current fee and paperwork with DPW directly. Prince George's County handles road occupancy through DPIE, Howard County through its Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits, and Anne Arundel County separately. The practical question in Laurel isn't just "where does the dumpster fit" — it's "which authority covers this exact street," and we are happy to help you sort that out before delivery day.

**Where the dumpster sits, neighborhood by neighborhood.** Placement reality changes across town. The Old Town historic district — seven contiguous sub-districts centered on Main Street, with a former 1840s mill-workers' home at 9th and Main now serving as the Laurel Museum — sits on narrow lots threaded with rear alleys, which often pushes placement to the alley or street and into right-of-way territory. South Laurel (ZIP 20708/20724) is the easy case: its post-WWII ranches, split-levels, and Colonial Revivals sit on standalone lots with private driveways, the simplest scenario for permit-free placement. Russett and the wider Maryland City area run dense with 1980s-90s townhomes and condos, so shared driveways, HOA rules, and tight guest parking come into play. North Laurel's newer Howard County builds vary block to block. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and we will scout the fit with you. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.
