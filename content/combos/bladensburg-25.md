## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 25-yard **starts at $640 for construction debris (other materials are priced separately)**, and that includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 2-ton weight allowance. Pickup is on request — you call to schedule it. Pricing stays per material rather than a single combined figure: extra days run $5/day, and weight over the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

It's the step up from our everyday residential containers. Where a 20-yard handles a kitchen-and-two-baths gut, the 25-yard is built for projects that generate debris on a larger or longer timeline — the kind where you'd otherwise be calling for a second smaller box halfway through. The footprint is the catch, though: at 20 feet of length plus clearance for the truck and an open rear door, it needs more straight room than the shorter sizes. On a narrow Bladensburg lot that's the deciding factor, so measure twice before booking, and let us look at the spot with you.

## Which Bladensburg jobs suit the 25-yard

The bulk of Bladensburg's housing is mid-20th-century — ZIP 20710 carries a median build year around 1969, with a heavy share of 1950s and 1960s homes, plus the older Decatur Heights bungalow blocks platted back in 1914 and 1917. That aging stock is exactly what drives the bigger jobs this size is for:

- **Home additions.** Adding a room or bumping out the back of a 1960s house generates framing offcuts, old siding, drywall, and demo debris over weeks. The 25-yard gives you the volume to keep one container on site through the build instead of swapping boxes.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full gut of one of these mid-century homes — kitchen, baths, flooring, and walls all coming out — fills the 25-yard's range without paying for a 30-yard you won't top off.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** A bigger or multi-layer roof on an older Bladensburg home runs heavy, and the 2-ton allowance covers a good share of that asphalt weight before the $105-per-ton overage matters.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** Along the Annapolis Road (MD 450) commercial corridor, a storefront or office cleanout produces a lot of mixed debris in a hurry. **Commercial accounts welcome — ask about billing**, so it fits how a business runs.

If your project is smaller — a single bath, a one-room flooring job — we'll tell you straight that a 20 or even a 15-yard is the better call. The 25-yard earns its keep on the major work, not the quick stuff.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and ground conditions

**Driveway placement is the default here, and it's permit-free.** The Town of Bladensburg requires a permit only when a dumpster sits in the street or public right-of-way; on your own driveway there's no permit, with same-week turnaround. On a town this dense, a 25-yard parked in the street would intrude on traffic on these short, narrow blocks, which is exactly why the right-of-way is regulated. The honest reality is that a 20-foot container needs real driveway length, and many Bladensburg lots — especially the tight-setback Decatur Heights bungalow blocks and the narrower 1950s–60s driveways — don't have it to spare. We'll scout your address with you, and if the driveway truly can't take this size, we'll either talk through a right-of-way permit or steer you to a container that fits.

**Watch the ground surface.** This is the placement factor that catches people in Bladensburg more than anywhere else we serve. The town has invested heavily in green stormwater infrastructure to protect the Anacostia — including permeable and grass-paved surfaces like the roughly 11,160 square feet of porous Grasspave2 paving installed at the historic Bostwick House. A loaded 25-yard runs thousands of pounds, and those permeable or grass-paved surfaces simply can't bear that weight. If your driveway or staging area is porous paving rather than solid asphalt or concrete, tell us up front so we can plan placement on a surface that holds. We lay boards under the wheels on request to spread the load and protect a sound surface.

**Mind the floodplain near the river.** Bladensburg's geography was shaped by serious Anacostia flooding in 1933, 1942, and 1954, which led to the late-1950s flood-control project and the levees along both banks. Low-lying riverside parcels near the Anacostia tributary corridor — out toward the Edmonston border and the Port Towns junction — still sit in or near the floodplain, where soft or saturated ground can be a real concern for setting a heavy container safely. If your site is down near the water, we'll check the footing before we drop the box.
