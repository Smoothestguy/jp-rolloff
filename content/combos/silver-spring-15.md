## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures roughly 14 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall. That low 4-foot wall is the practical part: you can step up and drop debris over the side instead of fighting a tall door. It holds about four and a half pickup-truck loads, and your rental includes a 1.5-ton (3,000-pound) weight allowance, with delivery, pickup, and that tonnage built into the price. For most Silver Spring jobs we quote, that runs **$425 to $485**, all in.

That weight allowance is the number to watch. Fifteen cubic yards of light bulky waste — old cabinets, drywall, carpet, framing lumber — fits comfortably under the limit. Dense material like roofing shingles, plaster, or tile will hit the weight ceiling well before the box looks full, so on tear-off jobs we'd rather talk through what you're loading than surprise you with an overage. A quick phone call before delivery usually settles it.

## Which Silver Spring jobs suit this size

The 15-yard earns its "single-room reno" nickname on exactly the projects that fill our schedule across the older parts of Silver Spring:

- **Kitchen remodel** — cabinets, countertops, flooring, and drywall from one kitchen pull-out land squarely in a 15.
- **Roofing tear-off** — a single-layer tear-off on a typical home fits, as long as we keep an eye on tonnage.
- **Two-bath gut** — fixtures, tile, vanities, and subfloor from a pair of bathrooms.
- **Basement cleanout** — clearing out a finished or storage basement before a refinish.

These are the bread-and-butter jobs in Silver Spring's near-in neighborhoods, and there's a reason. So much of the housing stock here is older and well-established. Woodside Park was platted back in 1922 and built up mostly from the 1920s through the 1950s — county historic-planning staff have called it the best-preserved early-20th-century neighborhood in Montgomery County. Homes of that vintage are forever in the middle of a renovation, an addition, or a whole-house remodel. Four Corners and South Four Corners — the post-WWII pocket of Cape Cods, ramblers, split-levels, and Tudors built largely from the 1930s into the 1960s — generate a steady stream of basement-finishing, kitchen and bath remodel, and roofing work. Kemp Mill, developed beginning in the late 1950s and filled in with infill homes in the 1980s and '90s, runs along the same lines. These are compact mid-century houses, and a 15-yard is usually the right call for one of them.

## Local logistics, permits, and placement

Here's where Silver Spring is genuinely different from the larger-lot suburbs around the DMV. The near-in neighborhoods — Woodside, Four Corners, Kemp Mill, and out toward the Wheaton border — are dense pre-1960 subdivisions with narrow streets and short driveways. Some homes back directly onto Sligo Creek Stream Valley Park, whose roughly 10-mile trail threads up from the Prince George's County line toward Wheaton Regional Park. Houses near that stream valley sit in or beside floodplain and parkland, which limits where a box can go and often forces driveway-only placement.

That's actually the easier path. Silver Spring is unincorporated Montgomery County, so a dumpster set on your private property or driveway needs no special county permit for a short-term home job. The 15's modest footprint is built for exactly this — it tucks onto most driveways without blocking the whole apron.

The street is the complicated option. Any placement within a Montgomery County roadway requires a **Public Right-of-Way Permit** through MCDPS. Those permits expire six months from issuance and can require traffic-control devices like cones or barricades — and on high-volume roads or in a business district, a full traffic-control plan. For a quick one-room remodel, that's a lot of process to avoid, which is exactly why we work hard to land the box on private property whenever we can. If your home sits on a state or municipal roadway instead, the permit comes from that jurisdiction. And even with a county permit in hand, some HOAs have their own rules about containers, so it's worth a check if you're in an association.

A couple of local notes we keep in mind. Downtown Silver Spring's redeveloped core — the dense mid- and high-rise blocks around Ellsworth Drive that Foulger-Pratt and Peterson built out between 2000 and 2013 — is mostly commercial and contractor work with tight street access, not open driveways, so those jobs get planned differently. In Forest Glen, the National Park Seminary Historic District and its adaptive-reuse units mean renovation debris there can carry historic-property considerations. And in Kemp Mill, home to one of the largest Orthodox Jewish communities on the East Coast, we're glad to schedule delivery and pickup around the Sabbath — nothing landing Friday evening through Saturday. Just tell us when you book.
