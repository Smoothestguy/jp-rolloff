## What fits in a 30-yard dumpster

The 30-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly fourteen pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Beltsville a 30-yard **includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with that 3-ton allowance — priced by material, so call for a quote**. Extra days run $5/day, weight over the allowance is billed $105 per additional ton, and pickup is on request — just call to schedule.

That extra foot of wall height over the mid-size bins, stretched across 22 feet of length, is what makes this the maximum-capacity option. It is built for volume, not for tossing a few bags over the side. The trade-off is footprint: a 30-yard needs a long, straight run-up to set down level, plus clearance for the truck to position and lift it. On the right site that is no issue. On a tight lot or a short driveway, it is the first thing we check, and sometimes the reason we will steer you to a 25-yard instead.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement, and why it matters more here.** Beltsville is wrapped by major roads — I-95, the Capital Beltway, the BW Parkway, the ICC, and MD 198 Powder Mill — and US 1 runs straight through the heart of town. Many homes and commercial lots front those busy through-roads, where dropping a container at the curb is restricted or simply unsafe. So private placement is strongly preferred. The catch with a 30-yard is its size: it needs a long, level run-up and real clearance for the truck. Driveways and grades vary by neighborhood — Calverton's smaller ranchers tend toward narrower single-car drives, while Vansville's split-levels often have wider two-car setups — so we scout the placement with you before delivery and lay boards under the wheels on request.

**The permit rule.** Beltsville is unincorporated, so permitting falls under Prince George's County. A container that sits entirely on private property — your driveway, lot, or a commercial yard — does **not** need a permit. A permit is only required when the container has to occupy the public right-of-way, meaning the county street, in which case you need a Prince George's County Use & Occupancy permit. Given how many Beltsville addresses front state through-roads, keeping the 30-yard on private ground is usually both the easier and the cheaper option, and we will flag it up front if your address looks like it would need the right-of-way.
