## What fits in a 10-yard

The 10-yard is the smallest footprint we run, and that's exactly the point. It carries a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance, which is plenty for the dense, heavy debris that a single space tends to produce. Think of it as three full pickup loads dropped in one place, with no second trips and no borrowing your neighbor's truck.

Because it sits low and short, it's the easiest container to place in a tight spot. That low height also means you're not heaving heavy bags up over a tall wall — you can walk debris in over the side or through the rear door.

## Local logistics, permits and drive time

Here's where Lanham gets a little particular, and where it pays to talk to a hauler who knows the area. Lanham is an unincorporated community in Prince George's County — there's no town hall of its own, so county DPIE rules govern placement, not a municipal office. What that means for your dumpster:

- **Private driveway: no permit.** A 10-yard set entirely on your own driveway or off-street pad needs no permit at all. Given Lanham's stock of older detached homes with their own driveways, this is the easy, common option for most jobs.
- **Street placement: county right-of-way permit.** If the container has to sit on a county road, that requires a PG County right-of-way permit. This comes up most with the newer townhouse rows around town, where narrow shared driveways or visitor-only parking leave no off-street room.

The 10-yard's compact footprint actually helps here. Because it only needs about one car space, more Lanham homes can keep it off the street entirely — including some townhouse setups that couldn't fit a larger box on-site. If you're near the western edge of town along Route 704 toward the Glenarden city line, just give us a heads-up: Glenarden is an incorporated city with its own rules, so a job that straddles that border may answer to a different permit authority than unincorporated Lanham.
