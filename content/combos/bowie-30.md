## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That tall, long box swallows about fourteen pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that produce a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Bowie a 30-yard is priced by material, so call for a quote. Every 30-yard includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with the 3-ton allowance built in. Extra days run $5/day, and any weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton, so heavy loads can add to the cost.

A quick word on the weight allowance: 30-yard volume fills up with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, cabinetry, roofing tear-off, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy (think a lot of concrete, brick, dirt, or tile), the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is often the smarter call. When you call, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size.

## Placement and permits in Bowie

Here is where Bowie is genuinely easy compared with most DMV towns. The heart of residential Bowie is Belair at Bowie, the roughly 7,500 mid-century homes that Levitt & Sons built between about 1960 and 1965 on the old Belair Estate. Those homes sit on uniform quarter-acre lots, and every one was built with a real driveway and an enclosed garage. The practical upshot for a roll-off: in the Levitt-built sections — Somerset, Buckingham, Kenilworth, Foxhill, and Tulip Grove — we can almost always stage a dumpster on the homeowner's own driveway instead of the street.

That matters because of how the city handles permits. **The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street; driveways and HOA-approved staging do not.** So across most of Belair, a street permit is the exception rather than the rule. One small bonus of the Levitt layout: every street in a section starts with that section's first letter, which makes finding your address straightforward when we are routing a truck and a long trailer.

A 30-yard does need a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes — it is a big box on a long frame — so a flat, open driveway with room to back in is ideal. When you book, let us know whether the approach is tight or sloped and we will plan the drop accordingly. If the only workable spot is the public street, the city's Public Works Department handles right-of-way review out of the city offices at 16500 Annapolis Road, and we are glad to talk through what that involves before your container arrives.

A few neighborhood notes worth raising when you call:

- **Belair (Somerset, Buckingham, Kenilworth, Foxhill, Tulip Grove) and Mitchellville and Pointer Ridge.** Standard suburban driveways, easy staging. These 60-plus-year-old homes are now deep into their second and third rounds of renovation — additions, basement finishes, roof replacements, full gut jobs — which is exactly where a 30-yard belongs.
- **Fairwood.** Bowie's newer planned community, built in phases from around 2004, is HOA-governed, so a 30-yard often needs HOA-approved placement, and the townhome sections have tighter shared driveways and parking than the older Belair ranchers. We will work with you on a spot that keeps the HOA happy.
- **Old Town Bowie.** The oldest part of the city, a railroad town dating to the 1870s, has older lots and narrower streets than the suburban Belair grid. A 30-yard is workable here, but placement takes more planning — tell us about the approach up front.
