## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and swallows roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2.5-ton (5,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical 25-yard rental in Rockville runs **$525 to $625, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — not a teaser rate with tipping fees added after the load hits the scale.

The jump up from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise need a mid-job swap — a full gut, a two-story addition, a large roof. The walls are still five feet, so a crew can walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but there is meaningfully more room behind them.

The advice we give every customer on this size is simple: **measure twice before booking.** Twenty feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the swing of the rear door, is a long footprint. On a Derwood driveway that is no trouble at all. On a tight Twinbrook street or a King Farm townhome alley it may not fit, and that is a conversation worth having before delivery day rather than after.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is where Rockville gets interesting, and the city itself sets the tone.** The City of Rockville's default expectation is that your dumpster sits on private property — the driveway or yard. Street and right-of-way placement is the exception: it requires a Public Works permit, and a condition of that permit is demonstrating the container *cannot* safely go on private property, a call the DPW makes at pw@rockvillemd.gov. Plan on 2–3 business days for that approval. Driveways and private property are permit-free.

For a 25-yard, that rule and the city's split geography push in the same direction. The big-lot side of Rockville is where this size fits best: Derwood's RE-1 acreage parcels and the long driveways out near Crabbs Branch and Lake Needwood take a 20-foot container with room to spare and no permit question at all. The dense side is the opposite story. King Farm — a 430-acre New Urbanist community of roughly 395 single-family homes plus about 1,021 townhomes on narrow, alley-loaded lots — and the high-rise Pike District simply do not have the driveway frontage a 25-yard wants, which is why we usually steer those addresses to a smaller can or scout placement carefully before we commit. A 25 needs about 20 feet of straight length plus clearance and a clear door swing; we lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**One more Rockville wrinkle: historic districts.** If your home sits in West End, East Rockville, or Lincoln Park — Rockville's three local historic districts, which hold some of the city's oldest houses — exterior alterations can require a Historic Area Work Permit before work begins, with review scheduled within roughly 45 days. That is a project-permit matter, not a dumpster matter, but on the kind of major remodel a 25-yard serves it is worth knowing, since the timing of your renovation drives the timing of your haul-off.

**Drive time.** Rockville is about 45 minutes from our Beltsville yard via I-495 or the ICC (Route 200). Standard turnaround is next-day, and we make same-day runs on most addresses when you call early enough in the morning.
