## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard measures roughly 12 feet long by 8 feet wide by 4 feet tall. That low profile is the whole point: it takes up about one car's worth of space and is easy to load by hand because the walls are short enough to lean over. It holds around three pickup-truck loads of material and comes with a 1-ton (2,000-pound) weight allowance, which is plenty for a one-room job.

Pricing is per material, so call us for a quote on your specific load. The price includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 1-ton (2,000-pound) weight allowance, with pickup on request when you call to schedule. Extra days run $5 each, and any weight over the 1-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. ## Which Riverdale Park jobs suit this size

People around here call the 10-yard the "garage cleanout" size, and that's no accident. It's sized for the four jobs that fill it without leaving you with empty space you paid for:

- **Garage cleanout.** Decades of stored boxes, old paint cans, broken furniture, and lawn equipment fit neatly in a 10-yard, and the low walls mean you're not heaving heavy items up over your head.
- **Bathroom remodel.** Riverdale Park's housing stock is genuinely old, with a median build year of 1943 and homes going back to the 1890s. That means most projects here are renovations rather than new builds, and a single bathroom gut, old tile, vanity, tub, and torn-out plaster, lands right in the 10-yard's wheelhouse.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Branches, brush, hedge trimmings, and storm debris from these mature, established lots load easily into a low container.
- **Estate downsize.** When you're clearing out a family home, especially one of the older Foursquares, bungalows, or cottages in the historic district, a 10-yard handles a room-by-room downsize without committing you to a giant bin parked out front for weeks.

There's one more job that comes up here more than in most towns: flood cleanout. Riverdale Park is one of the most flood-prone communities in the area. The Northeast Branch of the Anacostia River runs straight through it, and heavy storms have repeatedly pushed water into basements and ground floors. When that happens, you're suddenly dealing with soaked drywall, ruined flooring, and water-damaged furniture and appliances that have to go fast. A 10-yard is often the right call for clearing out a single flooded basement or ground-floor room, keeping the cleanup moving so the drying and rebuilding can start.

Because so many of these homes are pre-WWII wood-frame houses, renovation work here tends to mean tear-out: old plaster, knob-and-tube-era materials, lath, and original fixtures coming out before anything new goes in. If your project is bigger than one room, a 20-yard might serve you better, and we're happy to talk it through rather than oversell you. ## Local logistics, permits, and drive time

Riverdale Park was platted back in 1889 as a planned railroad "villa park," with a grid of streets named after presidents laid out around the old commuter station. Those original sections used fairly uniform lot dimensions and modest setbacks, which is a polite way of saying many properties here have narrow frontages and tight driveways. The 10-yard's small footprint is a real advantage in that setting. It fits where a larger bin simply won't, and on most properties we can set it on the driveway without disturbing the street or your neighbors.

If your driveway can take it, that's almost always the easiest path, and in Riverdale Park a driveway placement is typically permit-free. If you need the dumpster in the public right-of-way, on the street or curb, the Town of Riverdale Park requires a permit for that placement, and it usually takes one to two business days to clear. Plan that lead time into your project, and if you're not sure which way to go, call us and we'll help you figure out the simplest legal spot. We've placed plenty of bins around the Town Center and MARC station area, near the historic Riversdale Mansion neighborhoods, and on the close-set residential blocks throughout town, so we have a good sense of what works where.

A couple of practical notes for these older blocks. We'll need a clear, level spot and room for the truck to swing in and out, so move cars ahead of time. If you're working near the Northeast Branch Trail or one of the floodplain-adjacent lots, give us a heads-up on access, since those properties can have tighter buildable areas. And since we're only about ten minutes from our Beltsville yard, we can usually get a 10-yard to you quickly and swing back for the pickup without a long wait.
