## Where it goes, and which permit applies

Here's where Laurel is genuinely different from most towns we serve. One Laurel address can fall under the City of Laurel, Prince George's County, Howard County, or Anne Arundel County, and each one runs its own road and right-of-way rules. The central question here isn't just "where does the dumpster fit" but "which jurisdiction's permit applies to this exact street."

The good news: driveway placement is typically permit-free across all four jurisdictions. If you've got a private driveway and the 20-yard fits, you're usually clear of the permit question entirely. That's the easiest scenario, and it describes most of the detached homes in South Laurel.

Things get more involved when the container has to go in the street, on a sidewalk, or in an alley. Inside the City of Laurel, that means a Right-of-Way Permit from the Department of Public Works, which generally calls for proof of liability insurance and a site plan. (DPW can confirm the current fee and exact requirements.) If your address sits in Prince George's County, road-occupancy permits go through DPIE; Howard County handles them through its Department of Inspections, Licenses and Permits; and Anne Arundel County runs its own process.

This comes up a lot in two parts of Laurel. The Old Town historic core, with its narrow lots, broad streets, and rear alleys from another era, often leaves no room for a driveway placement, which pushes the container toward the alley or street and into city right-of-way permit territory. And Russett, the roughly 600-acre planned community out in the Maryland City area of Anne Arundel County, is dense with townhomes and condos that frequently mean shared driveways, HOA rules, and tight guest-parking spots. In both cases, a little planning before drop-off saves a headache. We'll walk you through which authority applies to your address before the truck rolls.
