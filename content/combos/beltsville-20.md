## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard is the workhorse of the residential lineup. It measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Beltsville a 20-yard is **priced by material, so call for a quote**. That rate includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with the 2-ton (4,000 lb) allowance. Extra days run $5/day, and any weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule when you're done.

The four-foot wall height is the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but the long footprint swallows the volume of a real project. That balance is why it is the size most customers end up choosing — bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but short of the 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo. It also fits most two-car driveways with room to spare, which counts for a lot in this town.

## Which Beltsville jobs suit the 20-yard

Beltsville's residential stock is heavily mid-20th-century. Intensive development came after World War II — early Cape Cods, then ranchers (ramblers) and split-levels through the late 1950s and 1960s — layered over older Victorian, Colonial Revival, and Craftsman bungalow homes from the town's 1891-to-1940s railroad-suburb days. (Beltsville traces back to a B&O Railroad stop on land owned by tobacco farmer Truman Belt, who gave the town its name.) That older-but-modest housing means the jobs people tackle are exactly the ones a 20-yard is built for:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the size's best-known job. Clearing a Beltsville rancher between owners, or after years of accumulation, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you won't use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-two-baths gut, or a run of rooms getting new drywall and flooring in a 1960s rambler, is the classic 20-yard project.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When a longtime family home in one of the older sections changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical single-layer asphalt tear-off on a Beltsville home fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most of these jobs.

Neighborhoods here also differ in driveway reality, and that affects placement more than size. Calverton, east of the freeways, is known for smaller ranchers with single-car garages, while Vansville tends toward larger split-levels with two-car setups — so driveway width and grade vary block to block. Either way, the 20-yard's 22-foot footprint fits most of them; we just scout the spot with you first. If your project is genuinely smaller, or genuinely larger, we will tell you straight which size you actually need.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Why driveway placement matters here.** Beltsville is wrapped by major highways — I-95, the Capital Beltway, MD 295 (the BW Parkway), the ICC, and MD 198 (Powder Mill Road) — and a lot of homes front busy state through-roads like US 1, Powder Mill, Edmonston, and Calverton Boulevard. On roads like those, curbside or right-of-way placement is restricted, so we strongly prefer setting the container on your private driveway. A 20-yard needs roughly 22 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The permit rule.** Beltsville is unincorporated, so permitting falls under Prince George's County rather than a city hall. The practical upshot is simple: a dumpster sitting entirely on your private driveway does **not** need a permit. A container placed in the public right-of-way — the street — requires a Prince George's County Use & Occupancy permit. Because so many Beltsville homes sit on through-roads where the street is not a realistic spot anyway, the great majority of our jobs go on the driveway and need no permit at all. If the street is the only option, we will flag the permit step when we look at your address.

**Commercial and industrial work.** If you are on the other side of Beltsville's economy — a warehouse cleanout, a tenant fit-out, or demolition in the Beltsville Industrial Park along Ammendale Road or out toward Konterra — the 20-yard handles plenty of mid-size commercial loads too, and we can talk through whether you need one container or a swap-out rotation.
