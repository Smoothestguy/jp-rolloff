## What fits in a 30-yard dumpster

The 30-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly fourteen pickup-truck loads of debris. It carries a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Rockville a 30-yard **includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with that 3-ton allowance — priced by material, so call for a quote**. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule it. Extra days run $5/day, and weight over the 3-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

That extra foot of wall height over the mid-size bins, stretched across 22 feet of length, is what makes this the maximum-capacity option. It is built for volume — for jobs where debris comes by the truckload, not by the bag. The trade-off is footprint. A 30-yard needs a long, straight run-up to set down level, plus room for the truck to position and lift it. On an open commercial lot, a Pike District construction site, or a wide Derwood driveway, that is no problem. On a narrow Twinbrook lot or a shared King Farm alley, it is the first thing we check — and sometimes the reason we will steer you to a 25-yard instead.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**In Rockville, the permit question turns on where the container sits.** The City of Rockville requires a Public Works permit for any placement in the public street or right-of-way, and the city's default expectation is that dumpsters go on private property — its DPW (pw@rockvillemd.gov) will only approve right-of-way placement after it is shown the container *cannot* safely sit on the driveway or lot. So a 30-yard on your own driveway, parking area, or a private commercial site generally needs **no permit**. A street or right-of-way placement does, and that approval runs about 2-3 business days, so tell us early if your address may need one.

**Placement by neighborhood matters more here than in most towns.** This is where "which Rockville?" decides everything. In Derwood, the long driveways and acreage lots make driveway placement easy and comfortably support a 30-yard. On a wide commercial or Pike District site, footprint is rarely the issue. But King Farm's townhomes sit on narrow, alley-loaded lots with shared garages and tight street frontage — a 30-yard often will not fit there, and street placement runs straight into the right-of-way permit. Twinbrook's narrow Cape Cod lots are the same kind of squeeze. And if your project sits in one of Rockville's historic districts — West End, East Rockville, or Lincoln Park — exterior alterations can require a Historic Area Work Permit before the work even starts, which the city aims to schedule within about 45 days. None of that changes the bin; it changes the timeline and the spot, so tell us your exact address and we will sort it out before anything is scheduled. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.
