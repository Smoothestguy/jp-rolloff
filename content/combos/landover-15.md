## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly four and a half pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Landover a typical 15-yard rental runs **$425 to $485, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage**. No tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the haul.

Four feet of wall height is the part people come to appreciate once the work starts. It is low enough to walk debris up and tip it over the side without a ramp, which matters on a one-room remodel where you are carrying material out by hand all day. The 15-yard is best known as the "single-room reno" size for exactly that reason: bigger than the 10-yard you would grab for a quick declutter, but not the 20- or 30-yard you would only reach for on a multi-room gut or a full estate cleanout. For a single project that throws off real volume but stays contained, it is the right call.

## Which Landover jobs suit the 15-yard

Landover's residential inventory leans heavily on mid-century suburban subdivisions — split-levels, brick ramblers and ranchers, and modest two-story colonials built in the decades after World War II. Spread across neighborhoods like Kentland, Kenmoor, Dodge Park, Palmer Park, Brightseat, and Columbia Park, that aging stock keeps a steady supply of one-room remodels and cleanouts in motion, and most of them land squarely in 15-yard territory.

- **Kitchen remodel.** Pulling original cabinets, countertops, flooring, and drywall from a single kitchen fills a 15-yard about right, without paying for space you will not use.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these 50-to-70-year-old roofs fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most jobs of that size. Heavier or multi-layer tear-offs may need the next size up, and we will tell you straight.
- **Two-bath gut.** Tile, fixtures, vanities, and subfloor from a pair of bathrooms is a textbook 15-yard load.
- **Basement cleanout.** Clearing a storage or finished basement before a remodel — or a turnover cleanout between tenants, which is common across Landover's higher share of rental properties.

The 20785 area in particular sees a lot of turnover and rehab work on aging homes, so we drop plenty of 15-yards for landlords and contractors clearing one unit at a time. When the job grows into a whole-house gut or a heavy demolition — the kind of work the warehouses and tenant build-outs along the Route 202 industrial strip and the Ardwick Industrial Park tend to generate — that is when we will point you to a 20- or 30-yard instead. For a single room or a single system in a single home, the 15 is usually the right pick.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement.** A 15-yard needs roughly 14 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance, and an open swing for the rear door. On Landover's larger unincorporated lots — the postwar ramblers and split-levels through Kentland, Dodge Park, and Palmer Park — there is usually room to set the container on the driveway and keep it there for the whole project. Tell us about your driveway pitch and any tight approaches when you call, and we will plan the drop accordingly and level the container on boards if a slope calls for it.

**The Landover permit rule — and the fault line to watch.** This is where Landover gets its own quirk, and where getting the answer right per address matters. The bulk of Landover is **unincorporated Prince George's County**. That means driveway and private-property placement needs **no permit** at all. Only if the container has to sit on the street or public right-of-way do you need a county permit, issued through the Prince George's County Department of Permitting, Inspections and Enforcement (DPIE). But a few blocks can change the rule entirely: **Landover Hills is a separate incorporated town**, and street or right-of-way placement there requires its own **town permit**, distinct from the county process. The two sit close together inside the same general area, so the placement answer depends on your exact address. If your 15-yard goes on the driveway — which on most Landover lots it can — you are clear with no paperwork either way. If the street is the only practical spot, tell us where you are and we will tell you which permit applies before it holds up your start date.

**Access and drive time.** Landover sits right along the Capital Beltway (I-495/I-95) where it crosses U.S. Route 50, with interchanges at Route 202 (Landover Road) and Brightseat Road. Route 202 is the commercial spine through the community, and the FedExField corridor — now Northwest Stadium, off Brightseat near Arena Drive — anchors the eastern side near the Summerfield and Glenarden edges. From the Beltsville yard we are a short hop, and we dispatch every morning, so early calls usually make the same-day drop and most other addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours. Landover Hills runs along Route 450 (Annapolis Road) with smaller wartime-era lots and narrower streets, so on those addresses we like to know the spot ahead of time to bring the truck in clean on the first pass.
