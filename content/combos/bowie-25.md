## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

This is the second-largest container most homeowners and contractors ever need, and the extra capacity matters most on dense, heavy jobs. The 25-yard holds about 10.5 standard pickup loads, which is the difference between finishing a big project in one haul and stacking debris on the lawn while you wait for a swap.

A few honest notes on weight. The 2-ton allowance is generous for mixed construction debris — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, flooring, trim, packaging, and general demo. But dense materials add up fast. A full layer of asphalt shingles, plaster, tile, or concrete can hit the tonnage cap well before the box looks full, so if your load is heavy rather than bulky, tell us up front and we will steer you to the right plan. For voluminous-but-light debris — a gutted kitchen, a finished basement torn back to studs, the contents of a whole-house remodel — the 25-yard's footprint is exactly where it shines.

Because this is a true "major construction" size, measure twice before booking. For longer driveways and bigger builds the 25 is ideal, but if you are doing a single bathroom or a modest declutter, a smaller box will save you money. When in doubt, call and describe the job — we would rather right-size it than oversell you.

## Which Bowie jobs suit the 25-yard

Bowie is, more than almost any town in the DMV, a Levitt suburb. The heart of the city is Belair at Bowie, roughly 7,500 near-identical homes that Levitt & Sons built between about 1960 and 1965 across seven named sections — Somerset, Buckingham, Kenilworth, Foxhill, Tulip Grove, Meadowbrook, and Heather Hills. Those houses are now well past 60 years old, and a whole neighborhood's worth of them are hitting the same renovation cycle at once. The original models — the Cape Cods, the Ranchers, the three- and four-bedroom Colonials, the Country Clubbers — were never large, so when owners go big, they tend to go all the way: a rear or second-story addition, a whole-house gut remodel, a full roof replacement, a finished basement carved out and rebuilt.

Those are exactly the jobs the 25-yard was built for. A **home addition** generates framing offcuts, old siding, excavated interior finishes, and packaging in volume. A **whole-house remodel** in one of the Belair Colonials produces a steady stream of cabinetry, drywall, flooring, and trim that a smaller box simply cannot keep ahead of. A **large roofing** tear-off on a four-bedroom Colonial — especially a multi-layer roof — is a classic 25-yard job, though keep the tonnage in mind since shingles are heavy. And for a **commercial cleanout** near Bowie Town Center or along the US 50 corridor, the 25 gives crews room to clear a space fast. Commercial accounts welcome — ask about billing, which keeps the paperwork simple when you are running several jobs at once.

The neighborhoods vary, and that changes the staging conversation. The older Belair sections — Somerset, Buckingham, Kenilworth, Foxhill, Tulip Grove — sit on uniform quarter-acre lots (many around 8,400 square feet), and every one was built with a real driveway and an enclosed garage. Old Town Bowie, the original railroad town, has older homes on smaller lots and narrower streets, so placement there takes a little more planning.

## Permits, placement, and drive time

Here is the part Bowie homeowners are usually relieved to hear. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. If your 25-yard goes on your driveway — or on HOA-approved staging in a community like Fairwood — no city street permit is needed. Given how Belair was built, with a driveway at every house, the street permit is the exception rather than the rule here. For the cases that do touch the public right-of-way, the City's Public Works Department handles engineering review and permitting out of the city offices at 16500 Annapolis Road.

A 25-yard box stands a tall 6 feet, so spend a minute on placement before drop-off. You want a flat, firm stretch of driveway with clear overhead access — no low branches or wires where the truck lifts the container — and ideally a spot that does not trap the cars you still need to use during the project. On the older Belair quarter-acre lots there is almost always a clean line for it. On a Fairwood townhome driveway or an Old Town lot, send us a photo or a quick description and we will confirm the fit before we roll.

From our Beltsville dispatch, Bowie is about 25 minutes out via Route 197 or the BW Parkway, and we cover the city's main ZIPs — 20715, 20716, 20720, and 20721. Bowie is one of Maryland's largest cities by area, nearly 19 square miles, so that one short drive lets us reach everything from Old Town to Fairwood without it becoming a logistics headache.
