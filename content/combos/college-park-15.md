## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures roughly 14 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall, and holds about four and a half pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: for construction debris, a 15-yard starts at $595 (other materials are priced separately). That price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 1.5-ton (3,000-pound) weight allowance. Go over the allowance and excess weight is billed at $105 per additional ton; need the box longer and extra days are $5 a day. Pickup is on request — just call us to schedule when you're ready.

Those four-foot walls matter more than people expect. They're low enough that you can walk a wheelbarrow up a ramp or pitch shingles over the side without an exhausting lift, but the footprint still holds enough volume for a genuine remodel's worth of waste. It's the in-between size: bigger than a 10, but still driveway-friendly for most homes, which is exactly why it's the one we recommend when someone isn't sure whether to size up.

## Which College Park jobs suit the 15

College Park's housing stock skews old and varied. Most homes here went up between roughly 1940 and 1969, with Old Town and Berwyn holding houses over a century old. That mix of pre-WWII frame houses, mid-century Cape Cods, ranches, and split-levels means renovation and tear-off projects are constant — and a surprising share of them land squarely in 15-yard territory.

- **Kitchen remodel.** Cabinets, countertops, old flooring, drywall, and a dead appliance or two add up fast, but a single-kitchen gut rarely overflows a 15. This is the classic use case.
- **Roofing tear-off.** The 1.5-ton allowance comfortably covers a single-layer tear-off on a typical College Park house. Those low walls make pitching shingles in genuinely easier than working over a taller box.
- **Two-bath gut.** Tile, vanities, tubs, and backer board from a pair of bathrooms fit well within the 15-yard volume and weight allowance.
- **Basement or attic cleanout.** Berwyn's older frame and bungalow homes — many built in the 1920s through the 1940s — tend to produce plaster, lath, and decades of stored debris. A 15 handles a full basement or attic clear-out without forcing you into a larger box you don't need.

Calvert Hills, down near the UMD campus, is another spot where the 15 earns its keep. Its early-1900s Colonial Revivals, Craftsman, and Tudor homes have often been converted into multi-unit rentals, and that turnover generates steady renovation and cleanout work — single rooms refreshed between tenants rather than whole-house teardowns. And every August, the university's lease cycle hits: most off-campus student houses run August-to-July leases, so late summer brings a wave of move-out cleanouts and landlord turnover hauling. A 15 is often the right call for clearing one house of left-behind furniture and junk without overshooting.

If your project is a full multi-room teardown or you're feeding heavy construction debris from the Route 1 redevelopment strip, that's usually a job for a bigger box — we're happy to talk you up to a 20 or 30 if that's the honest answer.

## Placement, permits, and getting it there

Here's where College Park gets specific. Old Town was platted in deep, narrow lots — the standard is about 50 by 200 feet — so driveways tend to be single-car width and homes sit close to their neighbors. The 15-yard's compact footprint is a real advantage on those lots; it slides into spots where a 20 or 30 would force tight backing or an awkward driveway-end drop. Tell us what your approach looks like and we'll plan the set accordingly.

Closer to campus and up in North College Park, plenty of homes have little or no driveway and rely on permit-only street parking. When a driveway truly can't take a box, street placement becomes the practical option — and the City of College Park requires a right-of-way permit for any roll-off set in the street, issued through Public Works. Driveways and private lots are generally permit-free, though we'd suggest confirming current City rules before placement, since municipal code on residential dumpsters does change. We can talk you through which situation you're in before we ever load the truck.

As for getting it to you: we're dispatched from Beltsville, so reaching College Park is a quick run straight down Route 1. That short hop means we can often be flexible on timing and quick to swing back for the pickup — one of the upsides of hiring a local family operation instead of a regional dispatcher.
