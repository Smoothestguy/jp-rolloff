## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard measures roughly 12 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall. That low profile is the whole point: it holds about three pickup-truck loads of debris and comes with a 1-ton (2,000-pound) weight allowance built into the price. Pricing is per material — priced by what you're putting in it, so call for a quote — and that rate includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with the 1-ton allowance. Go over that weight and extra tonnage is billed at $105 per additional ton; extra days run $5 a day, and pickup is on request when you call to schedule.

Because the walls sit low, you can walk heavy items right up to the side and tip them in instead of hoisting everything over your head. That makes the 10-yard the favorite for dense, heavy loads like dirt, brick, tile, and old roofing, where you'll hit the weight limit long before you fill the box. For a one-room project, that's exactly the trade-off you want.

This is the size people mean when they say the "garage cleanout" dumpster. It takes up about the same footprint as a single car, which matters more in Hyattsville than in most places — more on that below.

## Which Hyattsville jobs suit this size

The 10-yard shines on contained, single-area projects, and Hyattsville generates plenty of them.

**Garage cleanouts.** The detached garages so common in the older parts of town fill up fast with decades of paint cans, lumber scraps, broken furniture, and boxes nobody's opened since the last move. One 10-yard usually swallows a full garage with room to spare.

**Bathroom remodels.** A gut of a single bathroom — old tile, a cast-iron tub, vanity, drywall, and fixtures — lands neatly inside this size, and the heavy tile and tub fit comfortably under the 1-ton allowance for a typical one-bath tear-out.

**Yard waste and trees.** Hyattsville's mature, tree-lined streets mean storm cleanup, brush, branches, and the occasional removed tree. The low walls make it easy to pitch limbs and bagged yard waste straight in.

**Estate downsizing.** When a family home changes hands, the 10-yard is sized for clearing one level or one heavily packed room at a time without paying for capacity you'll never use.

## Local logistics, permits, and drive time

Hyattsville's biggest placement wrinkle comes straight from its history. The city grew up in the 1880s as one of the first streetcar and railroad suburbs of Washington, D.C., and the oldest neighborhoods were platted before cars. The Historic District — Downtown, Hyattsville Hills, and Castle Manor — sits on a tight gridiron of relatively narrow lots, rear alleys, and very few front-yard driveways. Many homes still rely on rear-alley access and detached garages rather than a wide drive out front. That changes where a roll-off can sit, and it's a big reason the compact 10-yard footprint is so useful here: it can tuck into a single parking spot or work off an alley where a longer box simply won't.

If we can set the box on your private driveway or in the back yard, you won't need a city permit. But if it has to go on a public street or shoulder — which is far more common in the older streetcar-suburb blocks than in the auto-era subdivisions — the City of Hyattsville requires a Street Access / Right-of-Way Usage permit, with a $50 fee, before a container goes down. City code also makes it unlawful to store materials on city streets or sidewalks without that permit, and any street obstruction has to be marked with a red lantern at night to warn passing traffic. Permits and questions run through the city's Office of Code Compliance, reachable at (301) 985-5032.

One thing worth checking: the Hyattsville ZIP codes (20781, 20782, 20783, 20784) reach well beyond the incorporated city. Areas overlapping Chillum, Adelphi, Mount Rainier, and University Park may sit outside the city limits, where Prince George's County rules apply instead of the city's right-of-way permit. If you're not sure which side of the line your address falls on, tell us when you call and we'll help sort it out before delivery day.
