## What fits in a 30-yard dumpster

The 30-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Greenbelt rental runs **$595 to $725, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises added after the fact.

That extra foot of wall height over the mid-size bins, stretched across 22 feet of length, is what makes this the maximum-capacity option. It is built for volume, not for tossing a few bags over the side. The trade-off is footprint: a 30-yard needs a long, straight run-up to set down level, plus clearance for the truck to position and lift it. On a commercial lot or an open office-park drive that is rarely a problem. In Greenbelt's tighter residential settings — the historic court lots, a narrow Greenbelt Station apron — it is the first thing we check, and sometimes the reason we will steer you to a 25-yard instead.

## Which Greenbelt jobs suit the 30-yard

The 30-yard earns its keep when the volume is genuinely large and a second haul would cost more in time and money than one bigger container. The jobs we drop it for most around Greenbelt:

- **Full demolition.** Tearing a structure down to the slab produces debris by the truckload. The 30-yard's nine-load capacity and 3-ton allowance are sized for exactly that kind of work, where smaller bins fill before you have made a dent.
- **Commercial site work.** Goddard is the city's signature institution — NASA's first space flight center, with roughly 10,000 civil servants and contractors — and the Capital Office Park / Ivy Lane corridor is an ~40-acre, ~810,000-sq-ft office complex that recently went through a roughly $7M modernization. That commercial and office base drives tenant fit-outs and build-out debris in bulk, which is classic 30-yard territory.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** When a job means clearing a large volume of mixed debris in one shot — a property clear-off, a large-scale junk removal — the 30-yard takes it in a single container instead of a string of smaller pickups.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** Greenbelt East is roughly 84% apartments and townhomes built between 1970 and 1999, and Greenbriar's condos date to about 1975. Turning over units across one of those communities at once generates far more than a single-family job, and the 30-yard handles a multi-unit cleanout without the back-and-forth of swapping bins.

It helps to know that Greenbelt is really several housing eras stacked in one city, because that changes whether this size is even the right call. The historic GHI row houses in Old Greenbelt sit on crawlspaces and boiler rooms rather than full basements, and the cooperative's multi-year Homes Improvement Program added insulation, air sealing, and vapor barriers down in those crawlspaces — so most work in the core is smaller weatherization, gut, and interior-renovation jobs that fit a 10 or 15-yard, not a 30. Greenbelt Station's 2010s Ryan Homes townhomes, with their narrow one-car-garage aprons, are firmly mid-size territory too. The 30-yard's home turf in Greenbelt is the commercial corridor and the larger multi-family turnover — the office fit-out, the apartment-building clear-out, the full teardown. If you are not sure your job is big enough to need this size, we will tell you straight rather than rent you more bin than you will fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is the big Greenbelt question, and the 30-yard makes it bigger.** In a typical suburb the dumpster goes beside the house. In Old Greenbelt it often cannot. The New Deal layout — a National Historic Landmark town of about 1,600 GHI cooperative row houses, built by the federal government starting in 1936 and 1937 with defense-era wood-frame units following in 1941 and 1942 — puts living space on the shared interior garden courts and pushes parking, trash, and services to the outer edge of each row. A roll-off serving a court home usually has to stage in the shared exterior parking lot or court drive rather than next to the unit, and the community's curvilinear streets, superblocks, and separated pedestrian walkways limit where a truck can position a container at all. For a box that needs 22 feet of straight run-up, that scouting matters. Most genuine 30-yard work in Greenbelt, though, lands on the commercial sites and apartment lots where the run-up and clearance are simply there — which is one more reason this size belongs more to the Goddard and Capital Office Park corridor and to Greenbelt East than to the historic core.

**GHI approval comes before placement in the core.** Any improvement, alteration, or addition to a GHI home requires a GHI permit, and contractors have to produce the approved permit for the scope of work before starting. Additions enroll in the Addition Maintenance Program, and crawlspace work means contacting the GHI Staff Engineer first. If your project somehow does call for a large container in Old Greenbelt, that means lining up the cooperative's approval for both the work and the staging spot. We can provide a service confirmation to forward to GHI or your board.

**The Greenbelt permit rule.** The City of Greenbelt requires a permit for placement in a city street or right-of-way, including most Old Greenbelt court parking. Driveways and HOA-approved staging typically do not. So in the historic core, where the realistic spot is often a shared court lot or court drive, a permit is frequently part of the plan. On a private commercial yard or an HOA-approved apartment lot — where most 30-yard work actually sits — it usually is not. The 1970s-to-90s HOAs of Greenbelt East and Greenbriar generally want their own approval and shared-lot staging before a container arrives, so give your board or property manager a heads-up and we will plan around the shared parking.

**Drive time.** Beltsville is about ten minutes out, and we dispatch every morning toward Greenbelt — the city sits just inside the Capital Beltway, near Greenbelt Park and not far from our base, so the haul is short. Most addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and early calls often make the same-day run.
