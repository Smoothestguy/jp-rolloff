## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. It carries a 2.5-ton (5,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Takoma Park a typical 25-yard rental runs **$525 to $625, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises tacked on afterward.

The step up from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a few extra feet. That extra capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise need a mid-job swap. The walls stay at five feet, so a crew can walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but there is meaningfully more room behind those walls.

The advice we give every customer on this size in Takoma Park is blunt: **measure twice before booking.** Twenty feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck to set it down and for the rear door to swing, is a long footprint. On the small lots and short driveways common in the historic district, that footprint is the whole question. Send us a photo or measurements of where you want it, and we will tell you honestly whether the 25-yard fits or whether two hauls of a smaller can would serve you better.

## Which Takoma Park jobs suit the 25-yard

The dominant housing stock here is late-Victorian and early-20th-century — Queen Anne and Stick/Shingle Victorians with ornate woodwork and front porches, Colonial Revival Foursquares, and Craftsman bungalows mostly built in the 1920s or earlier. Old houses generate big jobs, and these are the ones the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out the back of an Old Takoma Victorian or adding onto a North Takoma Foursquare throws off framing lumber, old siding, lath, and demolition debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full gut on a century-old home — pulling plaster, knob-and-tube wiring, and old flooring down to the studs across kitchen, baths, and living space all at once — is a classic 25-yard job. The plaster and lath alone run heavy, which is where the 2.5-ton allowance earns its place.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** Bigger or multi-layer roofs on these older homes run heavy with asphalt shingle, and the 25-yard's weight allowance gives more headroom than the smaller sizes.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** Along the Maple Avenue corridor, the city's multi-family pocket of mid-century apartments and condos, unit turnovers and HOA projects can produce a lot of material fast. The 25-yard handles those, and **commercial accounts get net-15 terms** so billing keeps pace with the work.

If your project is a single-room redo or a routine cleanout, a 20-yard or 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will say so. The 25-yard earns its keep when the job is genuinely large.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and the tree canopy

**Driveway placement, strongly preferred — and here it is more than a preference.** The City of Takoma Park's Public Works Department issues the permit for placing a dumpster or container in a city right-of-way, and many blocks ban street placement entirely. The city is explicit that **no permit is required if the dumpster sits in your driveway**, and it warns that you may not place a container in the right-of-way until you have received written notice that the permit is granted. On the small historic-district lots common here, the driveway is often tight for a 20-foot container, so we will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**Mind the tree canopy.** Takoma Park is a certified Tree City USA with one of the toughest tree-protection ordinances in Montgomery County — removing or significantly pruning a tree needs a city permit, with fines up to $1,000. The flip side is a dense, mature hardwood canopy of tall tulip trees and big street trees, which means low overhanging limbs are a genuine constraint when we spot the can and when the truck makes its delivery and pickup. We plan the approach around the limbs rather than through them. Properties backing onto the flood-prone Sligo Creek stream valley can also have soft or sloped placement options, so we will talk those through before delivery day.

**Tight, hilly streets.** Takoma Park sits on the fall line and is notably hilly, with narrow gridded streets set at odd angles — even Carroll Avenue, Old Takoma's main street, pinches to two lanes within the city. We place carefully on these streets, which is one more reason a planned driveway drop beats an improvised curbside one.

**Drive time.** Takoma Park is a short run from our Beltsville yard, so early calls routinely make a same-day run and most others are scheduled inside 24 hours. A note on the edges: the city straddles the Montgomery / Prince George's County line, so a project on the eastern edge can fall under PG County for non-city right-of-way matters — we will sort that out with you when we confirm the address.
