## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

This is the "major construction" size — the second-largest container most homeowners and contractors ever need, and the one that earns its keep on dense, high-volume work. The 25-yard's roughly 10.5-pickup-load capacity is the difference between finishing a big project in one haul and stacking debris in the yard while you wait on a second container.

A few honest words on weight. The 2-ton allowance is generous for mixed construction debris — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, flooring, trim, and general demo. But dense materials add up fast. A full load of plaster and lath, tile, or layered asphalt shingles can hit the tonnage cap well before the box looks full, so if your load runs heavy rather than bulky, tell us up front and we will steer you to the right plan. For voluminous-but-lighter debris — a gutted interior, a whole-house remodel back to the studs — the 25-yard's footprint is exactly where it shines.

Because this is a true major-construction size, measure twice before booking. For longer driveways and bigger builds the 25 is ideal, but a single bathroom or a modest declutter belongs in a smaller, cheaper box. When in doubt, call and describe the job — we would rather right-size it than oversell you.

## Which Cheverly jobs suit the 25-yard

Cheverly is a small, tree-lined bedroom community of historic single-family homes — just 1.35 square miles, all of it residential character, sitting about a mile from the northeastern edge of Washington, D.C. The housing stock is dominated by detached single-family homes built largely between 1921 and the 1960s: the original developer-built houses from 1921 to 1925, then Cape Cods, Colonial Revivals, ramblers, and mid-century ranch and split-level homes. When owners of these houses go big rather than cosmetic, the project lands squarely in 25-yard territory.

A **home addition** on one of Cheverly's older single-family homes generates framing offcuts, removed siding, excavated interior finishes, and packaging in real volume — exactly what a 25-yard is sized to absorb. A **whole-house remodel** is the most common reason crews here reach for this box: Cheverly holds Prince George's County's largest documented collection of 1920s mail-order and kit houses, 34 homes from Sears, Roebuck & Co. and the McClure Homes Company, now part of the National Register Cheverly Historic District. Renovating a century-old kit house means tearing out plaster, lath, original wood, and dated additions — a steady stream of debris that a smaller container simply cannot stay ahead of. (If your project is a lighter restoration of one of these historic homes, a 15- or 20-yard often fits better; the 25 is for the full gut.)

A **large roofing** tear-off on a Colonial Revival or a rambler is a classic 25-yard job, though keep the tonnage front of mind, since shingles are among the heaviest things you can load. And for a **commercial cleanout** — Cheverly mixes its residential streets with light-commercial and institutional sites, from the Prince George's County Health Department and the Publick Playhouse to the former hospital campus on Hospital Drive after operations moved to Largo — the 25 gives crews room to clear a large space quickly. Commercial accounts welcome — ask about billing, which keeps the paperwork simple when several jobs are running at once.

## Permits, placement, and drive time

Here is the part Cheverly homeowners are usually relieved to hear. The Town of Cheverly requires a permit only when a dumpster sits in the street or public right-of-way. If your 25-yard goes on your own driveway, no permit is needed. For the cases that do touch the street, the Town's turnaround runs about 24 to 48 hours, so it is worth a quick call to us a couple of days ahead so the timing lines up with your delivery.

Placement deserves a real minute of planning in Cheverly, more so than in flat, grid-platted DMV suburbs. The town was deliberately laid out as a Tree City USA "island of green" on rolling, wooded contours, with winding streets and a mature, protected oak canopy. That character is the whole charm of the place — and it is also a practical consideration for an 18-foot box. Mature overhanging limbs mean we need clear overhead access where the truck lifts and sets the container, so a spot without low branches or wires matters. The curving, narrow streets reward staging on the driveway rather than the road wherever possible. And because lots sit on the town's gently rolling grades rather than a flat plain, a sloped driveway may need a little leveling so the container sits stable and the doors swing true. Send us a photo or a quick description of your driveway and the trees around it, and we will confirm the fit before we roll.

From our Beltsville dispatch, Cheverly is a short 20-minute run, with U.S. Route 50 bordering the community for direct truck access. That compact footprint and easy highway approach let us reach the whole town — from the historic district to the streets near the Metro station — without it ever becoming a logistics headache.
