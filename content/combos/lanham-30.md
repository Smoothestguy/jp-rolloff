## What fits in a 30-yard

The 30-yard gives you the same 8-foot width and 6-foot walls as our mid-size bins, but the extra length stretches it to a full 22 feet, and that volume changes what kinds of jobs it can absorb. Think nine pickup-truck loads of debris in one container. It comes with a 3-ton weight allowance — that is 6,000 pounds included before any overage applies — and the typical all-in price runs $595 to $725 with delivery, pickup, and that tonnage built in. No separate trip fees, no surprise line items at the end.

Where the 30-yard earns its keep is on light, bulky, high-volume material: framing lumber, drywall, cabinetry, fixtures, furniture, packaging, and general construction debris that fills space fast without piling on weight. If your debris is dense — think concrete, brick, dirt, roofing tear-off, or other heavy aggregate — a smaller box is usually the smarter call, because dense material hits the weight ceiling long before it fills 30 yards of volume. We are happy to talk through your material on the phone and point you to the right size; we would rather get you the correct bin the first time than send a 30-yard to a job that wanted a 20.

## Placement, permits, and the run-up

Lanham is an unincorporated community in Prince George's County, ZIP 20706, with no town government of its own, so county DPIE rules govern placement rather than a municipal office. The permit question comes down to one thing: where the box sits.

If the 30-yard goes entirely on a private driveway, you do not need a permit. That is the common, easy path for most of Lanham's mid-century single-family homes, which sit on their own lots with their own driveways and generally have the room to take it. If any part of the container has to sit on a public street or county right-of-way, you will need a PG County right-of-way permit first. That situation comes up most on the newer townhouse rows, where driveways are narrow or shared and street placement is often the only option, and along the busy Greenway Center / Annapolis Road commercial corridor, where there is simply no off-street room.

One more wrinkle worth flagging: the City of Glenarden borders Lanham to the west along MD Route 704 (Martin Luther King Jr. Highway), and Glenarden is an incorporated city with its own authority. Jobs right along that western edge can straddle the Lanham county line and the Glenarden city line, which answer to different placement rules — so if your address is out near Glenarden or the NASA Goddard border, mention it when you call and we will help sort out which authority applies.

The 30-yard is our longest box, and it needs a long, clear run-up to set down level — a straight approach with no tight turns, low branches, or parked cars in the way. Lanham's CDP is entirely urban and suburban with no rural land, so off-street space on smaller lots can be tight; the more you can tell us about the driveway, the approach, and any overhead obstacles, the smoother the drop goes. If you are unsure whether your spot can take a 22-foot container, describe it to us and we will tell you straight.
