## Placement, permits, and the tight-driveway problem

Here's the defining challenge of a 25-yard rental in Silver Spring: finding the space for it. Unlike the larger-lot suburbs further out in the DMV, Silver Spring's near-in subdivisions — Woodside, Four Corners, Kemp Mill, the Wheaton border — are dense, pre-1960 layouts with narrow streets and short driveways. Plenty of homes back onto the Sligo Creek stream valley, where the roughly 10-mile trail and surrounding parkland thread past neighborhoods like Woodside, Four Corners, and Long Branch; properties near that floodplain often have no real choice but driveway-only placement.

The reason driveway placement is worth fighting for comes down to permits. Silver Spring is unincorporated Montgomery County, so permits run through MCDPS rather than a city hall. A dumpster set on your own private property or driveway generally needs no special county permit for a short-term job. But the moment a container sits in a county roadway, you need a Public Right-of-Way Permit — and those expire six months from issuance, can require traffic-control devices like cones and barricades, and on high-volume roads or in business districts may demand a full traffic-control plan. State or municipal roads require a permit from that jurisdiction instead. For a quick home addition or re-roof, that's a lot of process to avoid, which is exactly why we work hard to fit the box on your property.

Two more things worth a heads-up. Even with a county right-of-way permit, your homeowners association may have its own rules and may require approval before a container goes down, so check with them early. And in Kemp Mill specifically — home to one of the largest Orthodox Jewish communities on the East Coast, anchored by Young Israel Shomrai Emunah and several synagogues — Sabbath-observant households often need delivery and pickup scheduled to avoid Friday evening through Saturday. Tell us when you book and we'll plan the run around it.
