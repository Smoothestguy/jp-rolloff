## Which Riverdale Park jobs suit this size

Riverdale Park is an old town, and that shapes the work here. The median home was built in 1943, and dwellings in the town date back to 1890, so most projects are renovations, additions, knob-and-tube and plaster removal, and full gut-rehabs of pre-WWII wood-frame houses rather than new builds. The Riverdale Park Historic District, listed on the National Register in 2002, is an eclectic mix of Queen Anne, Colonial Revival, Craftsman, Tudor Revival, American Foursquares, and bungalows, ranging from large 2.5-story houses down to compact cottages. The bigger of those houses generate serious tear-out volume when you gut them down to the studs, and that is exactly where a 30-yard earns its place. A full interior demolition of a Foursquare or a large Queen Anne can fill a 30 in a way that would have meant two trips with anything smaller.

There is a second, distinctive driver of big-bin demand here, and it is water. Riverdale Park is one of the most flood-prone towns in the area. The Northeast Branch of the Anacostia River runs through it, and heavy storms have repeatedly caused flash flooding, enough that town officials have pushed the county for better stormwater infrastructure. When a basement or ground floor floods, the cleanup is rarely small: soaked drywall, ruined flooring, waterlogged furniture, and dead appliances all have to go at once. A 30-yard lets you clear a flooded level in a single push instead of stretching the job over multiple hauls while damp material sits and gets worse. If you are dealing with a flood cleanout layered on top of a renovation, the maximum-capacity bin is usually the sensible choice.

The 30 also fits the commercial and multi-family side of town. The Route 1 / Baltimore Avenue corridor and the Riverdale Park Station development near the MARC Camden Line stop bring commercial tenant turnovers, retail build-outs, and apartment and townhome cleanouts into the mix. For a commercial site cleanup, a bulk waste haul, or clearing several units in a multi-family property, the 30-yard moves the most material per trip and keeps the project on schedule.

## Placement, permits, and drive time

The one thing to plan for with a 30-yard is room. It is a long container and it needs a decent run-up to set down cleanly. That is worth thinking about in Riverdale Park specifically. The town was platted in 1889 as a planned railroad villa park, with three original sections laid out on relatively uniform lots and setbacks, which means a lot of properties have narrow, consistent frontages and modest driveways. Many driveways will still take a 30, but not all of them, and the historic blocks around the central ellipse and the Riversdale Mansion neighborhood can be tight. When you call, describe your driveway or the spot you have in mind and we will tell you straight whether the 30 will fit or whether you would be better served by a different placement. We would rather sort that out on the phone than send a truck that cannot set the bin.

On permits, the rule in the Town of Riverdale Park is straightforward. If the dumpster goes on your own driveway, you typically do not need a permit. If it has to sit in the public right-of-way, on the street or in the parking lane, the town requires a permit, and that generally takes one to two business days to arrange. On the close-set, narrow-frontage blocks here, right-of-way placement comes up more often than in newer neighborhoods, so build that day or two into your timeline if the driveway is not an option. We are glad to talk through which path applies to your address before you commit to a date.
