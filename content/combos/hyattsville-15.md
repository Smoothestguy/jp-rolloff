## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly four-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and around Hyattsville a typical 15-yard rental runs **$425 to $485, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprise after the fact.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides sit low enough that you can pitch debris over the rail without a ramp or a wrestling match, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or hauling it up from a basement. But there is still enough volume to hold a genuine single-room job from start to finish. That is why we think of the 15 as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would have you paying for empty air.

## Which Hyattsville jobs suit the 15-yard

The 15 lands right in the sweet spot for the kind of one-room, one-system projects that Hyattsville's older housing stock throws off constantly. The jobs we see it used for most here:

- **Kitchen remodels.** Tearing the cabinets, counters, and flooring out of a bungalow or Colonial Revival kitchen fills a 15-yard just about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare.
- **Roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on a Cape Cod, bungalow, or red-brick Colonial fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most modest roofs. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage before we drop the container.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing out of a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20.
- **Basement cleanouts.** Clearing a finished or half-finished basement, or hauling out the debris from finishing one, works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell far easier.

Those jobs show up across town, from the Hyattsville Arts District and Downtown out through West Hyattsville, University Hills, Castle Manor, and along the Riverdale border. ## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is the real conversation in Hyattsville.** This town's streetcar-suburb DNA shapes where a roll-off can actually sit. The Historic District — Hyattsville Hills, Downtown, and Castle Manor — was platted before the automobile on a tight gridiron of narrow lots, with rear alleys and detached garages instead of the wide front-yard driveways you find in auto-era suburbs. One representative historic lot runs only about 39 feet wide by 57.5 feet deep, served by a 15-foot rear alley. So unlike a newer DMV subdivision where the container just drops into a broad driveway, a Hyattsville set often means working an alley, threading past a detached garage, or planning around a lot with no front drive at all. A 15-yard needs roughly 14 feet of straight length plus clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and the 15's compact footprint is part of why it suits these older blocks better than a longer container would. We will scout the spot with you so the box fits the footprint you actually have.

Conditions shift by neighborhood. University Hills sits up against the University of Maryland in College Park, while West Hyattsville is bordered by the Northwest Branch of the Anacostia River — and lots near that stream valley and the Anacostia Tributary Trail corridor can bring floodplain, soft ground, and limited access into play, all of which affect where a heavy roll-off can safely rest. The surrounding subdivisions hold more mid-century Cape Cods, ranchers, and red-brick Colonial Revivals, which tend to offer more conventional driveway room than the gridded historic core.

**The Hyattsville permit rule.** If the container sits entirely on your private driveway or in your back yard, you do **not** need a permit. The moment it has to sit on a public street or shoulder, the City of Hyattsville requires a Street Access / Right-of-Way Usage permit, which carries a $50 fee. City code makes it unlawful to store materials in the street or on the sidewalk without that permit from the Director of Public Works, and any obstruction has to be marked with a red lantern at night to warn passing traffic. Because so many historic-district homes lack a front driveway, street placement — and that permit — comes up far more often here than in the auto-era suburbs we serve. One wrinkle worth knowing: the four Hyattsville ZIP codes (20781, 20782, 20783, 20784) reach beyond the incorporated city, so not every "Hyattsville" address sits on a City-maintained street. The City's permit rule only applies inside the city limits; addresses in unincorporated pockets fall under Prince George's County instead. Permit questions in the city run through the Office of Code Compliance at (301) 985-5032, and we are glad to help you sort out which rules apply to your block before we deliver.
