## Placement, permits, and the floodplain question

Here's where Bladensburg is genuinely different from the spread-out suburbs around it. The town packs roughly 9,657 residents (2020 Census) onto about one square mile of land, which works out to a population density near 9,000 people per square mile. That means narrow lots, short driveways, and limited curbside frontage on a lot of blocks, especially in the older Decatur Heights grid where setbacks are tight.

Because of that, driveway placement is the practical default here, and it's the easier path on permits, too. The Town of Bladensburg requires a permit when a dumpster sits in the street or public right-of-way, but placing it in your driveway is permit-free, with same-week turnaround. On Bladensburg's compact, in-town streets, a street-placed container would intrude on traffic anyway, so the driveway is usually the better spot for everyone. If your only option is the street, let us know up front and we'll help you sort out the right-of-way permit before we deliver.

The other thing we always check in Bladensburg is the ground a loaded box will sit on. Bladensburg is one of the four Port Towns, clustered with Colmar Manor, Cottage City, and Edmonston at the junction of the Anacostia's Northwest and Northeast branches, and that river has shaped the town. Severe floods in 1933, 1942, and 1954 led to a flood-control project completed in the late 1950s that straightened and dredged the river and built levees along both banks. Low-lying parcels near the Anacostia corridor and the Edmonston border still sit in or near the floodplain, which can affect ground conditions for a heavy container.

On top of that, the town has invested in green stormwater infrastructure to protect the Anacostia, including permeable surfaces like the roughly 11,160 square feet of Grasspave2 porous paving installed at the historic Bostwick House. Permeable and grass-paved surfaces can't bear a loaded 20-yard dumpster, so if your property has one, we'll work out a solid spot together rather than risk the surface. A loaded 20-yard is heavy, and we'd rather plan the placement than fix a problem later.

If it helps to describe your spot by landmark, the Port Towns area, the Anacostia tributary corridor, and reference points like Bladensburg Waterfront Park, the George Washington House on Annapolis Road, or the Bostwick House all give us a quick read on where you are and what the access looks like.
