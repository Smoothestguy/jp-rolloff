## What a 20-yard dumpster holds

A 20-yard container measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall. The low 4-foot walls are the part that matters most for the kind of work Silver Spring homes generate: they're low enough to walk a wheelbarrow up to and toss bags over without a ladder, but the 22-foot floor still swallows a serious volume. Figure on about nine pickup-truck loads of material in a single container.

Pricing is per material — a 20-yard is quote-based, priced by what you load, so call for a quote. That rate includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a two-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance. Extra days run $5 each, and weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton, so the final number depends on what you load. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule it. For most household and mid-size renovation debris, two tons is plenty of room. The jobs that bump up against the weight limit tend to be the heavy-material ones (we'll flag those below), and if your project sounds like one of those, we'll tell you straight when you call rather than letting you find out at pickup.

## Which Silver Spring jobs suit this size

This is the "whole-house cleanout" container, and Silver Spring has no shortage of houses that need one. The near-in neighborhoods here skew older and well-established — Woodside Park was platted back in 1922 and built up through the 1950s, and county historic-planning staff have called it the best-preserved early-20th-century neighborhood in Montgomery County. Homes of that vintage move hands, get cleared out, and get reworked, and a 20-yard is the natural size for emptying one.

A few of the projects we see most often on this container:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** Clearing a home before a sale, after a move, or in an estate situation. The compact mid-century homes in Four Corners and South Four Corners — Cape Cods, ramblers, split-levels, and Tudors built largely from the 1930s through the '60s — fill a 20-yard cleanly without overshooting into a bigger box you'd be paying for half-empty.

- **Estate cleanouts.** Decades of accumulated household goods, furniture, and miscellany. The volume is high but the weight stays manageable, which is what a 20-yard is built for.
- **Mid-size roofing.** A single-family tear-off where you don't need the full heft of a dedicated roofing container. Here's the one caution: roofing shingles are dense, so a mid-size roof can eat into that two-ton allowance faster than a cleanout will. If your project is a smaller single-room refresh, a larger size might be overkill; if it's a heavy demolition or a big addition, you may want to size up. For the broad middle — the everyday cleanout and remodel that most Silver Spring homeowners are actually doing — the 20-yard is the sweet spot.

## Placement, permits, and the tight-street reality

Here's where Silver Spring is genuinely different from the lower-density, big-lot suburbs elsewhere in the DMV. The near-in neighborhoods — Woodside, Four Corners, Kemp Mill, and out toward the Wheaton border — are dense pre-1960 subdivisions with narrow streets and short driveways. Plenty of homes back onto the Sligo Creek stream valley and its parkland, which can sit in or near the floodplain and effectively forces driveway-only placement. So the defining challenge here isn't space in general; it's fitting the container on tight private property.

The good news is that a 20-yard, at 22 feet, still slides into most two-car driveways with a little planning. When we can set it on your driveway or private property, the permit question largely disappears — Silver Spring is unincorporated Montgomery County, and containers on private land are generally allowed for short-term jobs without a special county permit.

The street is a different story, and it's worth understanding before you assume you can just drop the box at the curb. Because the area is unincorporated, road placement goes through the Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services (MCDPS). Putting a dumpster in a county roadway requires a Public Right-of-Way Permit. Those permits run for six months from issuance and can require traffic-control devices like cones or barricades — and on higher-volume roads or in business districts, an actual traffic-control plan. For a quick home cleanout, that's a lot of process to take on, which is exactly why driveway placement is usually the smarter route. (If your street is a state or municipal roadway rather than a county one, the permit comes from that jurisdiction instead.)

A couple of local notes worth keeping in mind. If your neighborhood has a homeowners association, it may have its own rules about containers even when the county side is settled, so it's worth a quick check before delivery day. And in Kemp Mill specifically — home to one of the largest Orthodox Jewish communities on the East Coast — we're glad to schedule delivery and pickup around the Sabbath, avoiding Friday evening through Saturday. Just let us know.

For the historic pockets — the National Park Seminary district in Forest Glen, or restoration work on Woodside Park's older homes — placement and debris handling can carry historic-property considerations, and we'll work with you on access where those apply. Downtown Silver Spring, by contrast, is dense mid- and high-rise development around Ellsworth Drive, so work there tends to be commercial or contractor jobs with tight street access rather than open driveways; if that's your situation, tell us the constraints up front and we'll plan the drop accordingly.
