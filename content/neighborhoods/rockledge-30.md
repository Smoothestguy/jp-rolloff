## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and our typical pricing runs $595 to $725 — a number that already includes delivery, pickup, and the included tonnage, so there are no surprise line items waiting at the end.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Placement on Rockledge's driveways

Here is where Rockledge is genuinely easy compared with a lot of DMV neighborhoods. As an original Levitt section, it is a non-gated, established subdivision of detached single-family homes, and nearly every one sits on its own private driveway. Unlike Bowie's newer townhome rows or gated communities, that means a 30-yard here can almost always be dropped on the homeowner's own driveway rather than out in the street.

That matters because of how the city handles permits. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Rockledge is one of the older Levitt sections rather than a modern dues-and-architectural-control subdivision, staging on your private driveway typically does not require an association sign-off either, and a street permit ends up being the exception rather than the rule. If your only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's Public Works Department handles that right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.

A 30-yard does ask for a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes — it is a big box on a long frame, and the driver needs room to back the trailer in straight. On a Rockledge lot that usually works fine, but it is worth a look before we arrive. And because every street in the section starts with the letter R — Rockledge Drive, Reston Lane, and the rest — telling us your exact street, whether you are over near Rockledge Park on Reston Lane or closer to Rockledge Elementary, helps us route the truck and trailer cleanly the first time.
