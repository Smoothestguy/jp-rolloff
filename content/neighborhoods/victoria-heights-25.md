## Why the 25-yard fits Victoria Heights specifically

Victoria Heights is one of the last "V"-section neighborhoods of Levitt & Sons' Belair at Bowie development, built circa 1964–1968 in the final wave before Levitt shifted to townhomes over in Belair Town and Lake Village. That timing matters more than it sounds. The earlier Belair sections leaned on smaller starter models — the entry-level Cape Cods and the like — but Victoria Heights came up at the end, when Levitt was selling its larger detached homes: the Ranchers, the three- and four-bedroom Colonials, and the top-of-the-line Country Clubber, marketed as "a luxurious house reflecting the best of American country colonial design." 

That means the V-section is dominated by full-size 1960s ramblers, colonials, and split-levels — and that housing stock is now 55 to 60 years old. Homes built in that window are hitting the major-renovation cycle all at once: kitchens and baths getting gutted to the studs, basements getting finished, roofs and siding coming off whole. Those are exactly the jobs that overwhelm a 15- or 20-yard box. When you're emptying an entire house instead of a single room, the bigger Country Clubbers and Colonials throw off enough debris that the 25-yard is the honest call.

A 25-yard is a tall box. ## The Bowie permit question — usually a non-issue here

Here's where Victoria Heights customers tend to come out ahead. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging don't require one. Because nearly every home in the V-section has its own driveway on that quarter-acre lot, the 25-yard goes on your own property and you skip the city permit entirely.

There's a second wrinkle that works in your favor. The original Levitt detached sections of Belair — Victoria Heights among them — were built as conventional single-family subdivisions and are generally marketed as no-HOA neighborhoods. The associations covering these streets are typically voluntary civic associations, not mandatory HOAs with architectural-control bonds and approval committees. For a big remodel that means fewer hoops before the box arrives. We'd still suggest a courtesy check: the City of Bowie keeps an official homeowners/civic association directory, so if you're unsure whether your specific street has any standing rules, it's worth a two-minute look before a multi-week project.
