## Why the 30-yard is the right call for big Two Rivers jobs

The 30-yard is our "commercial and demo" size. It's the one you order when a smaller bin would mean a second haul, and a second haul means a second trip charge and a stalled job. For the work that actually generates this much volume, the math almost always favors going big once.

In Two Rivers, that tends to mean a handful of specific jobs:

- **Full demolition** — taking a structure, a large addition, or an entire interior down to the studs.
- **Commercial site work** — clearing or fitting out space around the community's amenity hubs and shared buildings.
- **Bulk waste hauls** — a single load of heavy, high-volume material that a 10 or 20 simply can't swallow.
- **Multi-family cleanouts** — clearing out a cluster of units or a shared building in one coordinated push.

If your project is a kitchen refresh or a single-room cleanout, this is more box than you need, and we'll happily steer you to a 10 or 20 instead. But when the debris is going to keep coming for days, the 30 is what keeps one dumpster on site from start to finish.

## Placement: long driveways, tight villa lots, and HOA staging

The 30-yard is our longest box, and it needs a long, straight run-up to set down clean. On the larger single-family lots in villages like The Heritage, that's rarely a problem — there's usually driveway and frontage to work with. Where we slow down and plan more carefully is in the 55-plus active-adult sections such as The Regency and the villas, where driveways tend to be shorter and lots are clustered or share access. On those layouts, a full-length 30 can be a tight fit, and we'll talk through whether the 30 truly fits or whether staging it differently makes more sense.

Two Rivers is also an amenity-dense, trail-laced community — the Founders Club, the community park, and seven-plus miles of trails mean some interior streets and shared parking areas are narrow for a loaded truck. We'd rather know the address and the lot ahead of time so we can pick an approach that doesn't block a neighbor or scrape a turn. A quick description of your driveway and street when you call lets us tell you honestly whether the 30 will seat properly.

Because this is an HOA community, staging approval is a genuine step here, not an afterthought. Several sections have active homeowners' associations with their own rules about where equipment can sit and for how long. If you can confirm HOA approval for the spot before we roll, the drop goes smoothly. If you're not sure what your association allows, mention it when you call and we'll work with what you can get cleared.

## A note on permits in Two Rivers

Permit guidance for Two Rivers is different from what you'll see on our pages for neighborhoods inside the City of Bowie. Because Two Rivers is in Odenton, Anne Arundel County, the City of Bowie's right-of-way rule does not govern addresses here. As a practical matter, if your 30-yard sits on your own driveway or on an HOA-approved staging spot — which is where the vast majority of these go — you're generally clear of any street right-of-way permit question.

The one situation that calls for a closer look is a dumpster that would sit in a public street rather than on private property. In that case the relevant rules are the county's and the state's, not the City of Bowie's, and we'd rather confirm the right requirement with you before delivery than guess. We're not going to invent a permit answer for a street placement we haven't checked. Call us with the specifics and we'll help you figure out the correct path.
