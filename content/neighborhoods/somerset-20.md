Somerset is where Bowie began. When Levitt & Sons opened the Belair at Bowie model homes on Sussex Lane on October 8, 1960 — drizzly weather and all, with roughly 20,000 people turning out to walk through the manor-house model at the end of the lane — Somerset was the very first section of the development to go up, alongside Buckingham and Kenilworth. The families who bought those original Cape Cods and ranchers started moving in around 1962. That makes nearly every house in Somerset a roughly 65-year-old, modest single-family home on a mid-century lot with a standard single-width driveway. And that history is exactly why, when a Somerset homeowner finally tackles the big project, the 20-yard is the size they almost always land on — and rarely regret.

We are JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler, and we know this stretch of Bowie. Somerset's "S-street" layout — every street in the section starts with the same letter, so you get Sussex, and the rest of the S-named streets running off it — is one of the easiest neighborhoods in the county for a driver to find an address in, and we plan our morning runs around it.

## Why the 20-yard fits Somerset homes

The 20-yard measures 22 feet long by 8 feet wide by 4 feet tall and holds about nine pickup-truck loads of debris. In Somerset a 20-yard **includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental — priced by material, so call for a quote**. Pricing is per material rather than a single price for any load: additional days are available on request when you book, and dense or heavy materials such as concrete, dirt, brick, and shingles are quoted separately because weight matters, and pickup is on request — just call to schedule.

This is the container people in the trade call the "whole-house cleanout" size, and it earns the name in a neighborhood like this one. Somerset's original Levitt Cape Cods (which sold new for roughly $13,900 to $14,990 depending on bedroom count) and ranchers (around $15,500) are compact, well-built houses — not sprawling new-construction homes. A 20-yard is big enough to swallow a real project without paying for space you won't fill, and at four feet tall its walls are low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side without renting a ramp. For a 65-year-old house on a 65-year-old lot, that balance is just right.

Placement is the one thing worth planning for. At 22 feet long, the 20-yard needs a good run of straight driveway plus clearance for the rear door to swing. Many original Somerset homes have a single-width driveway, and most handle the 20-yard when we set it square — but it is longer than the smaller boxes, so we will scout the spot with you before the truck commits.

## The Somerset jobs that call for a 20-yard

Because Somerset's housing stock is the oldest in Levitt-Bowie, the work here skews heavily toward renovation, remodel, and clearing out long-held homes — not the large-lot, new-build staging you see in Bowie's newer sections. The 20-yard is built for exactly those jobs:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** Clearing one of these original Capes or ranchers between owners, or after decades of one family living in it, fills a 20-yard about right. This is the size's signature job, and Somerset gives us plenty of it.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-bath gut, a run of rooms getting new drywall and flooring, or a basement finish in a 1960s rancher is the classic 20-yard project. Interior updates on these homes are common, and the debris stream fits this container far better than a giant construction roll-off.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When a longtime Somerset family home changes hands — and after 65 years, many do — the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and a houseful of accumulated belongings in a single container without a swap-out.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical single-layer asphalt tear-off on a Somerset-sized roof fits comfortably; shingles are dense, so let us know it is a roofing job and we will quote it accordingly. Roof and siding replacement is a routine project on houses of this vintage.

If your project is genuinely smaller — say, a single-room declutter — a 10 or 15-yard may serve you better, and we will say so. If you are doing something closer to a full structural teardown, we will steer you up to a 30. But for the renovation, remodel, and cleanout work that defines Somerset, the 20-yard is usually the honest answer.

## Placement, permits, and getting it on your driveway

**Why we aim for the driveway.** Somerset's lots are modest and its driveways are typically single-width, so placement here is about scouting the right spot rather than worrying about size. A 20-yard needs roughly 22 feet of straight length, a few feet of side clearance, and an open swing for the rear door. On most Somerset driveways that works fine, and we lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface. We will look at your spot with you before the truck commits to it.

**The permit rule is simple.** The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit if the dumpster sits in a city street. If your container goes on your own driveway — or on HOA-approved staging — you do **not** need a permit. Since the great majority of Somerset jobs go right on the driveway, most of our customers here never deal with permitting at all. If the street turns out to be the only realistic spot for your address, we will flag the permit step before we deliver so there are no surprises.

**Drive time and dispatch.** Somerset is an easy run up Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, so most Somerset jobs are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day delivery. The "S-street" wayfinding means our drivers find your block without a hunt — once we have a Sussex Lane or other S-named address, we know right where we are headed.
