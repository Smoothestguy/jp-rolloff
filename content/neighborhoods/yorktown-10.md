## Why the 10-yard fits Yorktown homes

Yorktown is almost entirely free-standing single-family housing — roughly 99.8% detached single-family homes, a higher share than about 98.6% of U.S. neighborhoods. In plain terms, nearly every property here has its own private driveway, which is exactly the surface a roll-off is meant to sit on. The houses themselves are mostly medium-to-large three-to-five-bedroom homes, with reported living areas spanning roughly 1,512 to 2,807 square feet. That's real space inside, but these are mid-century lots, so the staging room outside the front door is genuine but finite.

That's where the 10-yard earns its place. Its footprint is just 12 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 3.5 feet tall — about the size of a single car spot. We call the 10-yard the "garage cleanout" size for a reason: it's the smallest footprint we offer, so you handle a one-area job without paying for cubic yards you'll never fill. On Yorktown's 55-to-60-year-old Levitt homes, the projects that fit it cleanly tend to be these:

- **Bathroom remodel.** Tearing out one of these original 1960s baths — tile, vanity, tub, subfloor — produces heavy debris but not high volume. That's precisely where the low 3.5-foot walls and the 1-ton allowance pull their weight.

## Staging it on your driveway — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's where Yorktown's layout works squarely in your favor. These are detached homes on private driveways, and the Yorktown at Belair area has no HOA — so placement on your own driveway is governed by you and city rules, not by an association sign-off. That's a real difference from Bowie's newer townhome and HOA communities, where staging often needs approval first. In Yorktown, you decide.

And because nearly every 10-yard here sits entirely on the homeowner's driveway, you generally do **not** need a City of Bowie right-of-way permit. The city only requires that permit when a dumpster sits in a city street; driveways don't trigger it. If the only workable spot truly is the street — a tight turnaround on a cul-de-sac like Youngwood Turn, say — just tell us when you call and we'll walk you through the right-of-way permit so you're covered. Given the 10-yard's small footprint, clearing a single-car spot the night before is usually all the prep you'll need.
