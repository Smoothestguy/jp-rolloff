## Which Old Town Bowie jobs suit the 15-yard

The housing stock here is overwhelmingly wood-frame and old — the MNCPPC historic survey documents roughly 590 buildings ranging from about 1880 to the present, packed into a small footprint. That age and that construction drive a very particular kind of work: gut-rehabs, tear-offs, and one-room renovations of homes that are genuinely a century-plus old, not the standardized 1960s tract remodels you see in the rest of Bowie. The 15-yard handles that one-room rhythm well:

- **Kitchen remodels.** Pulling cabinets, countertops, flooring, and old plaster from one kitchen in an 1880s-era frame house fills a 15-yard about right, with a little room to spare. On these older homes you are often dealing with lath-and-plaster behind the cabinets rather than modern drywall, which the 15 absorbs comfortably.
- **Roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these Victorians, Foursquares, or bungalows fits well, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most modest roofs. Roof and siding tear-offs are common on the older frame stock here, and shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. In a house this old, that often means original fixtures and plaster coming out together.
- **Basement and whole-house cleanouts.** Clearing out a century-old home — whether you are renovating, settling an estate, or emptying a longtime family house before a sale — works well in the 15, since the low walls make loading easier and the short body fits a tight lot. Full cleanouts of aging houses are a recurring job in this district.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house gut down to the studs, or a multi-room renovation running the length of the house — that may be a 20 or even a 30 instead, lot permitting. We will tell you straight which size your job needs, and we will be honest about what your particular Old Town lot can hold, rather than upsell you into a container you cannot place or fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement takes more thought here than in newer Bowie.** This is the original platted core of the city — small town lots, narrow tree-named streets, and driveways that are often short or absent on the oldest homes. The largest concentration of historic buildings runs along 9th Street, near the 1939 firehouse, where curbside room is genuinely limited compared with Bowie's wide suburban subdivisions. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door. On many Old Town lots that fits a driveway just fine; on the tightest ones, we will look at your specific address and figure out the cleanest staging spot before delivery day rather than guessing. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect older surfaces.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it can bite here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. In most of Bowie that permit is the exception, because nearly every home has its own driveway — but Old Town's small lots and short or missing driveways mean street staging is more likely here than in the Levitt sections. If the only practical spot is the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out when the truck arrives.

**A note on the historic district.** Old Town Bowie sits within a Development District Overlay Zone administered through Prince George's County and M-NCPPC, which applies tailored design and review standards to this historic district. That overlay governs the renovation work itself, not the dumpster — but if your project involves altering a structure here, it may fall under added review beyond standard Bowie zoning, so it is worth confirming your permits are in order before the demo starts. With the city-backed revitalization of Old Town now moving forward, bringing galleries, a brewery, and a performing-arts venue, we expect more tenant build-out and cleanout work in the district, and we are glad to talk through staging for those jobs too.
