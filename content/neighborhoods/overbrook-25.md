Locals know it by its street names: following Levitt's alliterative naming habit, every street in the section starts with the letter O — Overbrook Lane and the rest — which is exactly why it gets called the "O section." ## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: in Overbrook a 25-yard **starts at $640 for construction debris (other materials priced separately)**, and the price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton allowance. Extra weight beyond that runs $105 per additional ton, and you can keep the box longer for $5 per extra day. Pickup is on request, so just call to schedule the haul.

The step up from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a second haul partway through — a whole-house remodel, a substantial addition, a large roof, or a full commercial cleanout. The walls are still five feet, so a crew can walk debris up and pitch it over the side without a ramp, but there is meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size boxes give you.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Twenty feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint — and on Overbrook's narrow residential streets, that footprint matters. The good news is that almost every job here stages on the home's own driveway rather than the roadway, which keeps the long box off the tight pavement entirely. Still, it is worth confirming the spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Overbrook jobs suit the 25-yard

Overbrook's housing stock is the story here. This is a small, mature section — roughly seven blocks of 1960s Levitt single-family homes: Cape Cods, split-levels, and brick ranch-style houses set along narrow streets under moderate tree cover, on the kind of established lots typical of an older Levitt build. After 55 to 60 years, original-owner homes here are reaching the age that drives the biggest projects, and those are exactly the jobs the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out a brick rancher or building onto a split-level throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a smaller box can take without a mid-project swap.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full gut of an original Levitt home — kitchen, baths, flooring, and drywall all coming out at once — is a textbook 25-yard job. When a mid-1960s house gets modernized down to the studs, the debris stacks up fast, and one large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** Six decades in, original roofs reach the end of the line. Bigger or multi-layer tear-offs run heavy, so plan on the 2-ton allowance plus $105 per additional ton for the extra shingle weight.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** When a larger property or a business clearout near the section throws off material in bulk, the 25-yard handles it in one go.

If your Overbrook project is on the smaller side — a single-room redo, a bathroom refresh, a basement or garage cleanout, an estate clearout of a parent's original-owner home — a 20-yard or even a 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely big.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement, and Overbrook all but requires it.** The streets in this section are narrow, and they were never meant to host a 20-foot container parked at the curb. That is fine, because these are detached single-family Levitt homes with their own driveways — so the on-site driveway is almost always the right place to stage the box anyway. Putting the container on the driveway keeps the roadway clear for neighbors and keeps the long footprint off the tight pavement. We will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the driveway surface, which matters on mature lots where the drive may be as old as the house.

**The permit rule is in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster has to sit in a city street. A container placed entirely on your private driveway does **not** need one. Since nearly every Overbrook home has its own driveway and that is where the box belongs anyway, the vast majority of jobs here stage on-property with no permit at all. If your particular address somehow forces the container into the street, we will flag the permit requirement up front rather than let it surprise you on delivery day. The same goes for any HOA-approved staging — that does not require a city permit either.

**Confirming the address is easy here.** One quiet upside of the O section's naming convention: every street starts with O, so there is rarely any confusion about where the truck is headed. When you give us an Overbrook Lane address, we know the block, the kind of lot to expect, and how to line up the drop.

**Drive time.** Overbrook sits in the northeast part of Bowie, with easy access to U.S. Route 50 and close to Samuel Ogle Park and the WB&A Trail. From our Beltsville yard, that is about a 25-minute run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, and same-day delivery is common — so when you are running a build on a deadline, the container shows up when the crew needs it.
