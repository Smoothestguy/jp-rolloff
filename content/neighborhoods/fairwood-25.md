## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2.5-ton (5,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Fairwood a typical 25-yard rental runs **$525 to $625, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage**. No tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the haul.

The jump from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a mid-job swap — a whole-house remodel, a substantial addition, a large roof, or a full commercial clearout. The walls are still five feet, so a crew can walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but there is meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size containers offer.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Twenty feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. That matters more in Fairwood than in older Bowie — more on that below — so it is always worth confirming the spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Fairwood jobs suit the 25-yard

Fairwood's housing stock is the story here. The single-family homes are contemporary and Colonial Revival in style, commonly red brick and vinyl, typically on about quarter-acre lots, and nearly every one was delivered with a full basement — walk-out or standard — plus a gourmet kitchen and a deck. Houses this size, only fifteen to twenty years old, do not get gutted out of necessity; they get expanded and upgraded. That is what drives demand for a container this big. These are the jobs the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out the back of a quarter-acre lot or adding a room throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a smaller box can take without a second haul partway through.
- **Whole-house remodels.** When an owner modernizes a mid-2000s home top to bottom — kitchen, baths, flooring, and the full basement all at once — the debris adds up fast. Those full walk-out basements are a real driver here: finishing or re-finishing one means drywall, old carpet, framing, and flooring leaving the house in bulk, and a single large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** A bigger roof on a substantial Fairwood Colonial runs heavy, and the 2.5-ton allowance gives more headroom for shingle weight than the smaller sizes.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** When a larger property or business clearout in the area throws off material in bulk, the 25-yard handles it in one go.

If your Fairwood project is on the smaller side — a single-room redo, a bathroom refresh, a routine garage or basement declutter — a 20-yard or even a 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely big.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is the thing to plan in Fairwood.** This neighborhood was laid out with sidewalk-lined streets and a lot of cul-de-sac, wooded, and corner homesites, with about eight miles of trailways winding through dense forest. That tree-lined, cul-de-sac character is beautiful, but it means tighter street frontage and driveways framed by trees — which is exactly why a 20-foot container's footprint deserves a second look. On a single-family quarter-acre lot, the driveway is usually the right call, and we will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface. Fairwood also carries substantial townhome stock — the Retreat at Fairwood and Fairwood Sanctuary run roughly 2,100 to 3,400-plus square feet — and those homes have short, shared, or single-width driveways. If you are in a townhome, the staging plan often has to fit one driveway width or an HOA-approved common spot rather than a wide suburban pad, so call us before you book and we will sort the placement together.

**The real "permit" here is HOA approval, not a city street permit.** Fairwood is governed by the Fairwood Community Association, with a professional management company and an on-site general manager, and sub-communities like the Retreat and the Sanctuary have their own HOAs on top of that. For a dumpster, the relevant gatekeeper is HOA architectural and staging approval — not a City of Bowie permit. The city only requires a right-of-way permit when a container sits in a city street; a dumpster placed entirely on your private driveway or an HOA-approved staging area does **not** need one. In practice, that means most Fairwood jobs come down to a quick check with your association on placement rather than a city filing. If your particular address forces the container into the street, we will flag the city permit requirement up front rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.
