The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material: the 30-yard starts at $665 for construction debris (other materials priced separately), and that price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton allowance. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule it. Keep the rental past 14 days and it is +$5/day, and any weight over the 3-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, flooring, trim, roofing tear-off, and general construction debris. ## Gate access is the real question in Woodmore

Here is the one piece worth thinking through carefully before you book a 30-yard in Woodmore, because this neighborhood is genuinely different from Bowie's open detached-home subdivisions. In most Bowie neighborhoods, a hauler can simply drop a dumpster in the driveway. Woodmore's flagship section is a guardhouse-controlled gated community, and the defining logistics issue here is not a street permit — it is getting the delivery truck through the gate.

The only road through the subdivision is Pleasant Prospect Road, a horseshoe-shaped county road that branches off Woodmore Road, with electronic gates on both ends and a gatehouse on the western end. Residents use key cards. Non-residents — contractors and delivery trucks included — generally must be pre-cleared at the gatehouse or escorted by the resident, stating their purpose to an Association agent before they can reach the home. That means a roll-off truck hauling a 22-foot container cannot just roll up unannounced; access has to be arranged in advance. We will coordinate a delivery window with you so that either you, the homeowner, or the Association has cleared us at the gate before the truck leaves Beltsville. Pleasant Prospect Road is legally a public county road despite the gates, but in practice the gate clearance is the single biggest wrinkle for any vendor working in here, so it is the first thing we nail down.

The good news is that placement itself is usually the easy part. Because these are one-acre estate lots with wide driveways, a 30-yard can almost always be staged on-site — on the driveway or the yard — rather than in the street. That matters for two reasons. First, the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street; driveways and HOA-approved staging do not trigger that permit at all, so most Woodmore drops need no city permit. Second, staging on the driveway keeps the container off the road and out of the way of the gated community's traffic.

If your home backs to the golf course, there is one more thing to plan around. The course sits inside the gated community with 42 acres of lakes and cart paths threading the property, so on golf-adjacent lots we keep the container well clear of course frontage and cart paths and place it on the driveway side instead. Tell us your exact address and which Woodmore sub-development governs it — Woodmore North, Estates, Highlands, Meadows, or South each has its own HOA — and we will confirm any placement or staging rules before we schedule. A little planning here goes a long way, because the difference between a clean first-time drop and a wasted trip is almost always whether the gate and the staging spot were sorted in advance.
