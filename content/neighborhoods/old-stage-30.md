## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about fourteen pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material — the 30-yard is priced by what you're throwing away, so call for a quote, with other materials priced separately (note that aggregates are only available in the 10, 15, and 20-yard sizes). Every rental includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance; additional days are +$5/day, and any weight beyond the allowance is $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request, so call to schedule it when you're ready.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, flooring, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why an Old Stage job reaches this size

Old Stage is not one of Bowie's 1960s Levitt tracts. It is a newer, larger-home enclave — predominantly single-family houses built from the 1980s through the 2000s, with prominent split-level and Colonial Revival styles, most with attached garages. These are bigger homes on bigger lots than the smaller midcentury houses that dominate much of Bowie, and they price in the roughly $500,000 to $650,000 range depending on square footage and lot size. The Old Stage Road corridor itself includes large, sometimes multi-acre residential parcels, with one listing citing a 2.11-acre lot and others noting lots near 100 by 125 feet.

That housing profile is exactly why projects here tend to reach the largest size we run. A split-level or Colonial that has been lived in for two to four decades is now hitting the point where renovations stop being cosmetic and start being structural — and that is where the 30-yard earns its keep:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting a 1980s or 1990s split-level down to the studs throws off far more debris than most owners expect — drywall, dated cabinetry, flooring, trim, and built-ins come out in a steady stream. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs, additions, and whole-house remodels on these larger Colonials throw off a continuous run of fixtures, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** On a home held by one family since it was built, estate and downsizing cleanouts pile up fast — furniture, appliances, garage contents, and a finished basement all at once. Fourteen truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** When several units or properties turn over together — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and accumulated debris in one push — the 30-yard is built for precisely that volume.

For a single-room remodel, a bathroom refresh, or a routine garage cleanout in Old Stage, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Placement is the easy part in Old Stage — with one phone call

Here is the good news for an Old Stage drop, and it is genuinely different from Bowie's denser, tighter subdivisions. The roomy driveways and generous frontage on these 1980s-to-2000s lots mean a 30-yard can almost always sit on your own property rather than out in the public street. That matters for the longest box we run, because it needs a clear, straight run-up to set down — and Old Stage's wider lots and longer driveways give us exactly that approach. On most properties here we can place the container on the driveway without it ever touching a city street.

That on-property advantage simplifies the permit question. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street; the city's Public Works Department handles engineering review and permitting for any work in a public right-of-way, but driveway and approved staging placements do not trigger it. So when your 30-yard sits on your own driveway — which is the norm in Old Stage — you typically need no city permit at all.

The one gatekeeper worth checking is the homeowners association. Old Stage is governed by the Olde Stage Homeowners Association, which sets the community's bylaws and handles common-area maintenance. If your container will sit entirely on your driveway, that is usually straightforward; if the only workable spot is a common area or somewhere off your own frontage, HOA approval is the thing to confirm before the truck rolls — not a city street permit. A quick check with the association up front keeps a big demo or cleanout on schedule, and we are glad to time the drop around your approval.

A little planning still goes a long way. Old Stage branches off Old Stage Road at the western edge of Bowie, within minutes of Bowie Town Center, and the approach runs through residential streets rather than wide arterials. Telling us your exact address, where on the driveway you want the box, and whether the lot is one of the larger multi-acre parcels along the corridor helps us route the truck and trailer cleanly and set the 30-yard down right the first time.
