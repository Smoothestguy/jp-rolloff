## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about fourteen pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material, so a 30-yard is priced by what you're loading — call for a quote. That price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance — extra days run $5 per day, and any weight beyond the allowance is $105 per additional ton.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why Heather Hills jobs reach this size

Heather Hills dates from 1965 and was among the later sections completed when Levitt and Sons built out Belair at Bowie — the firm's fourth and final mega-community, opened for home sales in October 1960 and totaling roughly 7,500 houses. Like the rest of the Levitt portions of Belair, Heather Hills is single-family detached homes on roughly quarter-acre lots along tree-lined streets, built from the original model lineup: the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the Colonial, the Country Clubber, and the Manor House. Those homes are now around 60 years old, and that age is what drives the work the 30-yard was made for:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting a 1965 Levitt home down to the studs — pulling the original kitchen, taking out interior walls, clearing a finished basement, or removing a roof's worth of tear-off — produces far more debris than most owners expect. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs and tenant turnovers throw off a continuous stream of fixtures, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** Whole-house clear-outs, estate and downsizing cleanouts, and decades of accumulated material in a long-held family home pile up fast. Fourteen truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** Turning over several units at once — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and tenant debris together — is exactly the volume the 30-yard was built to handle.

For a single-room remodel or a routine garage cleanout in Heather Hills, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Getting a big box into a section across the bridge

Heather Hills has a wrinkle that no other Belair section shares, and it matters for a container this size. It is the only part of the Levitt community sitting south of US 50 — physically cut off from the rest of Belair, with no direct road connection across the highway. The pedestrian bridge over US 50 carries foot and bike traffic to the rest of Bowie, but it carries nothing on wheels. There is a reason the section got its own elementary school: you simply cannot drive into it from the main Belair road network.

The practical upshot for a 30-yard is that we route the truck and trailer in through Heather Hills's own local street access, not staged off the larger Belair grid the way a job north of US 50 would be. That is a routing detail we handle every time we dispatch down here, and it is exactly why telling us your specific street matters. Following Levitt's Belair convention, nearly every street in the section starts with the letter H, so the street name is the clearest signal of where you are. Give us the address when you book and we will plan the approach cleanly the first time.

## Placement on Heather Hills's quarter-acre lots

A 30-yard is a big box on a long frame, and the driver needs a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes to back the trailer in straight. The good news is that Heather Hills was laid out as quarter-acre single-family lots with real driveways, so the realistic plan is almost always to stage the container on your own driveway or lot rather than tying up the narrow, tightly gridded H-streets. On a quarter-acre Levitt lot that usually works fine — but if the approach is tight, sloped, or shares a curb cut with a neighbor, mention it when you book and we will plan the drop around it.

Placement also decides whether a permit even enters the picture. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Heather Hills's own driveways and lots give us room to stage off the street, a street permit ends up being the exception rather than the rule here. There is a Heather Hills Homeowners Association presence in the neighborhood, so if your spot involves any shared or association-controlled ground, a quick word with them ahead of the drop keeps everything clean. And if the public street genuinely is your only workable spot, the city's Public Works Department handles that right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.
