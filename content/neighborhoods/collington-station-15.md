## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 15-yard is **priced by what you're throwing away, so call for a quote** (aggregates are available only in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard sizes). That price includes delivery, pickup, and a **14-day rental** with a **1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance**; extra days run $5/day and any weight over the allowance is **$105 per additional ton**. Pickup is on request — give us a call to schedule it.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a full basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job from start to end. That is why the 15 is best known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

## Which Collington Station jobs suit the 15-yard

Collington Station's homes are predominantly mid-1990s Colonial Revivals, built as three-level colonials with attached garages and brick siding as the dominant features. That construction profile shapes the debris in two specific ways: nearly every home has a usable paved driveway for the container, and nearly every home has a full basement that drives heavy cleanout and finishing work. Three decades on, those systems are coming due across the whole subdivision at once — and that one-room, one-system pace is exactly what the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original 1990s kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is one of the most common reasons a 15 lands on a Collington Station driveway.
- **Roofing tear-off.** These three-level colonials are hitting the age where the original roof needs replacing. A single-layer asphalt tear-off fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most of these roof footprints. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 30-year-old build, that often means dated fixtures and original wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** The full basements under these colonials are what set Collington Station apart from Bowie's slab-built sections. Clearing out decades of accumulation — or the debris from finishing or re-finishing one of those lower levels — works well in the 15, since the low walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles a turnover cleanout when a long-held home changes hands.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house interior gut between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of the house — that may be a 20 or larger instead. This is an upper-middle-income, well-maintained neighborhood where larger remodels and full cleanouts are common, so we see plenty of jobs that step up a size. We will tell you straight which container your job needs rather than upsell you into one you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and the Collington Station HOA

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here.** Because these homes were built as three-level colonials with attached garages, the paved driveway is a given on nearly every lot — and for a one-room reno that is exactly what you want. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Collington Station driveways take that comfortably. Because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size that keeps your project entirely on your own property rather than spilling into the lane out front. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Collington Station home has its own attached-garage driveway, a city street permit is the exception rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops here never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Clear it with the association first.** Collington Station is governed by an active, professionally managed Collington Station Homeowners' Association (listed on Jordon Pond Lane), and the community maintains common amenities including a clubhouse, pool, tennis courts, playground, and picnic areas. Driveway placement on your own lot is straightforward, but if a container needs to stage anywhere on common ground, that should be cleared with the HOA first. We can provide a service confirmation to forward to the association if they ask for one.

**Drive time and the lane-style streets.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Collington Station is laid out as a suburban subdivision of lane-style residential streets — water and pond-themed names like Jordon Pond Lane and Johnstone Lane — rather than through-roads, so giving our driver the exact lane and house number keeps the delivery clean. The neighborhood sits at the south end of Bowie, bordered to the south by the Oak Creek Golf Club across a dense tree line, and it is well within easy reach of our yard. Most jobs go out within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run.
