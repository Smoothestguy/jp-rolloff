## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to when a job throws off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material: the 30-yard starts at $665 for construction debris (other materials priced separately), and that price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance. Extra days run $5 per day, and any weight over the 3-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton.

One honest note on weight before you book. The 30-yard's volume fills up best with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, cabinetry, roof tear-off, old furniture, and general construction debris. If your load runs heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly while the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. Tell us what is going in when you call and we will steer you to the right size rather than just the biggest one.

## Why Tulip Grove fits the big box

Tulip Grove is one of Levitt & Sons' "T-sections" of the Belair at Bowie development, built in the early-to-mid 1960s as the company pushed the community eastward. Locals call it the T-section for a reason: every street name here starts with the letter T — Tulip Grove Drive, Trainor Lane, Tallow Lane, Tarragon Lane — a Levitt alphabetized-layout quirk that sets it apart from Bowie's B, K and F sections. Two things about Tulip Grove make it especially friendly to a 30-yard. Homes that age are frequently due for major renovation, and the heavy end of that work is squarely 30-yard territory. Second, Tulip Grove and neighboring Long Ridge were built with slightly larger homes and lots than Levitt's earlier Bowie sections — generally about a fifth of an acre, with recent Tulip Grove Drive listings showing lots in the roughly 9,100 to 10,600 square-foot range and bigger corner lots beyond that. That extra side- and rear-yard space means even our largest box usually has somewhere to sit on the homeowner's own property.

## Placement and permits in Tulip Grove

This is where Tulip Grove's larger lots really pay off. Because the homes here are detached single-family Levitts on lots that run a fifth of an acre and up, with private driveways and room to spare along the sides and rear, we can almost always stage the 30-yard entirely on the homeowner's own driveway or an HOA-approved spot. That keeps the box off the street, and it keeps the paperwork simple: **the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not.** In Tulip Grove, a street permit is the exception rather than the rule, which sets it apart from the tighter, denser parts of Bowie.

The one thing to plan for is the box itself. The 30-yard is a big container on a long frame, and it needs a longer, clearer run-up to place than the smaller sizes. Tulip Grove's roomier lots and driveways usually give us that room, but the approach is still the thing to think about before we roll out — a flat, open drive with space to back the trailer in straight is ideal. Some Levitt sections fall under civic or homeowners associations, so it is worth confirming any HOA-approved placement rules alongside the city's street-permit rule. When you book, tell us whether your drive is tight, short, or sloped, and we will plan the drop accordingly. If the only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's Public Works Department handles right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through it before the container shows up.
