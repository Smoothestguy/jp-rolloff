Belair Town is one of the few dense, shared-wall enclaves inside Levitt and Sons' Belair at Bowie — the firm's fourth and final postwar planned community. While most of Belair filled in with detached ramblers and split-levels, Belair Town went up as roughly 300 townhouse units, built in two phases between 1968 and 1970 and flanking the stores on the north side of Annapolis Road (MD 450). Those townhomes are over 50 years old now, and that is exactly why the 20-yard roll-off is the size most projects here end up needing. It is the dumpster that handles a real renovation or a full cleanout without overwhelming the tight frontages and single assigned parking spaces that define this neighborhood.

We are JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler dispatched out of Beltsville, about 25 minutes from Belair Town by way of Route 197 or the BW Parkway. When you call, you reach the family that owns the trucks — not a call center, not a dispatcher reading off a script.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: construction debris in a 20-yard **starts at $620** (other materials are priced separately, and aggregates are accepted only in the 10, 15, and 20-yard sizes). That price **includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance**, with pickup on request — just call to schedule. Extra time runs $5 per day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton.

Those five feet of wall height matter more than people expect. The walls are low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that really only makes sense for ground-up construction or full structural demo. Around here it earns its nickname — the "whole-house cleanout" size — and it is the size most customers end up choosing and rarely regret.

## Why the 20-yard fits Belair Town specifically

Belair Town's townhomes are modest by square footage — roughly 918 to 1,538 square feet with two or three bedrooms — but they are now more than 50 years old, and that late-1960s Levitt-era construction is squarely in the window where original kitchens, baths, basements, and roofs all come due at once. When an owner finally takes one of these units down to the studs, or a family clears out a longtime home for turnover, the debris adds up fast even in a smaller footprint. A single-bath swap might only need a 10-yard, but the projects people actually run in Belair Town tend to be bigger than that.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again on these blocks:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's signature role. Clearing an original Belair Town townhome between owners, or after decades of accumulation, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use. Furniture, boxes, old carpet, and household debris all go in one container.
- **Multi-room remodels.** Modernizing a 50-plus-year-old townhome usually means hitting the kitchen and a bath or two in one push. The drywall, tile, cabinetry, and trim debris from a multi-room gut on a 1,000-to-1,500-square-foot unit is exactly the volume a 20 was built to hold.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Belair Town has held many of the same families since the units were new around 1969 and 1970. When one of those longtime homes changes hands, the 20-yard handles a full clear-out in a single drop.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on a townhome roof fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most jobs of that footprint. Because the units share walls, we will talk through staging so the container serves your section cleanly.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a closet purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better and save you a little. If you are combining a gut renovation with a basement clear-out and a roof, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight. We would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the parking reality

**Placement is the deciding factor in Belair Town, more than anywhere else in Bowie.** This is a dense, shared-wall layout where, under the Belair Town II parking regulations, each unit is entitled to the exclusive use of just one assigned parking space, with anything beyond that limited to street parking or rear-of-unit spaces. There is no roomy two-car driveway to drop a box on the way there is in Bowie's detached-home subdivisions. A 20-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door, so before we roll, we want to know exactly where you picture it sitting. Tell us when you call and we will confirm the spot works rather than show up and improvise.

**HOA coordination usually comes first.** Belair Town II was established in 1969 and is governed by a Declaration of Covenants, By-Laws, and Parking Regulations administered by its homeowners association (reachable at belairtownii@gmail.com), so container and equipment placement on or near the common areas should be cleared with the HOA. In practice, securing an HOA-approved staging spot is the single most important step for a Belair Town drop. Because the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved private staging do not — an approved spot off the street usually means no city permit at all. If the only workable location turns out to be the street itself, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address. We are also happy to provide a service confirmation you can forward to the association.

**Getting the truck in.** Belair Town's primary access is off Annapolis Road (Route 450) by way of Race Track Road, with the Belair Town II entrance sitting near that intersection. Interior streets like Morningside Lane — the HOA mailing address sits at 3620 Morningside Lane — are residential and tight, not wide arterials, so we route the truck in carefully and set the box exactly where it needs to go. The neighborhood is in ZIP 20715.

**One thing you will not need the dumpster for.** Belair Town II receives municipal trash, recycling, and yard-waste pickup, so routine household garbage is already handled at the curb. A rented roll-off here is for the larger stuff — renovation debris, bulk cleanouts, and construction loads that curbside service will not take. That is precisely where the 20-yard earns its keep.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning. Most Belair Town addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run.
