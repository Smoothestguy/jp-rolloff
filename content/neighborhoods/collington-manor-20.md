## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 20-yard for construction debris **starts at $620** (other materials are priced separately, and aggregates like concrete or dirt are only accepted in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard sizes). That price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a **14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance**; additional days run $5/day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

Those five feet of wall height are the part people tend to underestimate. The wall is low enough to walk debris up and pitch it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes here: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demolition. ## Placement, permits, and the Collington Manor HOA

**The large lots here are a real advantage.** Unlike the denser, Levitt-era tracts elsewhere in Bowie, Collington Manor's detached homes tend toward long private driveways and ample on-property room. A 20-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most of these driveways take that with room to spare — which means the container sits on your own property rather than spilling into the lane out front. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**Townhome staging is the tighter case.** Collington Manor also has three- and four-bedroom townhomes, and those come with shorter, shared, or single-width drives. On a townhome, a 20-yard often has to fit a single driveway lane or an HOA-approved common spot rather than a wide suburban pad. If you are in one of the row houses and tackling a multi-room job, tell us up front and we will plan the drop accordingly.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because most Collington Manor detached homes have long private driveways, a city street permit is the exception rather than the rule — most 20-yard drops here never touch the right-of-way at all. Townhome owners with shorter shared drives are the ones more likely to need either street placement (which means the permit) or HOA sign-off for common-area staging. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Clear it with the association first.** Collington Manor has a real, registered homeowners association — Collington Manor Homeowners Association, Inc. — so exterior projects and dumpster staging here can fall under HOA architectural or approval rules on top of the city rule. That is the variable that actually matters in this section: not tight street access, but association sign-off. Driveway placement on your own lot is usually straightforward, but if a container needs to stage on shared or common ground, that should be cleared with the HOA first. The City of Bowie publishes an official Homeowners & Civic Associations listing if you need to confirm your association's contact, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along if they ask for one.
