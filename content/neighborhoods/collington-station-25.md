## Staging a 25-yard on your driveway — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's where Collington Station's layout works in your favor, even for a container this large. Because these are detached homes with attached-garage paved driveways, most 25-yard drops here sit entirely on the homeowner's own driveway — which means you generally do **not** need a City of Bowie right-of-way permit. The city only requires that permit when a dumpster sits in a city street. A driveway drop doesn't trigger it. The thing to watch with the 25-yard specifically is room: it's 20 feet long, so you want a deep, clear run of driveway and enough overhead and side clearance for our truck to set it down and pick it back up. Clear both cars off the drive the night before and you're usually set.

One placement note that's specific to this community. Collington Station is governed by an active, professionally managed homeowners' association, listed on Jordon Pond Lane, that maintains common amenities like the clubhouse, pool, tennis courts and playground. A driveway drop is your own property and is straightforward. But a 25-yard is a big, visible box, and if your only workable spot pushes onto a common area or shared space, clear the placement with the HOA first — that's worth a quick call before a major build, not after. And if the street genuinely is the only option for a container this size, say so when you call and we'll walk you through the right-of-way permit so you're covered before the truck rolls.
