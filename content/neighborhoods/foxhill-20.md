Foxhill is the "F section" of Belair at Bowie, and the street signs tell you so the moment you turn in — every street here begins with the letter F, from Foxhill Lane on out, which is exactly how locals pin down where they live inside the section. It is one of the earliest neighborhoods Levitt and Sons built in the whole Belair development, with its houses going up in the early 1960s and the section finished out by about 1964. That makes these homes 60-plus years old now, and that single fact drives most of the dumpster work we do here. Foxhill is a neighborhood of original Levitt houses coming due for their second or third real renovation — and the 20-yard roll-off is the size most of those projects land on.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 20-yard **starts at $620 for construction debris (other materials priced separately)**, and aggregates are accepted only in the 10, 15, and 20-yard sizes. That price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance built in. Extra days run $5/day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton.

Those five feet of wall height are the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes in the F section: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demolition. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret.

## Why the 20-yard fits Foxhill specifically

Foxhill's homes were drawn from the handful of single-family detached models Levitt opened Belair with in October 1960 — the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the Colonial, the Country Clubber, and the Manor House. Those models were built assembly-line style on concrete slabs, finishing six to ten houses a day, and that construction detail shapes the work we see here more than anything else. Because so many of these homes sit on slab-on-grade rather than full basements, Foxhill renovations skew toward interior gut-and-remodel work and roof or siding tear-offs — not the basement digs and below-grade haul-outs you get in neighborhoods built with deep foundations. That is squarely 20-yard territory: a substantial, single-house project with debris measured in rooms and roofs rather than excavated dirt.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again on the F-named streets:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's best-known role — it is even nicknamed the "whole-house cleanout" size. Clearing an original Foxhill Levitt home between owners, or after decades of one family living in it, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-baths gut, or a run of bedrooms getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job in a 60-year-old Levitt home on its second or third renovation. Pulling an original Levitt kitchen and a bath or two generates exactly the debris volume a 20 was built for.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Foxhill has held many of its original-owner families for decades. When one of those longtime homes finally changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical Belair-era roof tear-off fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most single-layer asphalt jobs on these modest-footprint homes.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are taking the house to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the Foxhill advantage

**Driveway placement.** This is where Foxhill has a built-in advantage over Bowie's newer subdivisions. Belair laid the F section out on fairly uniform lots — most around 8,400 square feet, roughly a fifth of an acre — and gave the homes private driveways. The yards themselves are modest, so the driveway is almost always the right staging spot, and a 20-yard fits most of these driveways with room to spare. It needs roughly 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Foxhill home has its own private driveway on those standardized lots, a city street permit is the exception here, not the rule — most F-section projects stage entirely on private property with no permit at all. If the only practical place turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Drive time and getting around.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway, so we know exactly where these streets are. Foxhill anchors a recognizable corner of Bowie — Foxhill Park, the 45-acre M-NCPPC park at 5001 Collington Road (you may know it as Woodward Pond or Foxhill Lake), sits right alongside the historic Belair Mansion, and that landmark is how a lot of folks orient the section. Foxhill is in ZIP 20715, and most addresses here are scheduled inside 24 hours, with an early call often making the same-day run.
