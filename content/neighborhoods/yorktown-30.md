## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags at a time. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and our typical pricing runs $595 to $725 — a number that already includes delivery, pickup, and the included tonnage, so there is no surprise line item waiting at the end.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard fills its volume fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why Yorktown jobs reach this size

Yorktown is one of the later Levitt sections of Belair at Bowie, built among the development's final stages — roughly 1964 through the late 1960s — as part of the last detached single-family homes Levitt and Sons completed in Bowie. Those houses are now 55 to 60 years old, mostly medium-to-large three-to-five-bedroom homes running somewhere between about 1,500 and 2,800 square feet of living area, and they were sold in the familiar Levitt models — the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the Colonial, the Manor House, and the largest of them, the Country Clubber. After more than half a century, those original builds are reaching the age where the work gets ambitious, and that is exactly the work the 30-yard is made for:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting one of these Levitt homes down to the studs — pulling an original kitchen, taking out interior walls, clearing a finished basement, or removing a long-ago addition — throws off far more debris than most owners expect. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs and turnovers along the Race Track Road collector and the surrounding commercial edges produce a continuous stream of fixtures, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** Roof tear-offs and whole-house renovations on a 55-to-60-year-old home pile up fast — old shingles, sheathing, siding, and decades of accumulated material together. Nine truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** When several units turn over at the same time — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and tenant debris all at once — that is precisely the volume the 30-yard was built to absorb.

Yorktown is overwhelmingly owner-occupied, and a lot of these projects are the big once-in-a-generation kind: a full renovation of a long-held family home, a major addition, a complete roof tear-off, or an estate and downsizing cleanout when a house changes hands. For a single-room remodel or a routine garage clear-out, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard earns its keep when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Placement on Yorktown's driveways

Here is where Yorktown is genuinely easy compared with a lot of the DMV. The section is almost entirely detached single-family housing — close to 99.8% free-standing homes — which means nearly every property has its own private driveway. Unlike Bowie's newer townhome and HOA communities, Yorktown is original 1960s Levitt stock with no homeowners association, so staging a dumpster on your own driveway is governed by you and the city rather than by an architectural-control board waiting to sign off. The practical upshot for a 30-yard: we can almost always set the box on your driveway rather than out in the street.

That matters because of how the city handles permits. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Yorktown is a no-HOA Levitt section, a driveway drop typically needs neither an association approval nor a city street permit, and a permit ends up being the exception rather than the rule. If your only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's Public Works Department handles that right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.

A 30-yard does ask for a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes — it is a big box on a long frame, and the driver needs room to back the trailer in straight. On Yorktown's single-family lots that usually works fine, but it is worth a look before we arrive. And because every street in the section starts with the letter Y — Yorktown Drive, Yarmouth Lane, Yellowstone Place, Youngwood Turn — telling us your exact street is the cleanest way to route the truck and trailer right the first time. If the approach is tight, sloped, or shares a curb cut with a neighbor, mention it when you book and we will plan the drop around it.
