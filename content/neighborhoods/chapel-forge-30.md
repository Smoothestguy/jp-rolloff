## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about fourteen pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material: construction debris in the 30-yard is priced by material (other materials are priced separately), so call for a quote, and that rate includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance. After 14 days it is just $5 per extra day, and any weight over the 3-ton allowance runs $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request, so call to schedule the haul when you are ready.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Placement on Chapel Forge's mid-century lots

Here is the one piece worth thinking through before you book a 30-yard in Chapel Forge. These homes sit on modest mid-century lots — roughly 9,600 to 10,664 square feet, about 0.22 to 0.28 acre — with real but limited driveway and front-yard staging room. The 30-yard is the longest box we run, and it asks for a clear, straight run-up so the driver can back the trailer in without clipping a neighbor's curb cut or a mature tree. On most Chapel Forge driveways that works fine, but the tighter lots are exactly where getting the placement right matters most, because there is not a lot of spare room to correct on the fly.

That placement decision ties directly to the permit question. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Chapel Forge is one of the original Levitt sections, organized around a civic association rather than the deed-restricted, dues-collecting HOAs found in newer Bowie subdivisions, and there is no evidence it is gated. So in practice, if we can fit the 30-yard on your own driveway, you almost certainly need no permit and no association sign-off at all. If your only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's right-of-way review applies, and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.
