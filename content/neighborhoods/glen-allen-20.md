Glen Allen is not one of Bowie's original Levitt neighborhoods, and that distinction shapes nearly every dumpster we drop here. It is a roughly 400-home subdivision built in phases over a decade starting in the 1990s, largely by Drees Homes, in the "new Bowie" area along Mitchellville Road near U.S. 301. That means the housing stock is mature but only about 25 to 35 years old — bigger brick colonials and Craftsman-style homes that are now hitting their first or second real wave of major renovation rather than the full gut rehabs you see on the 60-year-old ramblers north of Route 50. For those mid-to-large remodels and cleanouts, the 20-yard roll-off is the size most Glen Allen homeowners land on, and rarely regret.

We are JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler dispatched out of Beltsville, about 25 minutes from Glen Allen by way of Route 197 or the BW Parkway. When you call, you get the family that owns the trucks — not a call center.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 20-yard **starts at $620 for construction debris (other materials priced separately)**, and that includes delivery, pickup, and a **14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance**. Keep it longer for $5 a day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance runs $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule when you are done.

Those five feet of wall height are the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and pitch it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes here: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demolition. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and it is even nicknamed the "whole-house cleanout" size for good reason.

## Why the 20-yard fits Glen Allen specifically

Glen Allen's homes are larger move-up houses — the new sections of Bowie offered bigger homes than the original Levitt developments, and the median single-family price here sits around $657,000, well above the Prince George's County median. Bigger houses mean bigger projects, and renovation debris volumes here tend to run toward 20-yard work rather than the small cosmetic jobs that fit a 10. At the same time, these homes are only 25 to 35 years old, so the typical job is a remodel or a refresh — not a top-to-bottom gut that would push you into a 30. That is exactly the band the 20-yard was built for.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again in Glen Allen:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's best-known role. Clearing one of these larger Drees colonials between owners, or after years of accumulation in a four- or five-bedroom home, fills a 20 about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen plus a bath or two, or an open-concept reconfiguration across a main floor, generates drywall, cabinetry, flooring, and trim by the truckload. On a move-up Glen Allen home, that is the classic 20-yard load.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When a longtime Glen Allen home changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container rather than stringing out multiple smaller hauls.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these homes fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most jobs of that footprint.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are taking the house to the studs or building a large addition, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and getting the truck in

**Driveway placement is the practical default here.** Glen Allen is laid out with numerous quiet cul-de-sacs, which means many homes sit on pipestem or court-end lots with curved frontage and limited street curb space. That makes the private driveway the natural home for a roll-off rather than the street. The good news is that these are larger detached homes with generous driveways — a 20-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most of the two-car driveways here take it with room to spare. When you call, tell us where you picture it sitting and we will confirm it fits before we roll. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved private staging do not. Because Glen Allen's homes have wide private driveways on detached single-family lots, the vast majority of placements here stage entirely on private property and avoid the permit altogether. If the only workable spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, which handles work in public rights-of-way, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**The HOA.** Unlike the original Levitt sections, Glen Allen is a deeded subdivision with its own homeowners association — listed among the City of Bowie's registered HOAs and reportedly managed by ProCom Management. It is a standard suburban, non-gated neighborhood with public street access, but projects that involve exterior staging or shared areas may be subject to HOA placement or screening rules. It is worth a quick check with your association before a longer project, and we are happy to provide a service confirmation you can forward along if they ask for one.

**Getting the truck in.** Glen Allen sits along Mitchellville Road near U.S. 301, about a mile south of Bowie Town Center, with U.S. Route 50 linking the area back toward our Beltsville yard — the standard haul corridor for us. The neighborhood borders Allen Pond Park, the City of Bowie's 85-acre park at 3330 Northview Dr, and Glen Allen Park sits embedded in the community with its playground, tennis courts, and multi-use field. Those are handy landmarks for directing a driver to a court-end lot. Because so much of the neighborhood is cul-de-sacs and curved streets rather than wide arterials, we route the truck in carefully and place the box with the swing room it needs. The section is in ZIP 20715, served by Northview Elementary, Benjamin Tasker Middle, and Bowie High School — all confirming it is within Bowie city limits for permitting.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning. Most Glen Allen addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run.
