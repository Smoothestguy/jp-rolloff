Old Town Bowie isn't like the rest of Bowie, and if you live here you already know it. This is the city's original core — platted back in 1870 as "Huntington City" by developer Benjamin Plumb, laid out on a tight rectilinear grid around the Baltimore & Potomac Railroad junction, with tree-named streets like Chestnut Avenue running one way and numbered streets crossing them. That grid predates the 1960s Levitt subdivisions that fill the rest of Bowie by roughly 90 years, and it shows: small town lots, narrow streets, and aging wood-frame houses instead of wide suburban cul-de-sacs. We're JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler dispatched out of Beltsville, and on these compact historic lots the 10-yard dumpster is usually the right call. It's the smallest can we run, and it's built for exactly the kind of work these old houses need. When you call, you get one of the owners — not a call center.

## The right jobs for a 10-yard here

We call the 10-yard the "garage cleanout" size for good reason: it's the smallest footprint we offer, so you handle a one-area job without paying for cubic yards you'll never fill. The housing stock here is predominantly wood-frame, dating from around 1880 onward — Victorian-era I-houses, front-gabled and L-shaped plans, American Foursquares, bungalows and Cape Cods, many with Queen Anne, Colonial Revival or Dutch Colonial Revival detailing. Those older frame homes throw off a different mix of work than the standardized tract houses elsewhere, and the projects that fit a 10-yard cleanly tend to be these:

- **Garage cleanout.** Detached garages and sheds behind century-old houses collect generations of storage. One 10-yard clears it, and the compact can tucks into a single spot on a tight lot.
- **Bathroom remodel.** Tearing out one dated bath in an old frame house — tile, vanity, tub, plaster and subfloor — makes heavy debris but not high volume. That's exactly where the low 4-foot walls and the included 1-ton weight allowance earn their keep — just know that heavy debris over the first ton runs $105 per additional ton.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Mature trees, overgrown beds and brush on a small Old Town lot fill a 10-yard when you're reclaiming a side or back yard along one of the narrow streets.
- **Estate downsize.** Many of these homes have been held in families for decades. When it's time to clear out a long-held house room by room, the 10-yard moves the job along at a respectful pace without crowding a small lot or a narrow street.

An honest note specific to this neighborhood. Because Old Town Bowie's homes are genuinely old — wood-frame and well past a century in many cases — some of the work here isn't a one-room job at all. Gut-rehabs, full roof and siding tear-offs, and whole-house estate cleanouts of these aging structures throw off far more debris than a 10-yard holds, and you'll fill it fast. The 10-yard is the no-waste choice when the work stays in one room or one corner of the lot.

## Staging it on a narrow street — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's the part worth thinking through, because Old Town is tighter than the rest of Bowie. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not trigger it. So if you've got even a short driveway or an off-street spot on your lot, that's almost always the best place for the can — and you generally won't need a permit at all.

But Old Town's reality is that some of these small lots simply don't have the off-street room. The narrow historic grid and limited curbside space — the kind you see along the district's commercial spine on 9th Street near the 1939 firehouse, or on the tree-named residential blocks — mean the only workable spot is sometimes the street itself. If that's your situation, don't worry about it: just say so when you call, and we'll walk you through the City of Bowie right-of-way permit so you're fully covered before the truck rolls. One more thing worth flagging — parts of Old Town fall under the Old Town Bowie Development District Overlay Zone, which can add design review for work that alters historic structures. That's a planning matter on your end, not a dumpster issue, but it's a good reminder to check before a big tear-off. Either way, because the 10-yard's footprint is so small, clearing a single spot the night before is usually all the prep you'll need.
