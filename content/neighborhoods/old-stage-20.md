That difference matters more than it sounds when you're renting a dumpster. Bigger homes mean bigger projects, and roomier driveways and frontage mean the container can almost always sit on your own property instead of out in the street. We're JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler based about 25 minutes away in Beltsville, and the 20-yard is the size most Old Stage homeowners end up choosing for the work these houses tend to need.

## Why the 20-yard fits Old Stage homes

The 20-yard is our "whole-house cleanout" size, and it's the one most customers pick without ever regretting it. It measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall, holds about nine pickup-truck loads, and comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance built into the price. For a neighborhood of mature split-levels and Colonials, that's the sweet spot. A 10 or 15-yard can feel cramped once a finished basement or a couple of bedrooms start emptying out, and a 30-yard is more steel than most of these projects ever fill. The 20 lands right in the middle: enough room for a serious job, low enough at four feet that you're not heaving debris over your head, and easy to tuck alongside a two-car driveway with space to spare.

Because Old Stage homes have been standing for a few decades now, the projects here are usually mature-home work rather than new construction. That's exactly what this size was made for. The most common jobs we deliver the 20 for around Old Stage are:

## Placement: your driveway, not the street

One of the real advantages of doing a project in Old Stage is that you rarely have to think about the city street at all. The lots and frontage out here tend to be generous — the Old Stage Road corridor includes some large, even multi-acre parcels, and even the subdivision's standard lots give you a real driveway to work with. That means we can almost always set the 20-yard right on your property.

That's worth knowing because of how Bowie's rules work. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster actually sits in a city street or on a public sidewalk — the Public Works Department handles that engineering review. If the container stays on your driveway, no city permit is involved. In Old Stage, the more relevant gatekeeper is usually the **Olde Stage Homeowners Association**, which sets the community's bylaws and handles common-area maintenance. If you're planning to stage the dumpster anywhere other than your own driveway — say, a shared or common area — it's worth a quick check with the HOA first. For the vast majority of Old Stage jobs, though, the driveway does the job and there's no paperwork at all. We'll lay down boards under the container to protect your surface and place it where it's easiest for you to load.

## Loading tips for a remodel or cleanout

A few things make a 20-yard go further on a mature-home project. Break down bulky furniture and built-ins so you're not wasting cubic footage around awkward shapes — a disassembled dresser or shelving unit lies flat and fills the corners. Load the heaviest material first and spread it across the floor of the container rather than piling it in one spot. Keep debris below the top rail; an evenly loaded box is safer to haul and means we can take everything in one trip. And if you're doing a phased remodel where the demolition runs over a couple of weeks, just let us know your timeline so we can plan the swap or pickup around your schedule rather than yours around ours.

For estate cleanouts especially, give yourself a little more time than you think you'll need. Going through a family home is rarely quick, and the nice thing about having the 20 on the driveway is that it's there when you're ready to keep going — no scrambling, no second guessing whether everything will fit.
