The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly four-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. ## Which Amber Meadows jobs suit the 15-yard

Amber Meadows homes were built between roughly 1970 and 1993, so most of them crossed the 30- to 50-year mark some time ago — well into the age when the original kitchen, the original baths, and the first roof all come due. These are modest, family-sized homes, generally running about 1,250 to 2,400 square feet with three to five bedrooms, which means renovation here tends to happen one room and one system at a time rather than as a sweeping whole-house gut. That is exactly the pace the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original kitchen in a 1970s-90s home fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is one of the most common reasons a 15 lands on an Amber Meadows driveway.
- **Roofing tear-off.** Homes of this vintage are well past the age where the first roof gives out, and many here are now on their second or third. A single-layer asphalt tear-off fits comfortably, and the 1.5-ton allowance covers most of these footprints. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop the container.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and dated plumbing from a pair of original bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a home this old, that usually means original fixtures and wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** Split-levels are common in Amber Meadows, and their finished lower levels generate a distinctive debris stream — multi-level flooring tear-outs and dated lower-level remodels. The 15 works well here because the low walls make loading from a stairwell easier, and decades of accumulation in a single basement is right-sized for one container rather than a fleet.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house interior gut, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of the house — that may be a 20 or larger instead. We will tell you straight which container your job needs rather than upsell you into one you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and staging in Amber Meadows

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here, and unusually so.** Amber Meadows sits apart from Bowie's denser townhome tracts: these are detached single-family homes with attached garages on quarter-to-half-acre lots, fronted by private driveways and wide setbacks along sidewalk-lined streets. For a one-room reno, that is exactly what you want — room to stage a 15-yard entirely on your own property. The container needs about 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most of these driveways and generous front lots take that comfortably. Because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size most likely to keep your whole project off the street. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Amber Meadows home has a private driveway and a deep lot, a city street permit is the exception rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops here never touch the right-of-way at all. If your particular setup does push a container toward the street, we flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.
