## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2.5-ton (5,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Woodmore North a typical 25-yard rental runs **$525 to $625, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage**. No tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the haul.

The step up from a 20-yard is genuine volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a mid-job swap — a whole-house remodel, a substantial addition, a large roof, or a full commercial clearout. The walls are still five feet, so a crew can walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but there is meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size containers offer.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Twenty feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. In Woodmore North that is rarely a problem — the lots and driveways here are generous — but it is still worth confirming the exact spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Woodmore North jobs suit the 25-yard

The housing stock is the story here. These are higher-end homes — recent sales generally fall in the $850,000 to $1,400,000 range, with a median near $1.28 million — and every one of them was delivered large, with a finished basement and the kind of square footage that turns a renovation into a serious haul. Owners of homes like these invest in them. That is what drives demand for a container this big. These are the jobs the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** With lots commonly around 0.73 acre and some over a full acre, there is real room to build out here — bumping out the back, adding a wing, or extending up. That throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold, more than a smaller box can take without a second trip.
- **Whole-house remodels.** When an owner modernizes a 5,000-to-8,400-square-foot home top to bottom — kitchen, multiple baths, flooring, and the finished basement all at once — the debris adds up fast. Those finished basements are a real driver: re-finishing one means drywall, old carpet, framing, and flooring leaving the house in bulk, and a single large container beats two hauls.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** A roof on a house this size runs heavy, and the 2.5-ton allowance gives more headroom for shingle weight than the smaller sizes carry.
- **Commercial and estate cleanouts.** Large homes change hands and clear out at scale, and a 25-yard handles a full estate or move-out cleanout in one go rather than forcing repeated swaps.

If your Woodmore North project is on the smaller side — a single-room redo, one bathroom, or a routine basement declutter — a 20-yard or even a 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely big.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is the easy part in Woodmore North.** This is the rare Bowie neighborhood where lot size works entirely in your favor. With homes sitting on roughly 0.7 to over 1.0-acre parcels and long private driveways running back to the house, a 25-yard container can almost always stage on your own driveway rather than in the street. That matters for both convenience and permitting. We will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the driveway surface — important on a long, finished drive — and the wide frontage along the main spine, Woodmore North Boulevard, means the truck has room to set and retrieve the container cleanly.

**The real "permit" here is HOA approval, not a city street permit.** Woodmore North is served by the Stewart's Landing HOA, so dumpster placement and staging should follow your association's approval. For a container that sits on your own driveway, the relevant gatekeeper is HOA architectural and staging guidance — not a City of Bowie filing. The city only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city street; a container placed entirely on your private driveway or an HOA-approved staging area does **not** need one. Given how this neighborhood is laid out, most jobs here never touch the street, so the practical step is a quick check with your HOA on placement rather than a city permit. If your particular address ever forces the container into the roadway, we will flag the city permit requirement up front rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.
