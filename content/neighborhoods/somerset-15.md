## Why the 15-yard fits Somerset homes

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds about four and a half pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Somerset rental runs **$425 to $485, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises added on after the haul.

We call this the "single-room reno" size, and it earns the name on these streets. The original Somerset Cape Cods and ranchers are compact homes on modest mid-century lots with a standard single-width driveway — not the wide two-car aprons you see in Bowie's newer sections. That changes the math two ways. First, the 15-yard's four-foot walls and 14-foot length tuck onto a single-width driveway far more comfortably than a longer 20- or 30-yard would, so you keep the container on your own property instead of out at the curb. Second, the work itself in a 65-year-old Levitt house tends to be one room at a time, which is precisely the volume a 15-yard is built to swallow.

It is the in-between size — bigger than a 10-yard quick cleanup, but still driveway-friendly for these homes. If you are gutting the whole house or staging a large estate clear-out, you may want to step up to a 20. For a contained project on one of these original homes, the 15 is the one we drop most.

## Which Somerset projects suit the 15-yard

Because Somerset's housing stock is original 1960s Levitt, the jobs that come up here are renovation and remodel work, not new-build debris. A 15-yard is sized right for the ones that hit one part of the house at a time:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the original cabinets, counters, flooring, and a wall of plaster or drywall from a Levitt kitchen fills a 15-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on a Cape Cod or rancher of this size sits comfortably inside the 1.5-ton allowance — these are modest roof footprints, not sprawling new-construction spans.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, vanities, tubs, and subfloor from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard job in a home that is on its second or third renovation.
- **Basement cleanout.** Many of these homes finished their basements decades ago; clearing out old paneling, carpet, and the accumulation of 60 years lands neatly in a 15.

If you are not sure whether your project is a 15 or a 20, tell us the room and the scope. We have hauled enough of these Levitt remodels to tell you straight which way to go before you commit.

## Placement, permits, and the S-streets

**Single-width driveway placement.** This is the detail that defines dumpster work in Somerset. These are not big-lot homes — they are mid-century Levitts with one-car-width driveways, so placement is about fit, not just capacity. A 15-yard needs roughly 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, which most of these driveways handle when we set it square. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size that keeps your project on your own property rather than out in the street.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Since Somerset jobs almost always stage on the homeowner's own driveway, a street permit is the exception here, not the rule. If your driveway truly cannot take the container and the street is the only option, we will flag that the moment we look at your address so there are no surprises.

**Finding your door.** Belair at Bowie was laid out with an alliterative wayfinding quirk, and Somerset follows it: every street in the section starts with the letter S — Sussex Lane and its neighbors. It is a small thing, but it means our drivers know the moment they turn in that they are in the right tract, and it cuts down on the wrong-address runs that slow a delivery. When you give us the address, the S-street tells us right away which corner of Belair we are heading to.
