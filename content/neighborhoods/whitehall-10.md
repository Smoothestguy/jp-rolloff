## Staging it on your driveway — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's where Whitehall's layout works in your favor. These are detached single-family homes on private driveways, not townhomes or a gated community, so most 10-yard drops here sit entirely on the homeowner's own driveway or an HOA-approved spot. That means you generally do **not** need a City of Bowie right-of-way permit. The city only requires that permit when a dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging don't trigger it.

The real planning question in Whitehall is usually placement, not permits. If your lot backs onto the Bowie Golf Club, the course boundary can complicate staging debris near the rear of the property — in those cases it's almost always easier to keep the can up front on the driveway, and the 10-yard's compact size makes that simple. A few practical notes: Whitehall is referenced under the City of Bowie's civic and homeowners association listing, so if your block has an active association, follow any approved placement rules alongside the city's street rule. (We'd just say check with your local association directly — there's a lot of bad info online about unrelated "Whitehall" groups elsewhere.) And if the only workable spot truly is the street, no problem; let us know and we'll walk you through the right-of-way permit so you're covered. For a tight single-car driveway, clear the spot the night before — the 10-yard won't ask for much room.
