## The real Woodmore issue: guardhouse and HOA access

Here's what makes Woodmore different from nearly every other Bowie neighborhood we serve. The flagship gated community is built around the Country Club at Woodmore, and its only access road — Pleasant Prospect Road, a horseshoe-shaped county road branching off Woodmore Road — runs through electronic gates controlled by a gatehouse on the western end. Residents use key cards. Non-residents, including contractors and delivery trucks, generally have to be pre-cleared or stop at the gatehouse and state their purpose to an Association agent. In practice, that means a roll-off truck typically needs pre-arranged clearance or a resident escort to reach the home.

That access step — not a street permit — is the single biggest logistical detail for any vendor working inside the gated section. So before delivery day, we'll ask you to do one of two things: either arrange gate clearance for our truck with your Association or gatehouse ahead of time, or plan to meet us at the gate and escort us in. A few minutes of coordination on the front end keeps the delivery from stalling at the guardhouse.

A note for golf-adjacent lots: because the Arnold Palmer-designed championship course sits inside the community, with cart paths and 42 acres of lakes woven through it, we keep the dumpster well off any course frontage and cart paths. Your one-acre driveway gives us plenty of room to stage on-site and stay clear of the course.

It's also worth knowing that Woodmore spans several distinct sub-developments — Woodmore North, Woodmore Estates, Woodmore Highlands, Woodmore Meadows and Woodmore South — and each carries its own HOA. Rules on placement, duration and which surface a container can rest on can vary by section, so a quick check with the HOA that governs your specific address is always worth it before we deliver.

## Why the city permit usually isn't the concern

The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city street. A dumpster on your own driveway or in an HOA-approved staging spot does not trigger that permit. Given Woodmore's one-acre estate lots and wide driveways, the typical 10-yard drop here stages entirely on your own property — so for most Woodmore jobs you do not need a city permit at all. The thing to nail down instead is gate access and, where it applies, your section's HOA placement rules. And in the rare case the only workable spot is genuinely a city street, just tell us when you call and we'll walk you through the Bowie right-of-way permit so you're covered.
