## Which Overbrook jobs suit the 15-yard

Overbrook's housing stock is classic 1960s Levitt: Cape Cods, split-levels, and brick ranch-style houses set along narrow residential streets with mature trees shading the lawns. Nearly six decades on, those mid-century homes are dated by today's standards, right down to their original kitchens and baths — and as one of the last detached sections Levitt completed, Overbrook is squarely in the renovation and cleanout cycle. That is precisely the one-room, one-system rhythm the 15-yard handles best:

- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these detached Capes or ranches fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most of these modest mid-1960s roof footprints. After 55-plus years, original roofs here are well into the replacement cycle. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 60-year-old Levitt build, that usually means dated fixtures and original wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** Clearing out decades of accumulation — or the debris from finishing or re-finishing a basement — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles an estate or turnover cleanout when a long-held, original-owner Overbrook home changes hands.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house interior gut between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of a split-level — that may be a 20 or larger instead. We will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and the O-streets

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here, and in Overbrook it is almost always the right move.** The section follows Belair's wayfinding quirk to the letter: every street begins with the letter O — Overbrook Lane and its neighbors — and locals simply call it the "O section." That alliterative scheme is the first thing our drivers confirm when they turn in, and it makes an address genuinely easy to verify before a drop, which cuts down on the wrong-address runs that slow a delivery.

The streets themselves are the real reason driveway staging matters. Overbrook is a small community — only about seven blocks — of detached single-family homes on narrow residential streets with mature tree cover over the lawns. Those tree-shaded streets are not where you want a steel container sitting: they are tight, and a box on the roadway gets in the way of neighbors and overhanging limbs alike. The good news is that these are established Levitt homes with real private driveways, so for a one-room reno you stage the 15-yard on your own driveway and keep it off the street entirely. A 15-yard needs about 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Overbrook driveways take that comfortably. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size best suited to a compact, mature lot on a narrow street.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Overbrook.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because these Levitt homes came with their own private driveways, a street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops in Overbrook never touch the right-of-way at all, which keeps the container off those narrow, tree-shaded roads. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day, and we will walk that permit through the city with you. If your block runs a formal association with placement rules, give them a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along.
