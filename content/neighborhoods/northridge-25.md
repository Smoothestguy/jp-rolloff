## Why the 25-yard is the major-construction size

The 25-yard is our "major construction" container. It measures roughly 18 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall, and holds about 10.5 pickup-truck loads. Pricing is per material — priced by what you are putting in it, so call for a quote. Whatever the material, the rental includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental. Additional days are available for a daily charge, and heavy materials such as shingles are quoted separately because weight matters, and aggregates such as concrete, dirt, and brick go only in a designated 10-, 15-, or 20-yard container.

That extra height compared to our mid-size boxes is the whole point. On a whole-house remodel or a home addition, the volume adds up fast: framing offcuts, drywall, old cabinetry, flooring, insulation, and trim all stack higher than people expect. The 25-yard gives you room to keep working without calling for a swap halfway through. But that tall box means you want to measure twice before you book. An 18-foot box needs real room to set down and to haul away, and it is worth a two-minute look at your driveway before delivery day. If you are not sure it fits, tell us about your lot when you call and we will help you figure it out — that is the kind of thing a family shop is happy to talk through.

## Northridge jobs that actually need 25 yards

Northridge skews toward higher-value, owner-occupied homes — the kind of housing stock where renovations tend to be substantial rather than cosmetic. The homes here run roughly 1,400 to 4,000 square feet with three to five bedrooms, a mix of contemporary, Colonial Revival, and Craftsman-style single-family houses plus some townhouses. With that footprint, the projects we see pulling a 25-yard are the bigger ones:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out a kitchen, adding a primary suite, or building over the garage on a contemporary 1990s home generates demolition debris plus new-construction scrap. The 25-yard handles both phases.
- **Whole-house remodels.** Taking a 2,000-plus-square-foot Northridge home down to the studs produces a tremendous amount of material. One large box beats three small ones.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** Many of these roofs are now 25 to 30 years old and reaching the end of their first or second shingle life. Shingles are heavy, so weight and volume both come into play here — talk to us about square footage so we get the size right.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** Clearing out an office, retail space, or estate generates mixed bulk that fills a 25-yard quickly.

If your job is smaller — a single-room refresh, a garage purge, a small bath remodel — we will happily steer you toward a smaller, cheaper container instead. We would rather get you the right box than oversell you the big one.

## Permits and placement in Northridge

Here is the good news for most Northridge addresses: you probably will not need a city permit at all. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a public city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging areas do not trigger that requirement. Because Northridge was built with larger lots, attached garages, and wide private driveways, almost every 25-yard we deliver here can sit on the homeowner's own driveway — no permit, no street paperwork, no hassle.

The thing to plan for instead is your HOA. Northridge is governed by homeowners associations that maintain the shared spaces and parks, and they may have their own guidelines on where and how long a dumpster can be staged. A quick check with your association before delivery day saves everyone a headache, especially on a multi-week addition or remodel where the box is sitting out for a while. If your association asks for specifics about the container — dimensions, how it sits, how long it stays — call us and we will give you exactly what you need to share.

The neighborhood is open-street access, not gated, which keeps delivery simple — we just want to make sure the drop spot has clearance for an 18-foot container and the truck that carries it.
