## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly seven-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2.5-ton (5,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Collington Manor rental runs **$525 to $625, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — not a teaser rate that balloons once the scale ticket comes back.

The thing to understand about the 25 is that it is genuinely large. At 20 feet long it is the size where you need to measure twice before booking, because it asks for real room: a clear, straight run of driveway with open space at the back for the rear door to swing. In return you get the volume to keep a major job moving without calling for a swap halfway through. That is why it is the size contractors reach for on a build that runs for weeks rather than a weekend.

## Which Collington Manor jobs suit the 25-yard

Collington Manor's housing leans large — sizable single-family homes on generous lots, alongside townhomes — and that mix produces exactly the kind of heavy, sustained debris the 25-yard is built for. On a 25-to-35-year-old home, the work has graduated past cosmetic, and these are the jobs that fill this container honestly:

- **Home addition.** Bumping out a family room, adding a primary suite, or building over the garage generates framing scrap, old siding, sheathing, drywall, and packaging in volume — the steady debris stream of a multi-week build. A 25-yard keeps it all in one container instead of forcing repeated swaps.
- **Whole-house remodel.** When a Collington Manor home gets gutted across multiple rooms at once — kitchen, baths, flooring, and trim coming out together — the 25 has the capacity to take it without becoming the bottleneck on the job site.
- **Large roofing.** These are big homes, and a tear-off on a four- or five-bedroom footprint produces a lot of weight fast. The 2.5-ton allowance is sized for these larger roofs, but shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop the container.
- **Commercial cleanout.** For a contractor, property manager, or an estate-scale clearout of one of the neighborhood's larger homes, the 25 swallows furniture, fixtures, and accumulated material in one pull rather than three small ones.

If your job is genuinely smaller — a single bath, a one-room reno, a modest cleanout — a 25 will leave you paying for air, and we will say so. We would rather put the right size on your driveway than the biggest one we can rent you. On the other end, if you are running a tear-down-scale project, we will talk through whether a 30 fits better.

## Placement, permits, and the Collington Manor HOA

**Driveway placement is the real advantage here.** What makes Collington Manor unusual for Bowie is the pairing of an active HOA with notably large lots — listings in and around the section sit on roughly one to two acres, with at least one sizable five-bedroom home marketed on about two acres off Jones Bridge Road. Large lots mean long private driveways and room to spare, so even a 20-foot container usually fits entirely on your own property. Unlike the denser, Levitt-era tracts elsewhere in Bowie, a 25-yard here rarely has to sit in the street. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and on a long drive there is generally a spot well clear of the garage and the turnaround.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it seldom bites here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because most Collington Manor detached homes have the driveway length to take a 25-yard on-property, a city street permit is the exception, not the rule. The townhomes are the place to watch: shorter, sometimes shared drives mean a 20-foot container may need either street placement — which brings the permit into play — or HOA sign-off to stage on common ground. We will flag which situation you are in the moment we look at your address, rather than letting you find out on delivery morning.

**Clear it with the association first.** There is a real, registered Collington Manor Homeowners Association, Inc. here, so exterior projects and dumpster staging can fall under HOA architectural and approval rules on top of the city's. For a 25-yard sitting on your own driveway that is usually a non-issue, but anything that touches shared or common area should be cleared with the association first. If you are not sure which community body covers your lot, the City of Bowie publishes an official Homeowners & Civic Associations directory you can use to confirm your contact. We can provide a written service confirmation to forward to the HOA if they ask for one.

Collington Manor sits in a green, wooded part of east Bowie — the corridor near Lottsford Road is the same one that holds the 125-acre Collington retirement community, a useful landmark when you are giving directions.
