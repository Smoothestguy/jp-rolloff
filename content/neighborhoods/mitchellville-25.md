## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 18 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly 10.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: in Mitchellville a 25-yard for construction debris is **priced by material, so call for a quote (other materials priced separately)**, and that price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton weight allowance. Extra days run $5/day, and weight over the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

The jump from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a second haul partway through — a whole-house remodel, a substantial addition, a large roof, or a full cleanout. The walls stand a full six feet, so the deep box swallows a lot of material, and a crew can still toss debris over the side without a ramp once the lower courses are loaded — there is meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size containers offer.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Eighteen feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. The good news in Mitchellville is that the long, wide driveways most homes here were built with were practically made for it — but it is still worth confirming the spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Mitchellville jobs suit the 25-yard

The age and scale of the housing stock is the story here. Because most Mitchellville homes were built between the late 1970s and the early 2000s, renovations out here are not 1960s teardowns — they are updates to 1980s, 1990s, and 2000s-era kitchens, baths, and finished basements that are now a couple of decades behind the times. On large Colonials that have sold in the ~$810,000-$865,000 range, those updates tend to be ambitious. These are the jobs the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** Adding onto a large Colonial on a half-acre or full-acre lot throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a smaller box can take without a mid-project swap.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full reworking of a 1980s-2000s-era home — kitchen, baths, flooring, and finished-basement materials all coming out at once — is a textbook 25-yard job. When a big house gets modernized room by room, the debris adds up fast, and one large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** The bigger footprints of Mitchellville's Colonials mean bigger roofs. A large or multi-layer tear-off runs heavy, and the included 2-ton allowance covers a good portion of the shingle weight, with any excess billed at $105 per additional ton.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** When a larger property or a business in the area clears out material in bulk, the 25-yard handles it in one go.

If your Mitchellville project is on the smaller side — a single-room redo, a bathroom refresh, or a routine basement declutter — a 20-yard or even a 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely big.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and gated access

**Driveway placement, and Mitchellville is built for it.** This is one of the real advantages of working out here versus the older, tighter Levitt sections of Bowie. These homes sit on roughly half-acre to one-acre lots — consistent with Prince George's County's larger-lot residential zoning, where estate-type zones allow lots of about an acre and R-R zoning permits roughly half-acre parcels — and they come with long, wide driveways. That space almost always lets an 18-foot container stage on private property rather than in the street. We will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The permit rule is in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster has to sit in a city street. A container placed entirely on your private driveway does **not** need one. Because Mitchellville's big lots and wide driveways so rarely force a container into the street, the vast majority of jobs here stage on-property with no city permit at all. If your particular address ever does require street placement, we will flag the permit up front rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.

**The gate is the real variable here.** This is what sets Mitchellville apart from open-access Bowie subdivisions. Woodmore — the area's signature development, centered on the Country Club at Woodmore and planned for 398 homes across 799 acres — is gated, and several HOAs operate in and around the Woodmore and Lake Arbor communities. In a gated, HOA-governed community, a dumpster delivery is not just a matter of pulling up to the driveway: access has to be coordinated through the gate, and many associations want the staging location approved in advance. None of that is a problem — we do it routinely — but it does mean we will ask, when you call, whether your community is gated and whether your HOA has a preferred staging spot. A two-minute heads-up keeps the truck from idling at a gate on delivery morning.

**Drive time and routing.** From our Beltsville yard, Mitchellville is about a 25-minute run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway. For the Woodmore-area subdivisions — Woodmore Meadows, Woodmore South, the Kingsford development off Enterprise Road — the practical landmarks for routing are the arterials that bound the area: Enterprise Road (MD-193) on the west, Central Avenue (MD-214) on the south, and Route 50 to the north, with Woodmore Road running east-west through the center. We dispatch every morning, so most addresses here are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run — so when you are running a build on a deadline, the container shows up when the crew needs it.
