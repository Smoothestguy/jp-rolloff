## Placement, permits, and the M-streets

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here, and in Meadowbrook it is almost always the right move.** The section follows Belair's wayfinding quirk to the letter: every street begins with M — Moylan Drive and its neighbors — and that alliterative scheme is the primary way locals, and our drivers, pin down exactly where they are. It is the first thing we confirm when we turn in, which cuts down on the wrong-address runs that slow a delivery.

The bigger placement story is the lots themselves. Most Belair homes sit on standardized parcels of about 8,400 square feet — roughly 0.19 acre, just under a quarter-acre — so Meadowbrook yards and frontages are genuinely compact. The front lawn is small, but the driveway is real: Levitt sold these houses complete with garages and paved driveways, so nearly every home here has a hard surface ready to take a container. For a one-room reno that is exactly what you want. A 15-yard needs about 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Meadowbrook driveways take that comfortably. Staging on your own driveway keeps the box off the tight front lawn and off the narrow M-named streets entirely — which is also what keeps Bowie's permit out of the picture. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size best suited to these sub-quarter-acre lots.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Meadowbrook.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because these standardized Belair lots came with their own private driveways, a street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops in Meadowbrook never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the City of Bowie's Engineering Review and Permitting office, which handles any obstruction within a public right-of-way, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day. If your block runs a formal association with placement rules, give them a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along.
