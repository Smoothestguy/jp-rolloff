## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Yorktown rental includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with that 1.5-ton allowance — construction debris and other materials are priced separately, so call for a quote. Extra time runs +$5/day, and any weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — call to schedule it when your container is ready.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job from start to end. That is why the 15 is best known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for empty air.

## Which Yorktown jobs suit the 15-yard

Yorktown is overwhelmingly detached single-family housing — roughly 99.8% free-standing homes, a higher share than almost any neighborhood in the country — and most of those are medium-to-large three-to-five-bedroom houses, with living areas running somewhere around 1,500 to 2,800 square feet. They are owner-occupied at a very high rate, which means the people doing the work usually own the home and are in it for the long haul. Combine that with the age of the housing stock and you get a steady demand for the one-room, one-system projects the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodels.** Tearing the cabinets, countertops, and flooring out of an original 1960s Levitt kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is the single most common reason a 15 lands on a Y-section driveway.
- **Roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these detached homes fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most modest roofs. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop the container.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a home this age that often means dated fixtures and older wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanouts.** Clearing a finished or half-finished basement — or hauling the debris from finishing one — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles an estate or downsizing cleanout when a longtime Yorktown family home changes hands, which is increasingly common as these original 1960s houses turn over to a new generation.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house gut between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of one of these larger Y-section homes — that may be a 20 or a 30 instead. We will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the easy part here.** Yorktown is instantly identifiable by its all-"Y" street names — the whole of Belair at Bowie was divided into 21 lettered sections, and every street in a section starts with that section's letter, so out here you get Yorktown Drive, Yarmouth Lane, Yellowstone Place, and Youngwood Turn. It is the navigation cue every driver uses, and ours is no exception. Because the neighborhood is almost entirely detached single-family homes, nearly every property has its own private driveway suitable for a dumpster. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and the typical Yorktown driveway in front of one of these three-to-five-bedroom houses takes that comfortably. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Yorktown.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Y-section home has its own driveway, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only workable spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than let you find out on delivery day.

**No HOA to clear — which sets Yorktown apart.** Unlike Bowie's newer townhome-and-HOA communities, the Yorktown at Belair area has no homeowners association, so staging a dumpster on your own driveway is governed by you and the city rules, not by a board's sign-off. That is a real difference: in much of newer Bowie you would be chasing architectural-committee approval before a container could land, while in Yorktown nearly every drop sits on the homeowner's private driveway with no association approval and no street permit. It is one of the reasons the work here skews the way it does — full-house renovations, additions, roof tear-offs, and estate cleanouts on aging Levitt homes, handled one room and one driveway at a time.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway.
