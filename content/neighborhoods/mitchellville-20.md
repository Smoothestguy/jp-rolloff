Mitchellville is the part of Bowie that does not look like the rest of Bowie. While the original Belair at Bowie sections were Levitt-built starter homes from the early 1960s, Mitchellville rose a generation later — most of its houses went up from the late 1970s through the 1990s and into the early 2000s, after Prince George's County rezoned the area's former tobacco and cattle farmland for residential use between roughly 1979 and 1995. What got built on that farmland was bigger and grander: large Colonial Revivals with front columns, set on half-acre to one-acre lots along curving subdivision streets, much of it inside the gated, country-club community of Woodmore. That housing stock is exactly why the 20-yard roll-off is the workhorse size out here, and why a few things about delivery in Mitchellville work differently than they do in older Bowie.

We are JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler dispatched out of Beltsville, about 25 minutes from Mitchellville by way of Route 197 or the BW Parkway. When you call, you reach the family that owns the trucks — not a call center.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Mitchellville rental runs **$450 to $525, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises tacked on afterward.

Those five feet of wall height are the part most people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and pitch it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard earned its nickname — the "whole-house cleanout" size — and why it lands in the sweet spot for so many homes here: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but short of the oversized 30-yard that really only makes sense for new construction or full structural demolition. It is the size most customers end up choosing, and rarely regret.

## Why the 20-yard fits Mitchellville specifically

Because Mitchellville's homes were built largely in the 1980s, 1990s, and early 2000s, the renovation work here looks different from the 1960s teardowns common in the older Levitt sections. Out here you are not gutting a tiny original Cape Cod — you are updating a 1990s-era kitchen, refinishing a finished basement, or modernizing the baths in a large brick Colonial that has aged into its first or second real renovation. These are substantial projects, but they are usually contained to one house. That is precisely the 20-yard's wheelhouse.

The neighborhood's affluence reinforces it. Mitchellville is one of the wealthiest predominantly African-American communities in the country, with high homeownership and the kind of discretionary budget that fuels real remodels and careful estate work rather than patch jobs. Here are the jobs that come up again and again across Woodmore Meadows, Woodmore South, Kingsford, and the rest of the subdivisions:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's signature role. Clearing one of these larger Colonials — between owners, before a sale, or after years of accumulation in a four- or five-bedroom house with a finished basement — fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-baths update, or a run of bedrooms and a basement getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job in a 1990s-era home reaching its first major modernization. Pulling an original kitchen plus a bath or two generates exactly the debris volume a 20 was built for.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Many Mitchellville homes have been held by the same families since the subdivisions were new. When one of those large homes changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these homes generally fits within the 2-ton allowance, though the larger roof footprints here are worth a quick conversation before we drop the can.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are taking a home down to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the Mitchellville difference

**Driveway placement.** This is where Mitchellville's big-lot layout pays off. With homes sitting on roughly half-acre to one-acre lots and the long, wide driveways that come with larger Colonials, there is almost always room to stage a 20-yard entirely on private property. The can needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door — and these driveways tend to have that to spare. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Mitchellville's lots and driveways are so generous, a city street permit is the rare exception here, not the rule — most placements avoid the street entirely and need no permit at all. If the only workable spot turns out to be a city street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Gates and HOAs — the part that matters most here.** This is the one way Mitchellville is genuinely harder to serve than the open-access Levitt sections, and it is worth planning around. Woodmore is a gated community centered on the Country Club at Woodmore, and gated access means we cannot simply roll up to your driveway — the delivery has to be coordinated through the gate, and the gatehouse or your HOA needs to know we are coming. On top of that, several homeowner associations operate across the Woodmore golf community and nearby Lake Arbor, and HOA-governed streets often expect an approved staging location. None of this is a problem; it just means a phone call ahead. When you book, give us your subdivision and gate details and we will sort out access before the truck leaves the yard, so the morning of delivery goes smoothly. We are also happy to provide a service confirmation you can forward to your association.

**Routing.** The practical landmarks for getting to you are the arterials that frame the area: Enterprise Road (MD-193) on the west, Central Avenue (MD-214) on the south, Church Road on the east, and Route 50 on the north, with Woodmore Road running through the middle. Tell us which subdivision you are in — Woodmore Meadows, Woodmore South, Kingsford off Enterprise, or elsewhere in 20721 — and we will route the morning run straight to your gate. We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning, so an early call often makes a same-day drop.
