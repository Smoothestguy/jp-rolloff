## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard is the smallest footprint we offer, measuring 12 ft long by 8 ft wide by 3.5 ft tall. It holds roughly three pickup-truck loads of debris and comes with a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance. In Glen Allen a 10-yard **starts at $550 for construction debris (other materials priced separately)**, and that price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 1-ton allowance. Keep it longer for $5 a day, and any weight over the first ton is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request, so just call to schedule when you are ready.

The low 3.5-foot walls are the quiet advantage. You can walk right up and drop debris over the side without a ramp or a heave, which matters when you are loading heavy, dense material like old bathroom tile, plaster, or yard soil. The 1-ton allowance is sized for exactly that kind of work — a single-room remodel or a focused cleanout, not a whole-house gut. And because the container fits in a single car spot, it tucks onto a Glen Allen driveway and leaves plenty of room beside it. These are larger move-up homes, so the bigger projects — a full kitchen gut, a deck-and-roof combination, a multi-room remodel — often run toward our 20 to 30-yard sizes. But for the jobs below, the 10 is the right call, and we will tell you so honestly when it is not.

## Which Glen Allen jobs suit the 10-yard

This is the size folks call the "garage cleanout," and around Glen Allen that is the call we field most. Here are the four jobs that land squarely in a 10-yard on these cul-de-sac streets:

- **Garage cleanouts.** These Drees homes are now 25 to 35 years old, which is right about when a garage hits capacity — paint cans, old lumber, broken patio furniture, holiday boxes, and the lawn gear that piled up across two decades. A single-bay clear-out empties into a 10-yard without overpaying for space, and the low walls make tossing it in straightforward.
- **Bathroom remodels.** A single-bath gut in one of these colonials — the dated tile, the tub, the vanity, drywall, and subfloor — is the textbook 10-yard job. That dense tile and plaster stays well inside the 1-ton allowance for one bathroom. With homes this age now on their first or second renovation cycle, updating an original 1990s bath is one of the most common projects we see here.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Glen Allen's manicured lawns and the mature landscaping that has filled in over the decades mean brush, branches, sod, and the occasional removed tree. The low-walled 10-yard loads green debris easily, and since yard waste is heavy once it is wet, the 1-ton allowance is sized for a single yard's worth. The neighborhood borders Allen Pond Park and wraps around its own Glen Allen Park, so there is no shortage of mature trees on these lots.
- **Estate downsize.** When a longtime family home on one of these courts changes hands, a 10-yard handles the furniture, household goods, and accumulated clutter of a focused downsize. It is a measured cleanout, not a full whole-house clear-out — the right size when you are clearing out steadily rather than all at once.

If you are gut-renovating a kitchen, replacing a roof and deck together, finishing a basement, or doing a multi-room modernization, those run heavier and bulkier than a 10 is built for, and we will point you to a 20 or 30-yard instead. The 10-yard earns its keep on the one-room, one-corner, one-weekend jobs.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the default in Glen Allen, and the neighborhood is built for it.** This is a subdivision of detached single-family homes with generous private driveways, and it is laid out with numerous quiet cul-de-sacs — which means many homes sit on pipestem or court-end lots with curved frontage and limited street curb space. That makes the private driveway the practical place to stage a roll-off rather than the street. A 10-yard needs only about 12 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and a clear swing for the rear door, and that fits easily in the wide driveways here. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface. If you need to point our driver in, landmarks like Allen Pond Park or Glen Allen Park make the turn easy to call out.

**The City of Bowie permit rule.** Glen Allen is inside the City of Bowie — its kids attend Northview Elementary, Benjamin Tasker Middle, and Bowie High — so the city's right-of-way rule applies. That rule is straightforward: a permit is required **only** if the dumpster sits in a city street. A container placed on your private driveway does not require one. Because nearly every Glen Allen home has a wide off-street driveway, the vast majority of our drops here never touch the right-of-way and never trigger a permit. Glen Allen does have its own homeowners association, reportedly managed by ProCom, so if your project involves exterior staging or a shared area it is worth a quick check on placement or screening rules — but for a dumpster sitting on your own driveway, that is generally yours to use.

**Drive time.** Glen Allen is about 25 minutes from our Beltsville yard, an easy run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway, with U.S. 50 and U.S. 301 carrying us the rest of the way into the neighborhood. We dispatch every morning, so same-day delivery is common when you call early, and the rest of the schedule fills inside 24 hours. You are close enough that getting a truck to a Glen Allen cul-de-sac is never the holdup.
