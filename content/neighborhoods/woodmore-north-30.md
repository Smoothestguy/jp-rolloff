## Staging it on a Woodmore North driveway — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's where Woodmore North stands apart from Bowie's older, tightly-platted Levitt-era neighborhoods with their small lots and narrow streets. The homes here sit on large lots — commonly around 0.73 acre, with some parcels reaching roughly 0.9 to over an acre — fronted by long private driveways. That's a real advantage with a container as long as the 30-yard: in nearly every case the can sits entirely on your own driveway, with the run-up the truck needs, and it never touches the street.

That placement matters for the permit, too. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city street. A driveway drop doesn't trigger it. So on a large-lot home along Woodmore North Boulevard or the surrounding streets, you'll almost always skip the city permit entirely — the main thing to sort out is HOA sign-off, not street access.

That HOA piece is the real variable here, and it's worth a word. This area is served by a homeowners' association — listings tie it to the Stewart's Landing HOA, administered locally and managed through a community association company, with modest monthly dues. Because an HOA is in place, exterior projects and dumpster staging can fall under approval or architectural rules, so it's worth confirming with your association before a delivery whether you need sign-off on where the can sits. In practice, with these long private driveways, staging on your own property is the simple path and keeps you clear of the street permit. If for some reason the street genuinely is your only option, just tell us when you call and we'll walk you through the right-of-way permit so you're covered. Either way, the prep on your end is simple: clear the run-up and the landing spot the night before, and we'll handle the rest.
