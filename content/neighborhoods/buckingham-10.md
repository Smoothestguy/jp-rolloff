## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard is the smallest footprint we offer, measuring 12 ft long by 8 ft wide by 3.5 ft tall. It holds roughly three pickup-truck loads of debris and comes with a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance. Pricing is per material: in Buckingham a 10-yard **starts at $550 for construction debris (other materials priced separately)**, which includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 1-ton allowance. Extra days run $5/day, and any weight over the first ton is $105 per additional ton.

The low 3.5-foot walls are the quiet advantage. You can walk right up and drop debris over the side without a ramp or a heave, which matters when you are loading heavy, dense material like old bathroom tile, plaster, or yard soil. The 1-ton allowance is sized for exactly that kind of work — a single-room remodel or a focused cleanout, not a whole-house gut. And because the container fits in a single car spot, it tucks neatly onto a Buckingham driveway without swallowing the whole thing. If your project turns out bigger than one room, we will point you to a 15 or 20-yard instead — but for the jobs below, the 10 is the right call.

## Which Buckingham jobs suit the 10-yard

This is the size folks call the "garage cleanout," and around Buckingham that is the call we field most. Here are the four jobs that land squarely in a 10-yard on these B-section streets:

- **Garage cleanouts.** Belair homes were sold in five Levitt models — the Cape Cod, the Rancher, three- and four-bedroom Colonials, and the Country Clubber — and every one came with a garage and paved driveway as standard. Those garages range from the Rancher's single bay to the four-bedroom Colonial's oversized two-car, and after 60 years they fill up with paint cans, old lumber, broken tools, and boxes. A single-car bay clears into a 10-yard without overpaying for space.
- **Bathroom remodels.** A single-bath gut in an original Levitt home — the dated tile, the tub, vanity, drywall, and subfloor — is the textbook 10-yard job. That dense tile and plaster stays well inside the 1-ton allowance for one bathroom, and modernizing those original baths is one of the most common projects on these streets.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Buckingham sits at the north end of the section right up against Whitemarsh Park, the 182-acre City of Bowie park with the Bowie Playhouse and its ballfields and trails. The mature trees that fill out these older lots mean brush, branches, sod, and the occasional removed tree load neatly into the low-walled 10. Green yard debris is heavy once it is wet, so the 1-ton allowance is sized for a single yard's worth.
- **Estate downsize.** When a longtime family home on Bradford or Brewster changes hands — and plenty of these original Levitt houses are reaching that point — a 10-yard handles the furniture, household goods, and accumulated clutter of a focused downsize. It is a measured cleanout, not a full whole-house clear-out.

If you are gut-renovating a dated Levitt kitchen, finishing a basement, tearing off a roof, or doing a full-home modernization at once, those run heavier and bulkier than a 10 is built for, and we will tell you so up front. The 10-yard earns its keep on the one-room, one-corner, one-weekend jobs.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the default in Buckingham, and the neighborhood is built for it.** When Levitt platted Belair, most lots — Buckingham included — were laid out at a uniform roughly 8,400 square feet to keep pricing fair, with corner lots costing a bit more. Those are modest, consistent suburban lots with smaller yards but a paved driveway on every property. That means the dumpster almost always stages on the driveway rather than the lawn. A 10-yard needs about 12 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and a clear swing for the rear door, and that fits comfortably in the standard Buckingham driveway. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The City of Bowie permit rule.** Buckingham is inside the City of Bowie, and the city's rule is straightforward: a right-of-way permit is required **only** if the dumpster sits in a city street. A container placed on your private driveway does not require one. Because nearly every Buckingham home has that included Levitt driveway, the vast majority of our drops here never touch the right-of-way and never trigger a permit. The B-section streets are also typically organized as civic associations rather than dues-and-architectural-control HOAs, so staging a dumpster on your own driveway generally does not call for separate HOA sign-off. If your particular street has its own rule, it is always worth a quick check with your association — but for most addresses here, the driveway is yours to use.

**Drive time.** Buckingham is about 25 minutes from our Beltsville yard, an easy run up Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, so same-day delivery is common when you call early, and the rest of the schedule fills inside 24 hours. You are close enough that getting a truck to a B-section address is never the holdup.
