The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. ## Which Long Ridge jobs suit this size

- **Full demolition.** Taking a tired Country Clubber down to the studs, gutting the whole interior of a 60-year-old two-story Colonial, or clearing an addition generates far more debris than most owners expect from a single house. Because every home in Long Ridge is the same large model, the demo footprint here is consistently bigger than in Bowie's Rancher sections — and the 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers.
- **Commercial site work.** A build-out, tenant turnover, or contractor project near Long Ridge throws off a continuous stream of fixtures, ceiling grid, flooring, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path so the crew never waits on a haul.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** A whole-house cleanout of a long-held, original-owner Country Clubber — decades of belongings, old appliances, and the contents of a five-bedroom home plus its basement — piles up fast. Fourteen truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** Turning over several units at once, with old carpet, mattresses, appliances, and tenant debris, is exactly the volume the 30-yard is built for.

There are two signature Long Ridge renovations that often start as smaller jobs but scale straight into 30-yard work. The **expandable-attic conversion** is the first: Country Clubbers were built with a distinctive unfinished attic that owners routinely turn into a bedroom, office, or beamed loft. A modest conversion fits a 15 or 20, but when the work opens up the entire top floor and gets combined with other rooms, the drywall, insulation, and framing debris adds up to a bigger box. The second is the **full roof tear-off.** These homes have a flat, low-slope rear dormer originally finished with roll roofing — a section that wears out faster than the main pitched roof — and on 60-year-old houses, replacing that failure-prone roofing along with a multi-layer tear-off of the main roof can run heavy. Roofing debris is dense, so we will talk through the square footage and layers before we drop the 30-yard and watch the tonnage with you.

For a single kitchen, one pair of baths, or a routine basement clear-out, a 10-, 15-, or 20-yard is almost always the better fit in Long Ridge. The 30-yard earns its keep when capacity — not budget — is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Placement and permits in Long Ridge

This is where Long Ridge's larger lots really pay off. Because the homes here are big Country Clubbers on slightly larger lots than the typical Levitt Bowie section, with private driveways and wider frontage, we can almost always stage the 30-yard entirely on the homeowner's own driveway or an HOA-approved spot. That keeps the box off the street, and it keeps the paperwork simple: **the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not.** In Long Ridge, a street permit is the exception rather than the rule, which sets this section apart from the tighter, denser parts of Bowie.
