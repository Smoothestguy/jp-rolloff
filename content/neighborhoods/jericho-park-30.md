## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and our typical pricing runs $595 to $725 — a number that already includes delivery, pickup, and the included tonnage, so there are no surprise line items waiting at the end.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

Jericho Park is not one of Bowie's tightly packed planned tracts. That combination of older housing stock and big, private ground is exactly what drives the heavier jobs the 30-yard was built for:

Original drywall, plaster, lath, dated cabinetry, flooring, and trim come out in a steady stream, and the 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.

One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** On homes held by one family for decades on these bigger lots, estate and downsizing cleanouts pile up fast — furniture, appliances, garage and barn-style storage, and the contents of a whole level at once. Nine truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** When several units or a larger property need turning over at the same time — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and accumulated debris together — that is precisely the volume the 30-yard was built to handle.

For a single-room remodel, a bathroom refresh, or a routine cleanout in Jericho Park, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Placement is the easy part in Jericho Park

Here is the good news that sets Jericho Park apart from Bowie's denser subdivisions: there is almost always room to stage the container entirely on your own property. These are large lots with long private driveways and open yard space, so a 30-yard rarely needs to touch the street. That matters for two reasons.

First, the 30-yard is the longest box we run and needs a clear, straight run-up to set down. On a tight subdivision frontage that can be a puzzle; on a half-acre-to-multi-acre Jericho Park lot, the long driveways and open ground here usually give the truck and trailer plenty of room to back in, drop clean, and pull out without any squeezing.

Second, it keeps the permit question simple. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because most Jericho Park drops stage on the driveway or in the yard, the typical job here needs no city permit at all. If the only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's right-of-way review applies, and we are glad to walk you through it before your container shows up.

A little planning still goes a long way. Access from the main roads is by turning at the traffic light onto Jericho Park Road, and the streets beyond run through quiet residential and semi-rural ground rather than wide arterials. Telling us your exact street, where you want the box, and the shape of your driveway or yard run-up lets us route the truck cleanly and place the 30-yard right the first time — clear of soft ground, low branches, and septic or well lines that come with larger rural-edge lots.
