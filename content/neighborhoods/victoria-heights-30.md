## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material — construction debris in the 30-yard starts at $665, with other materials priced separately. That price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance; extra days run $5 per day. Weight beyond the 3-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton, and pickup is on request, so just call to schedule the haul-away.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why Victoria Heights jobs reach this size

Victoria Heights is not one of the small early-1960s starter sections — it is the last detached single-family wave Levitt built in Belair, realized roughly between 1964 and 1968 before the company shifted to townhomes and Belair Town at the end of the decade. That timing matters for a roll-off. This is a section of the larger Levitt models — the ramblers, the three- and four-bedroom Colonials, and the top-of-line Country Clubber that Belair marketed as its most luxurious home (the original sample sat over on Sussex Lane). Those bigger detached houses are now 55 to 60 years old and squarely into their major-renovation cycle, which is the work the 30-yard is made for:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting a 1960s Country Clubber or Colonial down to the studs — pulling the original kitchen, taking out interior walls, clearing a long-finished basement, or removing a decades-old addition — produces far more debris than most owners expect. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs and tenant turnovers throw off a continuous stream of fixtures, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** Whole-house clear-outs and estate cleanups in a home a family has held since the original Levitt sale pile up fast. Nine truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** Turning over several units at the same time — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and tenant debris together — is exactly the volume the 30-yard was built to handle.

For a single-room remodel or a routine garage cleanout in Victoria Heights, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip. Because Victoria Heights leans toward those larger detached models rather than the compact early Capes, whole-house remodels here genuinely do generate the debris volume that justifies the biggest box we run.

## Placement on Victoria Heights's quarter-acre lots

Here is where Victoria Heights is genuinely easier than a lot of DMV neighborhoods. Standard Levitt Belair lots run about 8,400 square feet — roughly a quarter acre — and were sized uniformly to keep pricing fair across the section, with corner lots carrying a premium. The practical upshot for a roll-off: most Victoria Heights homes have a real, modest driveway and a side yard, so we can usually stage even a 30-yard on your own property rather than out in the street.

That matters because of how the city handles permits. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. And because the original detached Levitt sections like Victoria Heights were built as conventional subdivisions and are widely marketed as no-HOA, any neighborhood association covering these streets is generally a voluntary civic association rather than a mandatory HOA with architectural-control bonds. (If you want to be certain about your specific street, the City of Bowie keeps an official homeowners and civic association directory you can check.) Put those two things together and, for most jobs here, staging on your private driveway skips the street permit entirely. If your only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's review applies, and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.

A 30-yard does ask for a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes — it is a big box on a long frame, and the driver needs room to back the trailer in straight. On a uniform quarter-acre Victoria Heights lot that usually works fine, but it is worth a look before we arrive, especially if a remodel has narrowed the driveway with materials or a contractor's truck. And because Bowie's Levitt sections are named so every street in a section shares the same first letter, an address on Victoria Heights Drive reads unambiguously as the V section — telling us your exact street helps us route the truck and trailer cleanly the first time. If the approach is tight, sloped, or shares a curb cut with a neighbor, mention it when you book and we will plan the drop around it.
