While much of Bowie is built on Levitt-era ramblers and split-levels, Saddlebrook is a late-1990s-to-early-2000s subdivision of larger custom colonials — full-size homes that ran roughly 2,228 to 4,462 square feet when they were built between about 1997 and 2001, most with three to six bedrooms and full basements. The adjacent Saddlebrook West section, built a little later (around 2001 to 2004), pushed even bigger, with New Traditional homes ranging up to about 5,108 square feet behind their brick fronts and Colonial Revival details. When a house like that gets cleaned out or renovated, the debris adds up fast — and the 20-yard roll-off is the size most of those jobs settle on.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material, and for a Saddle Brook job it is **priced by what you are throwing away, so call for a quote**. Every rental includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance; extra days run $5/day and weight beyond the 2-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton. Aggregates like concrete, brick, and dirt are accepted in the 10, 15, and 20-yard sizes. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule when you are ready.

The four-foot wall height is the part people tend to underestimate in a good way. It is low enough that you can walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, while the box's 22-foot length gives it the floor space to swallow the volume of a real project. That balance is why the 20-yard is nicknamed the "whole-house cleanout" size, and it is the container most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret. It is the step up from the quick-job 10 and 15-yard, without committing to the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo.

## Why the 20-yard fits Saddle Brook specifically

Here is the thing about these homes: they are big, but a single one of them is usually still a single, contained project. A full-size colonial with a finished basement holds a lot — but clearing one house, or remodeling several rooms of it, lands squarely in the 20-yard's range rather than spilling into 30-yard territory. That makes the 20 the natural default on these streets.

The jobs we see most often in Saddle Brook:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's signature role. When a Saddlebrook colonial turns over, clearing the main floor, the bedrooms, and that full basement fills a 20-yard about right — enough room for the volume these larger homes generate, without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-baths refresh, or a run of bedrooms getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job. These homes are now 20-plus years old, old enough that original kitchens, baths, and finishes are reaching the age where owners want them updated. Tearing those out across several rooms generates exactly the debris a 20 was built for.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When one of these family homes changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and a basement's worth of accumulation in a single container — which matters when the house has the square footage Saddlebrook homes carry.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on a home this size fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most jobs. On the very largest Saddlebrook West roofs we may talk through a 30, but the 20 covers the majority.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are gutting one of these big colonials to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and HOA staging

**Driveway placement.** Saddlebrook was platted with uniform private driveways serving these two- and three-car-garage colonials, and that works in your favor. A 20-yard fits most of these driveways with room to spare — it needs roughly 22 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and a Saddlebrook driveway almost always has it. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Saddlebrook home has its own ample driveway, a city street permit is the exception here, not the rule — most projects in this neighborhood stage entirely on private property with no permit at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**HOA coordination.** This is where Saddle Brook differs from Bowie's older civic-association neighborhoods. Saddlebrook West is served by an active HOA that manages common amenities — a community pool, two tennis courts, and a playground — and Saddlebrook homes carry quarterly association dues. Staging on your own driveway is typically straightforward, but if a delivery would touch a shared drive or any common area, it is worth a quick check with the association before we roll. We are happy to provide a service confirmation you can forward to your HOA so the placement is squared away in advance.

**Drive time and the lay of the land.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway, so we know the Race Track Road corridor that forms the spine of this area well — Saddlebrook East Park sits right on it at 8311 Race Track Road, and the neighborhood's equestrian street names like Running Horse Place and Race Horse Lane trace back to the former Bowie Race Track next door, the old thoroughbred grounds the City has been clearing for recreation.
