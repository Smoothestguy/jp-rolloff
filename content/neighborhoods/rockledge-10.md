## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard is the smallest footprint we run, measuring 12 ft long by 8 ft wide by 3.5 ft tall. It holds roughly three pickup-truck loads of debris and comes with a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance. In Rockledge a typical 10-yard rental runs **$350 to $425, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping fee sprung on you after the load hits the scale.

Those low 3.5-foot walls are the quiet advantage on an older Levitt home. You can walk right up and drop debris over the side without a ramp or a heave, which matters when you are loading the heavy, dense material these houses give up — old tile, plaster, dirt, roofing scrap. The 1-ton allowance is sized for exactly that kind of focused job: a single-room remodel or a one-corner cleanout, not a whole-house gut. And because the container fits inside a single car spot, it slides onto a Rockledge driveway without swallowing the whole thing. If your project outgrows one room, we will steer you to a 15 or 20-yard instead — but for the jobs below, the 10 is the right call.

## Which Rockledge jobs suit the 10-yard

This is the size folks know as the "garage cleanout," and on these detached Levitt lots that is the call we field most. The four jobs that land squarely in a 10-yard around Rockledge:

- **Garage cleanouts.** The garages on these 1960s detached homes fill a 10-yard about right — years of paint cans, warped lumber, broken tools, and boxes come up fast. It is the classic single-bay clear-out without paying for space you will not use.
- **Bathroom remodels.** A single-bath gut in an original Rockledge home — old tile, the tub, vanity, drywall, and subfloor — is the textbook 10-yard job. On a house with its first-generation 1960s finishes, the dense tile and plaster from one bathroom stay well inside the 1-ton allowance.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Six decades of growth means mature trees and overgrown beds on a lot of these R-section lots, many of them within a short walk of Rockledge Park over on Reston Lane. Brush, branches, sod, and a removed tree or two load neatly into the low-walled 10, and the 1-ton allowance is sized for a single yard's worth of green, heavy debris.
- **Estate downsize.** When a long-held family home in Rockledge changes hands — and on a tract of original 1960s Levitt houses, plenty do — a 10-yard handles the furniture, household goods, and accumulated clutter of a measured downsize. It is the right size for emptying a home a room at a time rather than a full gut.

If you are taking on a kitchen tear-out, a roof, siding, or a whole interior gut at once, those run heavier and bulkier than a 10 is built for, and we will tell you so up front rather than watch you overfill it. The 10-yard earns its keep on the one-room, one-corner, one-weekend jobs that these older Levitt homes throw off.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the default in Rockledge.** This is a uniform tract of detached single-family homes, and nearly every one of them has its own private driveway — so the 10-yard, built to tuck inside a single car spot, drops onto a Rockledge drive without blocking the second car or the walk. A 10-yard needs about 12 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and a clear swing for the rear door. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, which is worth doing on these older driveways.

**Finding the door is easy on the R-streets.** Belair at Bowie was platted with an alliterative quirk that still helps our drivers: every street in a section starts with the same letter as the section name, so in Rockledge they all begin with "R" — Rockledge Drive, Reston Lane, and the rest. Give us the address and the truck knows it is hunting an R-street in the 20715 area, which keeps the morning drop quick and predictable.

**The City of Bowie permit rule.** Rockledge sits inside the City of Bowie, and the city only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster has to sit in a city street. A container placed entirely on your private driveway does **not** require the permit. Because Rockledge is a non-gated tract where almost every dumpster can land on the homeowner's own driveway, most jobs here never trigger it — and if your address is the rare case that needs the street, we will flag it before we roll.

**Drive time.** Rockledge is about 25 minutes from our Beltsville yard, an easy run up Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, so early calls regularly make the same-day drop and the rest are typically scheduled inside 24 hours.
