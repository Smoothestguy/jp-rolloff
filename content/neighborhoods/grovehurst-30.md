## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about 14 pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material, so the 30-yard is priced by what is going in — call for a quote. The rental includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance; extra days run $5/day and any weight over the allowance is $105 per additional ton.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, decking, siding, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why a Grovehurst job reaches this size

Grovehurst is not one of Bowie's dense, 1960s Levitt-built sections with narrow lots and shared frontage. It is a newer, smaller community — built primarily in the 1990s in Colonial Revival and New Traditional styles, mostly four- and five-bedroom homes running roughly 2,400 to 4,200-plus square feet on quarter- to half-acre lots that back up to hardwood forest. That combination of large homes and generous lots is exactly what occasionally produces a job too big for a smaller bin:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting one of these 1990s Colonials down to the studs throws off far more debris than most owners expect — drywall, dated cabinetry, flooring, trim, and fixtures come out in a steady stream. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs, additions, and whole-house renovations on homes this size throw off a continuous run of framing, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** On homes held by one family since the '90s, estate and downsizing cleanouts pile up fast — furniture, appliances, and the contents of a finished basement all at once. Roughly 14 truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** When several units or an entire property turn over at the same time — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and accumulated debris together — that is precisely the volume the 30-yard was built to handle.

For the day-to-day Grovehurst project, the 30-yard is usually more box than you need. A roof or siding tear-off, a deck rebuild, a sunroom addition, or a big yard-and-tree-debris cleanout from those forested back lots typically fits a 15- or 20-yard. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip. If you are not sure, describe the job and we will size it honestly — even if the honest answer is a smaller container.

## Placement is the easy part in Grovehurst

Here is the good news, and it is where Grovehurst differs sharply from Bowie's tighter, shared-wall neighborhoods. These are large detached Colonials with attached garages on quarter- to half-acre lots, which means most homes have a wide private driveway and real side-yard room. On a lot like that, even the 30-yard — the longest box we run, and one that needs a clear, straight run-up to set down — can almost always be staged entirely on your own property.

That matters for two reasons. First, the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street; the Bowie Public Works department handles that engineering review, but placement on a private driveway does not trigger it. Because Grovehurst's driveways are deep enough to take a 30-yard, most drops here never touch a city street and never need a permit at all. Second, Grovehurst has an active homeowners association that maintains common areas and sets community standards, so it is worth a quick check on architectural and placement rules before a long-term container sits out front. HOA-approved staging on private property does not require a city permit either — so between a deep driveway and a green light from the association, placement is rarely the obstacle it can be in older parts of Bowie.

The one thing the 30-yard does ask for is room to maneuver. It needs a long, straight approach to set down and pick up cleanly, so when you call, tell us your street — Grovehurst streets include Danbury Drive and Dartford Lane — and where you are picturing the box. That lets us confirm the driveway has the run-up the trailer needs and route the truck cleanly the first time. The mature tree canopy that makes these lots so appealing also means we like to know about low limbs and tight side-yard gates before we arrive, not after.
