Buckingham is one of the original "B section" neighborhoods of Belair at Bowie, and you can read its age in the street signs: Buckingham Drive, Bradford Lane, Brewster Lane, Briercrest Road, Brunswick Lane — Levitt gave every street here a name beginning with B so the section had its own character and was easy to find your way around. The houses behind those signs went up in overlapping stages between roughly 1960 and 1964, among the very first Belair sections built alongside Somerset and Kenilworth. That makes Buckingham's housing stock 60-plus years old now, and that single fact drives most of the dumpster work we do here. These are original Levitt homes coming due for their second or third real renovation, and the 20-yard roll-off is the size most of those projects land on.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Buckingham rental runs **$450 to $525, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises added after the fact.

Those five feet of wall height are the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes in the B section: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret.

## Why the 20-yard fits Buckingham specifically

Buckingham was built in the Levitt era on a handful of repeating models — the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the three- and four-bedroom Colonials, and the Country Clubber — and every one of them was delivered with a garage and a paved driveway as standard. That detail matters more than it sounds. Sixty years on, the original builds feel dated: standardized kitchens, original baths, dark or undersized layouts that today's buyers want opened up. When a Buckingham home turns over or a longtime owner finally pulls the trigger on a modernization, the work tends to be substantial but contained to one house. That is exactly the 20-yard's wheelhouse.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again on the B-named streets:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's best-known role — it is even nicknamed the "whole-house cleanout" size. Clearing an original Buckingham Levitt home between owners, or after years of accumulation, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-baths gut, or a run of bedrooms getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job in a Levitt home on its second or third renovation. Tearing out an original Levitt kitchen and a bath or two generates exactly the debris volume a 20 was built for.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Buckingham has held many of the same families for decades. When one of those longtime homes changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical Belair-era roof tear-off fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most single-layer asphalt jobs on these modest-footprint homes.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are gutting the house to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the Buckingham advantage

**Driveway placement.** This is where Buckingham has a built-in advantage over Bowie's newer subdivisions. Belair laid the section out on fairly uniform lots — most around 8,400 square feet — and gave every model its own driveway, from the Rancher's one-car garage up to the four-bedroom Colonial's oversized two-car garage. The yards themselves are modest, so the driveway is almost always the right staging spot, and a 20-yard fits most of these driveways with room to spare. It needs roughly 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Buckingham home has its own paved driveway, a city street permit is the exception here, not the rule — most B-section projects stage entirely on private property with no permit at all. If the only practical place turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Associations.** The original Levitt sections like Buckingham are generally organized as civic associations rather than the mandatory dues-and-architectural-control HOAs you find in Bowie's newer planned communities, so dumpster staging on your own driveway typically does not need approval. If your block does have an active association and you would like to give them a heads-up, we are happy to provide a service confirmation to forward along.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Buckingham sits at the north end of the section, up by Whitemarsh Park — the 182-acre City of Bowie park with the Bowie Playhouse and the ballfields that many of you can walk to — so we know exactly where these streets are. Most Buckingham addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run. The section is in ZIP 20715.
