Old Stage is a small suburban subdivision branching off Old Stage Road at the west end of Bowie, and it does not look like the rest of the city. While much of Bowie is built on 1960s Levitt tracts of smaller, lower-priced midcentury houses, Old Stage came up later — predominantly single-family homes raised from the 1980s through the 2000s, with prominent split-level and Colonial Revival styles and most carrying attached garages. These are larger homes on generous lots, pricing in roughly the $500,000 to $650,000 range depending on square footage. That newer, bigger-home character changes the kind of work that comes up here, and it usually points to a 15-yard roll-off: enough container for a serious single-room project, with a footprint that still tucks onto one of these roomy driveways.

## Why the 15-yard fits an Old Stage home

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds about seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material — **priced by what you load into it, so call for a quote** (aggregates are only accepted in the 10, 15, and 20). That quote **includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance**, with pickup on request. Keep the box past 14 days and it is just $5 a day extra, and if you go over the tonnage, additional weight runs $105 per ton.

We call this the "single-room reno" size, and it is the one we drop most often on a mature home like the ones out here. The 15 is the in-between container — bigger than a 10-yard quick cleanup, but still short and low enough to stay driveway-friendly. That balance matters in Old Stage. These split-levels and Colonials are now 20 to 45 years old and squarely in renovation territory, but they are also big enough that a single project — one kitchen, one roof, a pair of baths — is a real container's worth of debris on its own. The 15 swallows that defined scope without making you pay for a 20- or 30-yard box you would only half-fill on a one-room job.

If you are gutting an entire 1980s split-level to the studs at once, or clearing a full estate turnover, you may want to step up to a 20. But for a single contained project in an Old Stage home, the 15 is the right call far more often than not.

## Which Old Stage projects suit the 15-yard

Because the housing stock here dates to the 1980s through the 2000s rather than the 1960s, the work tends to be first major modernization and turnover — original-build kitchens and baths reaching the end of their service life, and roofs hitting replacement age. A 15-yard is sized right for the projects that hit one part of one of these larger homes at a time:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the original cabinets, counters, flooring, and a wall of dated drywall from a roomy split-level or Colonial kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — without paying for space you will not use on a single-room job.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off over the modest span of one of these detached homes generally sits inside the 1.5-ton allowance. For a steep multi-story Colonial with multiple layers to strip, tell us before we drop so we can size the tonnage honestly.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, vanities, tubs, and subfloor from a pair of bathrooms in a multi-bedroom home is a classic 15-yard job — and common in an Old Stage house going through its first real renovation round.
- **Basement cleanout.** Split-levels and Colonials of this era come with real lower-level square footage. Clearing decades of accumulation, old paneling, and carpet from a finished or unfinished basement lands neatly in a 15, especially as a long-held home changes hands.

If you are not sure whether your project is a 15 or a 20, tell us the room and the scope. We have hauled enough of these mature-home remodels around Bowie to point you the right way before you commit.

## Placement, lots, and HOA coordination

**Roomy driveways and frontage are the advantage here.** This is what sets Old Stage apart from Bowie's denser, narrower subdivisions. The Old Stage Road corridor includes large residential parcels — some listings cite lots near 100 by 125 feet, and others run to multiple acres — and most homes carry attached garages and generous frontage. That tends to mean there is genuine room to set a 16-foot container on your own property, on the driveway or an approved staging spot, rather than having to put it in the street. The 15-yard's compact footprint makes that even easier.

**Coordinate with the Olde Stage HOA first.** The neighborhood is governed by the Olde Stage Homeowners Association, which sets the bylaws and handles community maintenance. If your placement is on a clear private driveway, that is usually straightforward — but staging in a common area or any non-driveway spot may need HOA approval. In practice, out here the association is the real placement gatekeeper, not the city. Confirm where the HOA is comfortable with the box sitting, and we will work around it — give us the location and any constraints, and we will square the container to fit and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street or sidewalk; the Public Works Department handles that review. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not require it. Given Old Stage's generous lots, most drops here land on the property and never touch that rule. If the only workable spot truly puts the container in a city street, we will flag the right-of-way permit the moment we look at your address so there are no surprises.

**A note on your ZIP.** Public listings consistently place Old Stage and Old Stage Road in ZIP code 20720 — the Olde Stage HOA itself lists a 20720 address — rather than the 20715 you may see elsewhere. It makes no difference to our trucks, but if you are filling out paperwork, 20720 is the one the records use.
