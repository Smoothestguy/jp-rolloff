That makes these detached single-family homes about 60 years old in 2026 — and that age is what drives most of the dumpster work we do here. ## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 20-yard for construction debris **starts at $620**, with other materials priced separately (aggregates like concrete, dirt, and brick are available only in the 10, 15, and 20-yard sizes). That price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance. Keep the box longer at $5 per extra day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

Those five feet of wall height are the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes here: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret. Folks even nickname it the "whole-house cleanout" size, which tells you exactly where it shines.

## Why the 20-yard fits Rockledge specifically

Rockledge is a uniform tract of 1960s Levitt detached single-family homes, and the work that fills a dumpster here is almost never new construction — it is the modernization of an aging house. These homes were built with practical mid-century layouts and features like natural gas heat, copper plumbing, and central air. Six decades on, their original 1960s finishes, kitchens, and baths are exactly what comes due, which makes kitchen and bath remodels, basement finishing, and full-house updates the typical project on these streets. A 20-yard is built for precisely that scale of debris.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again in Rockledge:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's best-known role. Clearing an original Rockledge Levitt home between owners, or after years of accumulation, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** Tearing out original 1960s kitchen cabinetry, bathroom fixtures, and finishes across a couple of rooms at once is the classic 20-yard job here. The drywall, plaster, cabinet, and tile debris from a multi-room gut on a detached Levitt home is exactly the volume a 20 was built for.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Rockledge has held many of the same families since the homes were new. When one of those longtime houses changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these homes fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most jobs of that footprint.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are gutting the house to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and getting the truck in

**Driveway placement is the key decision here, and Rockledge makes it easy.** Unlike Bowie's newer townhome rows or gated communities, this is a section of detached single-family homes that each have their own private driveway and no HOA gatekeeping. That means nearly every dumpster here can be dropped on the homeowner's own driveway. A 20-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most of these driveways take it with room to spare. When you call, tell us where you picture it sitting and we will confirm it fits before we roll. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule works in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and approved private staging do not. Because nearly every Rockledge home has its own driveway, a street permit is the exception here, not the rule — most projects stage entirely on private property with no permit at all. That is the practical advantage of an established detached-home tract: you almost always avoid the permit entirely. If the only workable spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Associations.** The original Levitt sections like Rockledge are not the deed-restricted, dues-collecting HOAs found in Bowie's newer subdivisions, and we have found no gate or HOA staging approval to clear in the R-section. Placement is governed mainly by the Bowie right-of-way rule plus neighborly courtesy. If your block has an active civic association and you would like to give them a heads-up, we are happy to provide a service confirmation to forward along.

**Getting the truck in.** Rockledge's streets all begin with R — Rockledge Drive is the spine, with Reston Lane running through the section near the 8.5-acre Rockledge Park at 4910 Reston Lane. These are residential streets and loops, not wide arterials, so we route the truck through the neighborhood and place the box carefully. The section sits in a semi-established, recreation-adjacent setting near the WB&A Railroad Trail and the Bowie Golf Club, with Rockledge Elementary serving the area. For our records, the verifiable Rockledge addresses fall in ZIP 20715.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning. Most Rockledge addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run.
