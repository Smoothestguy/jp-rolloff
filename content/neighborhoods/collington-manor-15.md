## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material — the 15-yard is **priced by material, so call for a quote** (aggregates are accepted only in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard containers). Every rental includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance. Keep it past two weeks and it is just $5 a day extra; go over the tonnage and additional weight is $105 per ton. Pickup is on request, so call to schedule when you are done.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a finished basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job start to end. That is why the 15 is known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

## Which Collington Manor jobs suit the 15-yard

Collington Manor's housing went up across the 1990s and early 2000s, which means most homes here crossed the 25- to 35-year mark together — the age when original systems start coming apart one project at a time. That one-room, one-system pace is exactly what the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original 1990s or early-2000s kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is one of the most common reasons a 15 lands on a Collington Manor driveway.
- **Roofing tear-off.** Homes built in this window are hitting the age where the first roof needs replacing. A single-layer asphalt tear-off fits comfortably, and the 1.5-ton allowance covers most of these footprints. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop the container.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and dated plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 25- to 35-year-old build, that often means original fixtures and wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** Many of these detached homes have lower levels that are now due for finishing, re-finishing, or a long-overdue clearing-out. The 15 works well here because the low walls make loading from a stairwell easier, and it is sized right for the debris from one level rather than a whole house.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house interior gut, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of the house — that may be a 20 or larger instead. Collington Manor is a predominantly owner-occupied community of sizable homes, so we do see jobs that step up a size. We will tell you straight which container your job needs rather than upsell you into one you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and the Collington Manor HOA

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here, and unusually so.** Collington Manor is set apart from much of Bowie by its lot sizes — several area listings sit on roughly one to two-plus acres, with the larger detached homes fronted by long private driveways. For a one-room reno, that is exactly what you want: room to stage a 15-yard entirely on your own property. The container needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most of these driveways take that comfortably. Because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size most likely to keep your whole project off the street. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because so many Collington Manor homes have long private driveways, a city street permit is the exception rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops here never touch the right-of-way at all. The townhomes are the more likely exception: their shorter, sometimes shared drives can push a container toward the street (where the permit applies) or onto common ground (where the HOA comes in). Either way, we flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Clear it with the association first.** Collington Manor has a real, registered homeowners association — Collington Manor Homeowners Association, Inc. — so exterior projects and dumpster staging here can fall under HOA architectural and approval rules on top of the city's. Driveway placement on your own lot is usually straightforward, but if a container needs to stage on shared or common area, that should be cleared with the association first. The City of Bowie publishes an official Homeowners & Civic Associations directory if you need to confirm your association's current contact, and we are glad to provide a service confirmation to forward along if they ask for one.
