## What a 25-yard holds, and why it is the big one

The 25-yard measures 18 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall — roughly 10.5 pickup-truck loads — and comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance built in. In Bowie a 25-yard **includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with that 2-ton allowance — priced by material, so call us for a quote.** Extra days are $5/day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — just call us when you are ready and we will schedule it.

This is the size people in the trade call "major construction," and the name is honest. The walls are six feet, so most crews will want a ladder or a wheelbarrow ramp to load the upper reaches, but there is real volume behind those walls — enough to keep one container on site through a job that would otherwise need a mid-project swap. That is exactly when the 25 earns its keep: longer projects, bigger builds, and the kind of all-at-once tear-out a 20-yard would outgrow.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size, and it matters more in Somerset than almost anywhere else in Bowie: **measure twice before booking.** Eighteen feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. Somerset's homes are original 1960s Levitt Cape Cods and ranchers — modest single-family houses on standard mid-century lots with single-width driveways. That is a tighter staging area than the wide drives in Bowie's newer sections. We would rather talk through placement on the phone than find out on delivery day that the spot is short.

## Which Somerset jobs actually call for a 25-yard

Be honest with yourself about the project. Most work on a 65-year-old Levitt Cape Cod — a kitchen or bath remodel, a basement finish, an estate cleanout of a long-held home — produces a debris stream that fits a 10-, 15-, or 20-yard container, and we will steer you there to save you money. The 25-yard is for the genuinely large jobs:

- **Home additions.** When you bump out the back of a Somerset rancher or push a Cape Cod's footprint outward, you generate framing, old siding, sheathing, and tear-out in the volume the 25 is built to hold. On a 1960s slab- or crawl-era house, an addition usually means opening up original structure, and that adds up fast.
- **Whole-house remodels.** Taking an original Levitt down to the studs — kitchen, baths, flooring, plaster and drywall, dated fixtures all at once — is a classic 25-yard job. A smaller box here means stopping work to swap containers mid-gut.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** A full roof replacement on these homes, especially a multi-layer tear-off built up over six decades, runs heavy. The 2-ton allowance gives more headroom for shingle weight than the smaller sizes do.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** If you are clearing out a larger space and the material comes fast and in quantity, the 25 keeps pace where a smaller container would fill before the day is done.

If your Somerset project is a single room, a routine declutter, or a modest estate cleanout, tell us — a 15- or 20-yard is almost always the smarter spend, and we will say so plainly. The 25 is the right answer when the job is truly big.

## Placement, the Bowie permit rule, and getting there

**Driveway placement is the plan.** Somerset's homes sit on standard single-width driveways, so we scout the spot with you and set the container square on the drive. On a 65-year-old property the driveway surface deserves respect, and we will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect it. Because the 25 is our longest box, we will also confirm there is room for the truck to position and the rear door to swing before we commit to a delivery window.

**The permit rule is simple.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street. A container placed on your private driveway or on HOA-approved staging does **not** need one. On Somerset's compact lots the driveway is almost always the workable spot anyway, so most jobs never touch the permit question. If your address is one where the street is the only option, we will flag that up front rather than let it surprise you on delivery day.

**Drive time works in your favor.** Somerset is about 25 minutes from our Beltsville yard by way of Route 197 or the BW Parkway, and we dispatch every morning, so most requests are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day delivery. When you are running an addition or a gut on a deadline, a hauler that is dispatched fresh each morning and knows the S-street layout keeps the job moving.
