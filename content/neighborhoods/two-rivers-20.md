## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material, so the cost depends on what you are throwing away — call for a quote. Aggregates are only accepted in the 10, 15, and 20-yard sizes. Every drop includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance. Extra days run $5/day, and weight beyond the 2-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton.

Those four feet of wall height are the part people tend to underestimate. The wall is low enough to walk debris up and pitch it over the side, but the long footprint is tall enough to swallow the volume of a real project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for ground-up construction or full structural demolition. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret. It has earned the nickname the "whole-house cleanout" size for good reason.

## Placement, permits, and the Two Rivers HOA

**The HOA is the variable that actually matters here.** Two Rivers is a master-planned, association-governed community, with active-adult Regency sections where the HOA even handles lawn care and snow removal. That means dumpster staging can fall under HOA placement and approval rules, and it is worth a quick check with the association before delivery day rather than after the container is already on the ground. We can provide a service confirmation to forward along if they ask for one.

**Driveways take the container; villa lots are the tighter case.** A 20-yard needs about 22 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door. The single-family homes in sections like The Heritage generally have driveways that handle that with room to spare, which keeps the container on your own property. The 55-and-better villas are the case to plan around — those lots often have shorter driveways and clustered or shared layouts, so on a villa the box may need to fit a single drive lane or an HOA-approved common spot. The community is also amenity-dense and trail-laced, with shared parking around hubs like the Founders Club clubhouse, so some interior streets run tight for a large truck. Tell us up front which village and street you are on and we will plan the drop accordingly. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**About permits — the Bowie rule does not apply here.** This is the one place the Bowie-versus-Odenton distinction has real teeth. Because Two Rivers is in Odenton and Anne Arundel County, not inside the City of Bowie, the City of Bowie's right-of-way permit rule does not govern your address. If a container needs to sit in a public street rather than on your driveway or an HOA-approved spot, the relevant rules are Anne Arundel County's, not Bowie's. The good news is that street placement is usually the exception in Two Rivers — most single-family drops here sit on your own driveway and never touch the right-of-way. If the only workable spot turns out to be the street, we will flag it the moment we look at your address and help you confirm the correct county requirement, rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Drive time and getting around.** We dispatch every morning from Beltsville, roughly half an hour out by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Most addresses here are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run. Two Rivers has more than seven miles of trails and a large community park woven through it, and the named villages cluster around shared amenity hubs, so giving our driver the exact village, street, and house number keeps a delivery clean and gets the container exactly where you want it.
