## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and our typical pricing runs $595 to $725 — a number that already includes delivery, pickup, and the included tonnage, so there are no surprise line items waiting at the end.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roofing tear-off, lath and plaster, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why Buckingham jobs reach this size

Buckingham is one of the original sections of the Levitt-built Belair at Bowie development, put up in overlapping stages roughly between 1960 and 1964 alongside Somerset and Kenilworth. That makes it some of the oldest housing stock in Bowie — homes now 60-plus years old, sold back then in a handful of Levitt models (the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the three- and four-bedroom Colonials, and the Country Clubber). After six decades, those original builds are dated by today's standards, and Buckingham has become a neighborhood of major renovations and gut jobs. That is the work the 30-yard is made for:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting a Levitt home down to the studs — pulling the original kitchen, taking out interior walls, clearing a finished basement, or removing a long-ago addition — produces far more debris than most owners expect. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs and tenant turnovers throw off a continuous stream of fixtures, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** Whole-house clear-outs, estate cleanups, and decades of accumulated material in a long-held family home pile up fast. Nine truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** Turning over several units at once — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and tenant debris together — is exactly the volume the 30-yard was built to handle.

For a single-room remodel or a routine garage cleanout in Buckingham, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Placement on Buckingham's driveways

Here is where Buckingham is genuinely easy compared with a lot of DMV neighborhoods. Every Belair home, Buckingham's included, was delivered with a paved driveway and a garage as standard features — the Rancher came with a one-car garage, the four-bedroom Colonial with an oversized two-car. The lots were laid out at a uniform roughly 8,400 square feet to keep pricing fair across the section. The practical upshot for a roll-off: those yards are modest, but the driveways are real, so we can almost always stage a 30-yard on your own driveway rather than out in the street.

That matters because of how the city handles permits. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Buckingham is one of the older Levitt sections rather than a modern dues-and-architectural-control subdivision, staging on your private driveway typically does not require an association sign-off either, and a street permit ends up being the exception rather than the rule. If your only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the city's Public Works Department handles that right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.

A 30-yard does ask for a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes — it is a big box on a long frame, and the driver needs room to back the trailer in straight. On a uniform Buckingham lot that usually works fine, but it is worth a look before we arrive. And because every street in the section starts with the letter B — Buckingham Drive, Bradford Lane, Brewster Lane, Briercrest Road, Brunswick Lane — telling us your exact street helps us route the truck and trailer cleanly the first time. If the approach is tight, sloped, or shares a curb cut with a neighbor, mention it when you book and we will plan the drop around it.
