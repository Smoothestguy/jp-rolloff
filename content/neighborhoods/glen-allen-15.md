## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 15-yard for construction debris is **priced by material, so call for a quote (other materials priced separately, and aggregates load only in the 10-, 15-, or 20-yard)**. That price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance. Extra days run $5/day, and any weight beyond the 1.5-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a finished basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job start to end. That is why the 15 is known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

## Which Glen Allen jobs suit the 15-yard

Glen Allen's homes went up across the 1990s into the early 2000s, so most crossed the 25- to 35-year mark together — the age when original systems start coming apart one project at a time. These are larger move-up homes than Bowie's original Levitt sections, but the typical project here is still a single-room or single-system job, and that pace is exactly what the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original 1990s or early-2000s kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is one of the most common reasons a 15 lands on a Glen Allen driveway.
- **Roofing tear-off.** Homes built in this window are hitting the age where the first roof needs replacing. A single-layer asphalt tear-off fits comfortably, and the 1.5-ton allowance covers most of these footprints. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop the container.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and dated plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 25- to 35-year-old build, that often means original fixtures and wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** Many of these detached brick colonials and Craftsman homes have lower levels now due for finishing, re-finishing, or a long-overdue clearing-out. The 15 works well here because the low walls make loading from a stairwell easier, and it is sized right for the debris from one level rather than a whole house.

If your project runs bigger — a whole-house interior gut, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of one of these larger move-up homes — that may be a 20 or larger instead. Glen Allen's homes are sizable, so we do see jobs that step up a size. We will tell you straight which container your job needs rather than upsell you into one you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and the Glen Allen HOA

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here, and unusually so.** Glen Allen is laid out with numerous quiet cul-de-sacs, which means many homes sit on pipestem or court-end lots with curved frontage and limited street curb space. That layout makes private driveway placement the practical default rather than the exception — and these Drees-built move-up homes generally come with generous off-street driveways to stage on. For a one-room reno, that is exactly what you want: room to set a 15-yard entirely on your own property. The container needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most of these driveways take that comfortably. Because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size most likely to keep your whole project off the street. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites here.** The City of Bowie's Public Works Department handles permitting for any work in the public right-of-way, but a permit is required only when a dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not need one. Because Glen Allen's cul-de-sac lots steer almost every drop onto a private driveway, a city street permit is the exception rather than the rule — most 15-yard placements here never touch the right-of-way at all. On a court-end lot where curb space is tight to begin with, the driveway is usually the only sensible spot anyway. Either way, we flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Clear it with the association first.** Glen Allen is a standard suburban, non-gated subdivision, but it has its own homeowners association — listed among the City of Bowie's registered HOAs and reportedly managed by ProCom Management. That means exterior projects and dumpster staging can fall under HOA placement or screening rules on top of the city's. Driveway placement on your own lot is usually straightforward, but if a container would need to stage on shared or common ground, that should be cleared with the association first. We are glad to provide a service confirmation you can forward along if they ask for one.

**Drive time and finding the address.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Glen Allen sits along Mitchellville Road near U.S. 301, about a mile south of Bowie Town Center, with U.S. Route 50 linking the area back toward Washington — the standard haul corridor for a container coming out of Beltsville. The neighborhood borders Allen Pond Park, and Glen Allen Park sits embedded in the community with its playground and tennis courts, so those make handy orientation points for our driver. With cul-de-sac lots and curved frontage in the mix, giving us the exact street and house number keeps the delivery clean. Most jobs go out within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run.
