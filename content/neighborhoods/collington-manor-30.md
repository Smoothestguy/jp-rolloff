## Staging it on a Collington Manor driveway — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's where Collington Manor stands apart from the denser, Levitt-era tracts elsewhere in Bowie. Many of the detached homes here sit on large lots — area listings run from roughly an acre to two-plus acres — with long private driveways and plenty of room on the property itself. That's a real advantage with a container as long as the 30-yard: in most cases the can sits entirely on your own driveway, with the run-up the truck needs, and it never touches the street.

That placement matters for the permit, too. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city street. A driveway drop doesn't trigger it. So on a large-lot detached home in Collington Manor, you'll usually skip the city permit entirely — the main thing to sort out is HOA sign-off, not street access.

That HOA piece is the real variable here, and it's worth a word. Collington Manor has an active, registered homeowners' association, so exterior projects and dumpster staging can fall under architectural or approval rules — especially for the townhomes, where shorter shared drives mean a big can is more likely to need either common-area staging (which the HOA should clear) or street placement (which would need the city permit). The City of Bowie keeps an official Homeowners & Civic Associations directory you can use to confirm your association's contact before a delivery. If the street genuinely is your only option, just tell us when you call and we'll walk you through the right-of-way permit so you're covered. Either way, the prep on your end is simple: clear the run-up and the landing spot the night before, and we'll handle the rest.
