Belair Town is one of Bowie's few dense, shared-wall enclaves — roughly 300 Levitt-built townhouses raised in two phases (Belair Town I and Belair Town II) between 1968 and 1970, flanking the stores on the north side of Annapolis Road (MD 450). It was the townhouse component of Levitt & Sons' Belair at Bowie, the firm's fourth and final big postwar planned community, and that origin shows: consistent late-1960s attached construction, modest footprints, and a shared-wall layout that sets Belair Town apart from Bowie's roomier detached-home subdivisions. These homes are now more than 50 years old and squarely in renovation territory — and on a 2-3 bedroom townhouse running roughly 918 to 1,538 square feet, the 15-yard roll-off is usually the size that fits the work and the lot.

## Why the 15-yard fits a Belair Town townhouse

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds about four and a half pickup-truck loads of debris. It carries a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Belair Town rental runs **$425 to $485, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the haul.

We call this the "single-room reno" size, and it earns that name here more than almost anywhere in Bowie. The 15 is the in-between container — bigger than a 10-yard quick cleanup, but still short and low enough to stay driveway-friendly for most homes. In Belair Town, where the townhouses are modest and the parking is tight, that compact footprint is the whole point. A 30-yard box has no business sitting in front of a late-1960s shared-wall townhouse with narrow frontage; the 14-foot length of the 15 is far easier to tuck into an assigned space, a short driveway, or an HOA-approved staging spot without blocking your neighbor's wall.

So the 15 fits on two counts: it swallows a contained, one-room project in a 50-plus-year-old Levitt townhouse, and its short footprint stages where there is genuinely room to stage it out here. If you are gutting an entire unit at once or clearing a full estate turnover, you may want to step up to a 20. For a single defined project in a Belair Town home, the 15 is the one we drop most often.

## Which Belair Town projects suit the 15-yard

Belair Town II receives municipal trash, recycling, and yard-waste pickup, plus public water and sewer, so routine household garbage is already handled at the curb. What that leaves is exactly what a roll-off is for: larger renovation debris, bulk cleanouts, and construction loads that blow past what curbside service will take. And because the housing stock is original late-1960s Levitt — attached homes now past the half-century mark — the work that comes up is aging-home modernization and turnover, not new-build debris. A 15-yard is sized right for the projects that hit one part of one of these townhouses at a time:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out original cabinets, counters, flooring, and a wall of dated drywall from a Belair Town kitchen fills a 15-yard about right, without paying for space you will not use in a home of this size.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off over the modest roof span of an attached townhouse sits comfortably inside the 1.5-ton allowance — these are shared-wall rooflines, not sprawling detached spreads.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, vanities, tubs, and subfloor from a pair of bathrooms in a 2-3 bedroom townhouse is a classic 15-yard job on a home in its second or third round of renovation.
- **Basement cleanout.** Clearing decades of accumulation, old paneling, and carpet from a lower level lands neatly in a 15 — a common need as these long-held Belair Town homes change hands or get refreshed.

Because every one of these homes came out of the same Levitt catalog and shares the same modest footprint, we have a good sense of what one room's worth of demo weighs before we ever pull up. If you are not sure whether your project is a 15 or a 20, tell us the room and the scope. We have hauled enough of these Levitt remodels to point you the right way before you commit.

## Placement, parking, and HOA coordination

**Tight, assigned parking is the deciding factor here.** This is the detail that matters most in Belair Town, and it is what makes this neighborhood different from Bowie's detached-home tracts. Under the Belair Town II parking regulations, each unit is entitled to the exclusive use of just one assigned parking space, with anything beyond that limited to street parking or rear-of-unit spaces. That single-space allotment, combined with narrow late-1960s townhouse frontages, means there is no automatic place to set a 14-foot container the way there is on a quarter-acre lot. The 15-yard's short footprint helps, but placement still has to be planned — which assigned space, which stretch of street, or which approved spot near the rear or common area the box will occupy.

**Coordinate with the HOA first.** Belair Town II is governed by a Declaration of Covenants, By-Laws, and Parking Regulations administered by its homeowners association, and exterior projects and equipment placement on or near common areas should be cleared with the HOA. In practice, confirming an HOA-approved spot is usually the single thing that determines a clean Belair Town drop. We are glad to work around whatever the association approves — give us the location and any constraints, and we will square the container to fit it and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. So in Belair Town the practical question is rarely the city permit — it is the HOA spot. If your only workable placement truly puts the container in a city street rather than an approved on-property space, we will flag the right-of-way permit the moment we look at your address so there are no surprises.

**Finding your door.** The primary way in is Race Track Road off Annapolis Road (Route 450); Belair Town II's entrance sits near that intersection, and streets inside the neighborhood include Morningside Lane. That access pattern means our trucks work residential streets off Race Track Road rather than wide arterials to reach your unit, so a clear address and a known cross-street keep the delivery moving through a tight, shared-wall neighborhood.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Most Belair Town addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run.
