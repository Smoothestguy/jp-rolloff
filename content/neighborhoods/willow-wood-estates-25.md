## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 20 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly 7.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2.5-ton (5,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Willow Wood Estates a typical 25-yard rental runs **$525 to $625, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the haul.

The jump from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a second haul partway through — a whole-house remodel, a substantial addition, a large roof, or a sizable cleanout. The walls are still five feet, so a crew can walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but there is meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size containers offer.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Twenty feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. In a neighborhood of midsize homes on residential lots, that is worth confirming before delivery day rather than after — more on placement below.

## Which Willow Wood Estates jobs suit the 25-yard

Here is the honest version: in Willow Wood Estates, the 25-yard is the exception, not the rule. The housing stock here is a relatively homogeneous, mature cohort — homes built in a tight 1977-to-1983 window, now 40-plus years old and squarely in the renovation-and-replacement cycle. Most of that work — kitchen and bath gut-remodels, deck rebuilds, basement finishes, HVAC and water-heater swaps — fills a 15- or 20-yard roll-off, not our largest box. So before you book this size, it is worth asking whether the job is genuinely big enough to earn it. These are the ones that are:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out the back of a 1970s detached home or putting on a second-story or in-law addition throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a mid-size box can take without a mid-project swap.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full gut of one of these 40-plus-year-old homes — kitchen, baths, flooring, and drywall all coming out at once — is a textbook 25-yard job. When a midsize home gets modernized down to the studs, the debris adds up fast, and one large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** Homes built in the late 1970s and early 1980s are well into the original-roof replacement window. On the bigger floor plans in the neighborhood — the four- and five-bedroom homes pushing toward 2,790 square feet — a full or multi-layer tear-off runs heavy, and the 2.5-ton allowance gives more headroom for shingle weight than the smaller sizes.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** When a larger property or a business clearout in the broader Bowie area throws off material in bulk, the 25-yard handles it in one go.

If your Willow Wood project is a single-room redo, a bathroom refresh, a routine garage or basement cleanout, or a standard-sized roof, a 20-yard or even a 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so on the phone. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely major.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the norm here.** Willow Wood Estates is a subdivision of owner-occupied, single-family detached homes, and a uniform-age neighborhood like this tends to renovate in waves — neighbors replacing roofs or finishing basements around the same lifecycle. The practical upshot is that nearly every home has its own driveway, and the driveway is almost always the right place to stage a 25-yard. We will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface. Because this is our longest container, take a minute to confirm the driveway run is clear of low branches, gates, and tight turns before delivery.

**The permit rule is in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster has to sit in a city street. A container placed entirely on your private driveway — or on HOA-approved staging — does **not** need one. Given that these are detached homes with their own driveways, the large majority of Willow Wood jobs stage on-property with no permit at all. If your particular address or a long-driveway situation forces the container into the street, we will flag that up front; the City of Bowie's Public Works Department on Annapolis Road handles street-placement permits, and we will point you to it rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.

**Drive time.** From our Beltsville yard, Willow Wood Estates is about a 25-minute run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, and same-day delivery is common — so when you are running an addition or a whole-house gut on a deadline, the container shows up when the crew needs it.
