## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: construction debris in the 15-yard is **priced by what you load** (other materials are priced separately, and aggregates are accepted only in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard), so call for a quote. That quote **includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance**. Keep it longer for $5 a day, and any weight past the 1.5-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — give us a call to schedule it.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a basement. But there is still enough volume to hold a real single-room job start to finish. That is why the 15 is best known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

## Which Victoria Heights jobs suit the 15-yard

Because Victoria Heights came later in the Belair build-out, it skipped the smaller entry-level Cape Cods that fill the earlier sections and got the bigger detached models instead — the larger colonials and the top-of-line Country Clubber, "a luxurious house reflecting the best of American country colonial design." Six decades on, those larger 1960s homes are prime candidates for the one-room, one-system projects the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodels.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original Levitt kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is the single most common reason a 15 lands on a V-section driveway.
- **Roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these detached homes fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most modest roofs. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 55-to-60-year-old Levitt build, that often means dated fixtures and older substrate coming out together.
- **Basement cleanouts.** Clearing a finished or half-finished basement — or hauling the debris from finishing one — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles a move-out or downsizing cleanout when a longtime family home changes hands.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house gut between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of one of the larger Country Clubbers — that may be a 20 or a 30 instead. The bigger detached models in these later sections can generate real demolition volume, and we will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the easy part here.** Victoria Heights is part of the "V section," and Bowie's Levitt neighborhoods follow a signature alliterative naming system where every street in a section shares the section's first letter. The main spine, Victoria Heights Drive, is unambiguously V-section — for us that means an address on this street is easy to place and easy to route to. The lots themselves help, too: standard Levitt Belair lots run about 8,400 square feet, roughly a quarter acre, uniformly sized to keep pricing fair, with corner lots as premiums. The yards are modest but the driveways are real, and since these are the larger detached models, most homes have a driveway and side yard that take a roll-off without entering the street. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Victoria Heights driveways handle that comfortably. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Victoria Heights.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every quarter-acre V-section home has its own driveway, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**A note on associations.** The original Levitt detached sections of Belair — which include Victoria Heights — were built as conventional single-family subdivisions and are widely treated as "no HOA" neighborhoods, generally covered by voluntary civic associations rather than the mandatory dues-and-architectural-control HOAs of Bowie's newer subdivisions. So staging a dumpster on your private driveway typically does not require board sign-off. We say "typically" on purpose — if your block does run a formal association with placement rules, give them a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along. The City of Bowie keeps an official association directory if you ever need to confirm your street.
