## Why the 20-yard is the right call in Victoria Heights

Victoria Heights is one of the last detached, full-size sections of Levitt & Sons' Belair at Bowie development, built in the mid-to-late 1960s as the final wave before Levitt shifted toward townhomes. That timing matters more than it sounds. While the early Belair sections were filled with smaller starter Cape Cods, Victoria Heights got the larger 1960s models — ramblers, Colonials, split-levels, and the big Country Clubber. These are full-size houses, and they're now 55 to 60 years old, which means a lot of them are hitting their first or second major renovation cycle all at once.

That's exactly the kind of work the 20-yard handles best. It's our "whole-house cleanout" size: roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 feet tall, holding about nine pickup-truck loads of debris. For a single bedroom or a small bathroom, it would be overkill. For a true gut job spread across several rooms, or the contents of an entire house, it's the sweet spot — big enough that you're not calling for a swap halfway through, but not so big that it dominates the property or your budget.

## Jobs this size is built for

When folks in Victoria Heights book a 20-yard from us, it's usually one of a few projects:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** Years of accumulation in the basement, the attic, the garage, and the spare bedrooms all at once. The 20-yard swallows it in a single haul.
- **Multi-room remodels.** Pulling cabinets, flooring, drywall, and fixtures out of a kitchen plus a bath or two. The mixed debris from a couple of rooms adds up faster than people expect, and this is the size that keeps up.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When a longtime family home changes hands, decades of belongings have to go. These are some of the most common calls we get out of the older Levitt sections, and we try to handle them with the patience the situation deserves.
- **Mid-size roofing.** A tear-off on one of these 1960s rooflines, where you need the weight capacity and the volume but aren't dealing with a sprawling new-construction footprint.

If your project is really only one room, ask us about the 10-yard instead — no sense paying for space you won't fill. And if you're taking a Country Clubber or a larger split-level all the way down to the studs across the whole house, the 30-yard might serve you better. We'll tell you honestly which way to lean; we'd rather get you the right box once than rent you the wrong one twice.

## It fits the driveway — and that usually means no permit

One of the quiet advantages of Victoria Heights is the lot. Standard Levitt Belair lots run about 8,400 square feet, roughly a quarter acre, and they were laid out uniformly across the development. That gives most homes here a real driveway and a side yard with room to spare — typically a two-car driveway that takes a 20-yard container with space left over.

That matters for more than convenience. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city street. Place it on your own driveway or an approved staging spot on your property, and you generally skip the permit process entirely. Because Victoria Heights homes were built as conventional single-family houses on those quarter-acre lots, driveway placement is the norm here, and most of our customers never have to deal with the city at all.

A note on associations: the original detached Levitt sections like this one are widely known as no-HOA neighborhoods, and the associations covering them tend to be voluntary civic groups rather than mandatory HOAs with architectural-control rules. That said, streets vary, so if you're unsure whether your block has any say over placement, it's worth a quick check — the City of Bowie keeps an official homeowners and civic association directory. When in doubt, a call to your association and a few minutes of planning save everyone a headache.
