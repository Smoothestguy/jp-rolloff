## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to when a job throws off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and our typical Overbrook pricing runs $595 to $725 — and that number already includes delivery, pickup, and the included tonnage, so there is no surprise line item waiting at the end.

One honest note on weight before you book. The 30-yard's volume fills up best with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, roof tear-off, worn furniture, and general construction debris. If your load runs heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly while the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. ## Placement and permits in Overbrook

Here is where Overbrook's particular character matters most. These are mature Levitt lots on narrow, tree-shaded residential streets, so the home's own driveway is almost always the right staging spot — and the good news is that Levitt sold these houses with driveways included, so the paved space is there to use. Putting the box on the driveway also keeps it clear of the tight O-named roadways, where a long container parked at the curb would crowd a street that was never built wide.

Staging the 30-yard on your own driveway keeps the container off the city streets entirely, and that is what keeps the paperwork simple: **the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not.** On these established Overbrook lots, putting the box on the driveway is almost always the move, so a street permit is the exception rather than the rule.

The one thing to plan for is the box itself. The 30-yard is a long container on a long frame, and it needs a longer, clearer run-up to place than the smaller sizes — which is exactly the trade-off to weigh on Overbrook's mature lots and narrow streets. A flat, open driveway with room to back the trailer in straight is ideal; if the drive is short, sloped, lined with low tree limbs, or crowded, tell us when you book and we will plan the approach or talk through whether a 25-yard would seat more easily. If the only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the City of Bowie's permitting office handles right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through it before the container shows up. It is also worth confirming any HOA or civic-association placement rules alongside the city's street-permit rule.
