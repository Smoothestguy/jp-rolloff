The 10-yard is the smallest footprint we offer, measuring 12 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall. It holds roughly three pickup-truck loads of debris and comes with a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance. Pricing is per material: in Idlewild a 10-yard is **priced by what you're loading, so call for a quote — it includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with that 1-ton allowance**. Extra days are +$5/day, and any weight beyond the 1-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule.

The low 4-foot walls are the quiet advantage. The 1-ton allowance is sized for exactly that kind of work — a single-room remodel or a focused cleanout, not a whole-house gut. And because the container fits in a single car spot, it tucks neatly onto an Idlewild driveway without swallowing the whole thing. If your project turns out bigger than one room, we will point you to a 15 or 20-yard instead — but for the jobs below, the 10 is the right call.

## Which Idlewild jobs suit the 10-yard

This is the size folks call the "garage cleanout," and around Idlewild that is the call we field most. The section was built from a small set of repeating Levitt models — the Cape Cod (later the Ardsley), the Rancher (the Buckingham), the Colonials (Cambridge and Gladstone), the Country Clubber (Framingham), and the Manor House — and the plans selling in 1968 were essentially the same ones launched in 1960. Because so many homes share a handful of floor plans, the projects here tend to be predictable in scope. Here are the four that land squarely in a 10-yard on these I-section streets:

- **Garage cleanouts.** Every Belair home, Idlewild included, was delivered with a garage and a private paved driveway as standard. After about 60 years, those garages fill up with paint cans, old lumber, broken tools, and boxes. A single bay clears into a 10-yard without overpaying for the space, and you can stage the container right on the driveway a few steps from the door.
- **Bathroom remodels.** A single-bath gut in an original Levitt home — the dated tile, the tub, vanity, drywall, and subfloor — is the textbook 10-yard job. Because the I-section runs on a handful of repeating models, these baths are remarkably similar from house to house, and that dense tile and plaster stays well inside the 1-ton allowance for one bathroom. Modernizing those original baths is one of the most common projects on the street.
- **Yard waste and trees.** Idlewild sits in northeastern Bowie near the 182-acre Whitemarsh Park, and these mature Levitt-era lots have had six decades to grow in. Brush, branches, sod, and the occasional removed tree load neatly over the low walls of the 10. Green debris is heavy once it is wet, so the 1-ton allowance is sized for a single yard's worth — not a whole-block clearing.
- **Estate downsize.** With a section this old, plenty of longtime family homes on Idlewild Drive are reaching the point of changing hands. A 10-yard handles the furniture, household goods, and accumulated clutter of a focused downsize — a measured cleanout, not a full whole-house clear-out.

If you are gut-renovating a dated Levitt kitchen, finishing a basement, tearing off a roof, or doing a full-home modernization all at once, those run heavier and bulkier than a 10 is built for, and we will tell you so up front. The 10-yard earns its keep on the one-room, one-corner, one-weekend jobs.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the default in Idlewild, and the neighborhood is built for it.** Every Belair home came with its own paved driveway, so the container almost always stages on the driveway rather than the lawn. Idlewild Drive alone carries roughly 90-plus addresses on uniform Levitt-era lots — the kind of mature, fully-built-out suburban lots where a 10-yard fits comfortably alongside the cars. A 10-yard needs about 12 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and a clear swing for the rear door, and that fits the standard Idlewild driveway easily. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The City of Bowie permit rule.** Idlewild is inside the City of Bowie, and the city's rule is straightforward: a right-of-way permit is required **only** if the dumpster sits in a city street. The city code prohibits obstructing streets, sidewalks, or alleys without permission and requires amber lanterns and a permit for materials stored in a public street — but a container placed on your private driveway triggers none of that. Because nearly every Idlewild home has that included Levitt driveway, the vast majority of our drops here never touch the right-of-way and never need a permit. The original 1960s Levitt sections also generally do not carry a mandatory homeowners association — listings in these Belair sections regularly advertise "No HOA" — so unlike newer Bowie subdivisions, there is usually no design-review or staging-approval step before you place a dumpster on your own driveway. If your particular address has its own arrangement, it is always worth a quick check, but for most of Idlewild the driveway is yours to use.

**Drive time.** Idlewild is about 25 minutes from our Beltsville yard, an easy run up Route 197 — Collington Road — or the BW Parkway, with quick access to US Route 50. Route 197 and Belair Drive are the practical haul routes right into the section. We dispatch every morning, so most addresses are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run. You are close enough that getting a truck to an I-section address is never the holdup.
