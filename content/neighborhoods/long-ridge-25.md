## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard measures 18 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly ten-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material — priced by what you are throwing away, so call for a quote (other materials priced separately). Every rental includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton allowance. Keep it past 14 days for $5 a day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance runs $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — give us a call to schedule it.

The full six-foot wall height is what makes this size a true major-construction bin. Those tall walls let you stack bulky framing, demolition debris, and the volume of a whole-house job deep into the container, and the 2-ton allowance gives real headroom for the mixed, heavy loads that come off that kind of work — anything over that runs $105 per additional ton. The trade-off is the height itself: at six feet, the walls are tall enough that you may want to load from the rear door rather than heave everything over the side. Long Ridge's bigger lots give you plenty of room to stage it, but a 25 is still a serious container — if your job is smaller than you think, tell us and we will talk through whether a 20 fits the same work.

To be honest about where the line falls: if you are taking apart one kitchen or a pair of baths, this is too much container and you will pay for air. Step down to a 15. The 25 earns its keep when the scope is genuinely large — when the debris is going to keep coming for days or weeks.

## Which Long Ridge jobs suit the 25-yard

The Country Clubber is the largest of all the Bowie Levitt homes — a two-story Colonial that later came in five-bedroom configurations and originally topped out around $24,900 as the premium model. Now roughly 60 years old, these uniformly large homes drive exactly the kind of full-scale projects the 25-yard is meant for:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out the back, expanding the kitchen footprint, or adding a primary suite to a Country Clubber generates demolition debris and new-construction offcuts together — framing, sheathing, old siding, drywall, and packaging. A project of that size keeps debris flowing for weeks, and the 25 lets one container stay on site through the whole build instead of swapping bins.
- **Whole-house remodels.** Gutting a Country Clubber down to the studs is common here. These are big two-story Colonials, and stripping cabinets, flooring, plaster and drywall, fixtures, and trim across two full floors produces far more debris than any single-room size can hold. The 25's volume and 2-ton allowance are built for the mixed-material weight of a full gut, with extra weight billed at $105 per ton.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** The Country Clubber was built with a flat, low-slope rear dormer originally finished with roll roofing — a section that wears out faster than the main pitched roof. On 60-year-old homes, a full tear-off that replaces both the failure-prone low-slope dormer roofing and the entire main roof is a heavy job. Shingles and roll roofing carry serious weight, so we will go over the total square footage before we drop the bin so you know up front whether a big tear-off is likely to run past the 2-ton allowance, at which point the extra weight is $105 per additional ton.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** For larger-scale clearouts — an estate, a multi-unit turnover, or a contractor staging a big job — the 25 handles the volume that smaller residential bins cannot.

One classic Long Ridge job lands here when it goes big: the **attic conversion.** Country Clubbers were built with a distinctive expandable, unfinished attic, and owners here routinely turn it into a bedroom, office, or beamed loft. A modest conversion fits a 15 or 20, but when the work opens up the whole top floor — full framing, insulation, and drywall across the expandable attic plus connected work below — the 25 keeps it to one container. Tell us the real scope and we will size it honestly rather than push you into a bin you cannot fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Placement is where the 25 needs a little planning.** Long Ridge is an "L" section — under Belair's alliterative convention, every street name begins with the same letter as the section, so the L-streets are how locals navigate this corner of Bowie. Long Ridge was built with larger Country Clubbers on slightly larger lots than the earlier Levitt sections, and it backs up toward Collington Road as one of the fully completed sections that expanded the community eastward. Those bigger lots and wider frontages are a genuine advantage for a container this size: most properties have a driveway with room to take this bin on private ground. We need about eighteen feet of straight length plus clearance for the truck and an open swing for the rear door, so an open driveway is ideal. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Long Ridge.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Long Ridge homes sit on those slightly larger lots with real driveways, even a big 25-yard drop usually stays on private property and never touches the right-of-way. If the only workable spot turns out to be the street, that permit runs through the City of Bowie Department of Public Works on Annapolis Road, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We know the run into the L-streets well, most jobs are scheduled within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run.
