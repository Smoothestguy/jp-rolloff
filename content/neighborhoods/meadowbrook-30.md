## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to when a job throws off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. It comes with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance, and our typical Meadowbrook pricing runs $595 to $725 — and that number already includes delivery, pickup, and the included tonnage, so there is no surprise line item waiting at the end.

One honest note on weight before you book. The 30-yard's volume fills up best with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, cabinetry, roof tear-off, old furniture, and general construction debris. If your load runs heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly while the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. Tell us what is going in when you call and we will steer you to the right size rather than just the biggest one.

## Placement and permits in Meadowbrook

Here is where Meadowbrook's particular character matters most. These are standardized Levitt lots — most of Belair/Bowie was platted at about 8,400 square feet, just under a quarter-acre — so Meadowbrook yards and frontages are compact. The front lawns are small and the streets are tree-lined and tight. That makes the home's own driveway the right staging spot almost every time, and the good news is that Levitt sold these houses complete with garages and driveways included, so the paved space is there to use.

Staging the 30-yard on your own driveway keeps the container off the M-named city streets entirely, and that is what keeps the paperwork simple: **the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not.** On a sub-quarter-acre Meadowbrook lot, putting the box on the driveway is almost always the move, so a street permit is the exception rather than the rule.

The one thing to plan for is the box itself. The 30-yard is a long container on a long frame, and it needs a longer, clearer run-up to place than the smaller sizes — which is exactly the trade-off to weigh on these tighter Meadowbrook lots. A flat, open driveway with room to back the trailer in straight is ideal; if the drive is short, sloped, or crowded, tell us when you book and we will plan the approach or talk through whether a 25-yard would seat more easily. If the only workable spot turns out to be the public street, the City of Bowie's Public Works / Engineering Review & Permitting office handles right-of-way review, and we are glad to walk you through it before the container shows up. Some Bowie sections also fall under civic or homeowners associations, so it is worth confirming any local placement rules alongside the city's street-permit rule.
