The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Extra time runs +$5/day, and any weight over the 1.5-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule it.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. ## Which Glenridge jobs suit the 15-yard

Glenridge is a small, late-phase Levitt section, and that drives a very particular kind of demand. Tax records list the ten homes on Galaxy Lane (11900–11913 Galaxy Ln, 20715) all built in 1969, with living areas running from about 1,583 to 2,063 square feet — modest-to-mid-size colonials, including the multi-level "split colonial" variants you see described on Galaxy Lane and the adjoining Gradys Walk. After more than half a century, those original kitchens, baths, and roofs are all coming due, and that lands right in the heart of what the 15-yard handles best:

On a home that has carried the same kitchen for 55-plus years, this is the single most common reason a 15 lands on a Glenridge driveway.

Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it — especially on homes that have picked up a rear addition over the years, which adds roof area.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 1969 build, that often means dated fixtures and older finishes coming out together.
- **Basement and addition cleanouts.** Clearing a basement, or hauling the debris from finishing one, works well in the 15 since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. Many Glenridge homes were expanded decades ago with rear additions — listings around here mention a rec room addition, a sunroom, and slider-to-patio family rooms — so tearing out or reworking one of those older additions tends to throw off framing lumber, drywall, and old roofing in 15-yard quantities.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house cleanout between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of the house — that may be a 20 or a 30 instead. We will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the easy part here.** Glenridge is the "G-section," and Belair's alliterative, alphabetical naming means every street in the section starts with the same letter — Galaxy Lane and Gradys Walk are the documented examples. Residents will tell you they live in "the G section," and that scheme is one of the few things about the neighborhood that hasn't changed since Levitt laid it out. What also hasn't changed is the build standard: these Levitt colonials were delivered with a private driveway and an attached or built-in garage, which gives you a flat, code-compliant spot to stage a roll-off on your own property rather than the street. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Glenridge driveways take that comfortably. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Glenridge.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because every Glenridge home came with its own driveway off a quiet interior street, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Getting in and out.** Glenridge sits off Maryland Route 197 (Collington Road / Laurel-Bowie Road), and the usual approach is from Rt 197 onto Gradys Walk, then onto Galaxy Lane. That is a quiet interior residential layout rather than a through road, which is one more reason driveway placement beats street staging — there is no reason to put a container on a narrow neighborhood street when the driveway is right there.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. With only a couple dozen homes in the G-section, this is a small, well-defined run for us, and most jobs go out within 24 hours. An early call may still make a same-day run.
