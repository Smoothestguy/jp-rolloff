## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material — construction debris in a 15-yard includes delivery, pickup on request, and a **14-day rental with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance** — priced by material, so call for a quote (other materials are priced separately, and aggregates are only available in the 10, 15, and 20). Keep it longer and it is just $5 per extra day. Go over the allowance and additional weight is billed at $105 per ton.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job. That is why the 15 is known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

A word of honesty, though, specific to this section: because Long Ridge is wall-to-wall Country Clubbers, a lot of the work here is *not* single-room. Whole-house and multi-room renovations of these big two-story Colonials are common, and those skew toward a 20 or a 30. We will say so if that is your situation. The 15 is the right tool when you are disciplined about one room or one system at a time.

## Which Long Ridge jobs suit the 15-yard

The Country Clubber is the largest of all the Bowie Levitt homes — a two-story Colonial that later came in five-bedroom configurations and originally topped out around $24,900 as the premium model. Now roughly 60 years old, these uniformly large homes drive a particular set of one-room, one-system projects the 15-yard handles well:

- **Kitchen remodels.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original Country Clubber kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is the single most common reason a 15 lands on an L-section driveway.
- **Roofing tear-offs.** The Country Clubber was built with a flat, low-slope rear dormer originally finished with roll roofing — a section that wears out faster than the main pitched roof. On 60-year-old homes, replacing that failure-prone low-slope roofing (often along with a single-layer asphalt tear-off on the main roof) is a recurring job here. Shingles and roll roofing are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage before we drop the container, and a smaller tear-off fits the 1.5-ton allowance comfortably — anything heavier just adds $105 per extra ton.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a home this age, that usually means dated fixtures and older wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanouts.** Clearing a finished or half-finished basement — or hauling out the debris from finishing one — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles a move-out or downsizing cleanout when a longtime Country Clubber changes hands.

One Long Ridge job sits right on the line: the **attic conversion.** Country Clubbers were built with a distinctive expandable, unfinished attic, and owners here routinely turn it into a bedroom, office, or beamed loft. Finishing it produces drywall, insulation, and framing debris — typically a 15- to 20-yard load. A modest conversion fits the 15; a larger one that opens up the whole top floor may want a 20. Tell us the scope and we will size it right rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the easy part here.** Long Ridge is an "L" section — under Belair's alliterative convention, every street name begins with the same letter as the section, so the L-streets are how locals navigate this corner of Bowie. More to the point, Long Ridge was built with larger Country Clubbers on slightly larger lots than the earlier Levitt sections, and it backs up toward Collington Road as one of the fully completed sections that expanded the community eastward. Those bigger lots and wider frontages mean most properties have a driveway that can take a dumpster on private ground. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and Long Ridge driveways generally handle that with room to spare. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Long Ridge.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Long Ridge homes sit on those slightly larger lots with real driveways, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit runs through the City of Bowie Department of Public Works on Annapolis Road, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We know the run into the L-streets well, most jobs go out within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run.
