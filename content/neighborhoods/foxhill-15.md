## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly four-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Foxhill rental runs **$425 to $485, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprise tacked on after the haul.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job from start to end. That is why the 15 is best known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

## Which Foxhill jobs suit the 15-yard

Foxhill's houses are drawn from the original Belair single-family models Levitt opened for sale in October 1960 — the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the Colonial, the Country Clubber, and the Manor House. Six decades on, those mid-century homes are dated by today's standards, right down to their original kitchens and baths. And because Levitt built Belair assembly-line style on concrete slabs, most Foxhill work is interior gut/remodel and roof or siding tear-off rather than basement digging. That shapes the debris, and it is precisely the one-room, one-system rhythm the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original Levitt kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is the single most common reason a 15 lands on a Foxhill driveway.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these detached homes fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most of these modest mid-century roof footprints. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a 60-year-old Levitt build, that often means dated fixtures and original wall material coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** Clearing out decades of accumulation — or the debris from finishing or re-finishing a basement — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles an original-owner estate or turnover cleanout when a long-held Foxhill home changes hands.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house interior gut between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of the house — that may be a 20 or larger instead. We will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and the F-streets

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here.** Foxhill follows Belair's wayfinding quirk to the letter: every street in the section begins with F — Foxhill Lane and its neighbors — and that alliterative scheme is the primary way locals identify where they are within the section. It is also the first thing our drivers confirm when they turn in, which cuts down on wrong-address runs that slow a delivery. Most Belair lots were platted at a uniform ~8,400 square feet — roughly one-fifth of an acre — to keep pricing fair across the development, so the yards here are modest but the private driveways are real. For a one-room reno that is exactly what you want: the 15-yard stages on your own driveway rather than the smaller yard or the street. A 15-yard needs about 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Foxhill driveways take that comfortably. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size that keeps your project on your own property.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Foxhill.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because these standardized Belair lots came with their own private driveways, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops in Foxhill never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day. If your block runs a formal association with placement rules, give them a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along.
