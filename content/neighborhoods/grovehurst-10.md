We call the 10-yard the "garage cleanout" size, and on a Grovehurst Colonial with an attached garage that name earns itself. The projects that fit this container cleanly tend to be these:

- **Garage cleanout.** Thirty years of stored boxes, lawn equipment and forgotten projects out of an attached two-car garage empties neatly into one 10-yard, and the compact can tucks into a single driveway spot beside it.
- **Bathroom remodel.** Pulling out one of these original 1990s baths — tile, vanity, tub, subfloor — makes heavy debris but not high volume. That's exactly where the low 3.5-foot walls and the 1-ton allowance earn their keep.
- **Yard waste and trees.** This is a big one in Grovehurst. The lots back up to mature hardwood forest, so storm limbs, leaf fall, brush and the occasional taken-down tree are a steady source of debris. Reclaiming a back bed or clearing a fence line fills a 10-yard fast.
- **Estate downsize.** These homes have largely been held by their original families since the '90s. When it's time to clear a long-held Colonial room by room at a respectful pace, the 10-yard moves the job along without crowding the driveway.

An honest note specific to this neighborhood. Because Grovehurst's homes are now around thirty years old and sit under a heavy tree canopy, a lot of the work here is exterior renovation — roofs, decks, siding and sunroom projects on those brick-and-vinyl elevations. A single-room interior job or a yard cleanup fits the 10-yard well. If that's your project, tell us on the phone and we'll point you to a larger size. The 10-yard is built for the jobs that stay in one room or one corner of the lot.

## Staging it on your driveway — and the Bowie permit rule

Here's where Grovehurst's layout works squarely in your favor. These are detached Colonials on wide private driveways, with side yards and quarter- to half-acre lots, so a 10-yard drop here almost always sits entirely on your own property. That matters for one practical reason: the City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when a dumpster sits in a city-maintained public street. Driveways and private property don't trigger it. The city's Public Works Department handles engineering review and permitting for work within the public right-of-way, but a can staged on your own driveway falls outside that — which, in Grovehurst, is the overwhelming norm.

Two more practical notes for this community. Grovehurst has an active Homeowners Association that maintains the common areas and sets community standards, so it's worth a quick check on where the association prefers a container to sit and how long it can stay — placing it on your own driveway or an approved staging spot keeps you on the right side of those standards. And because the 10-yard's footprint is so small, clearing a single-car spot the night before is usually all the prep you'll need. If for some reason the only workable spot is the street, just say so when you call and we'll walk you through the Bowie right-of-way permit so you're fully covered.
