## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 18 feet long by 8 feet wide by 6 feet tall and holds roughly 10.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: in Yorktown a 25-yard for construction debris and other materials is priced separately, so call for a quote — the price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton weight allowance. Extra days run $5/day, and weight beyond the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — just call to schedule it.

The jump up from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a second haul partway through — a full remodel, a substantial addition, a big roof, or a bulk cleanout. The walls stand a full six feet, so a crew can pitch debris over the side, and there's meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size cans offer.

One piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Eighteen feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. The good news in Yorktown is that the lots and driveways were built to handle real vehicles — but it's still worth confirming the spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Yorktown jobs suit the 25-yard

Yorktown's housing stock is the whole story here. The section is overwhelmingly detached single-family housing — roughly 99.8% free-standing single-family homes, a higher share than about 98.6% of U.S. neighborhoods — and the houses are mostly medium-to-large three-to-five-bedroom homes, with reported living areas spanning roughly 1,512 to 2,807 square feet. These are the Levitt models that filled the later 1960s sections: Cape Cods, Ranchers, Colonials, and the larger Country Clubbers and Manor Houses. They're now 55 to 60 years old, and that age is exactly what drives demand for a container this size. These are the jobs the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out a Rancher or adding onto a Colonial on one of the Y-named streets throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a smaller box can take without a mid-project swap.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full gut of an original Levitt home — kitchen, baths, flooring, and plaster or drywall all coming out at once — is a textbook 25-yard job. When a 1960s house gets modernized down to the studs, the debris adds up fast, and one large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** After six decades, original roofs reach the end of the line. Bigger or multi-layer tear-offs run heavy, so plan for the 2-ton allowance up front — weight beyond it is billed at $105 per additional ton.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** When a larger property or a business clearout near the section throws off material in bulk, the 25-yard handles it in one go.

If your Yorktown project is on the smaller side — a single bathroom, a garage cleanout, or clearing brush from an established lot — a 10-yard or 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we'll tell you so on the phone. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely big. Given how many of these homes have been held by the same families for years, the work we see at this size often skews toward the heavy end: full-house renovations on aging Levitt stock, additions, roof tear-offs, and large estate or downsizing cleanouts that empty an entire 1960s home.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement, and Yorktown is built for it.** This is one of the real advantages of working a Levitt section like this. With nearly every property a detached single-family home, almost every address here has its own private driveway — the exact surface a roll-off is meant to sit on. That matters even more at this size: an 18-foot container wants room, and a Yorktown driveway gives the truck a place to set it down cleanly rather than wrestling with the street. We'll scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The permit rule is in your favor — and there's no HOA in the way.** The Yorktown at Belair area has no homeowners association, so staging a dumpster on your own driveway is governed by you and city rules, not by an association sign-off. That's a real difference from Bowie's newer townhome and HOA communities, where placement often needs approval first. On top of that, the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster has to sit in a city street; a container placed entirely on your private driveway does **not** need one. Because almost every Yorktown home has its own driveway, the vast majority of jobs here stage on-property with no HOA approval and no city permit at all. If your particular address forces an 18-foot container into the street, we'll flag the permit requirement up front rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.

**Drive time.** From our Beltsville yard, Yorktown is about a 25-minute run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, and most addresses here are scheduled within 24 hours, with an early call sometimes still making a same-day run — so when you're running a build on a deadline, the container shows up when the crew needs it. The all-Y street naming actually helps our drivers: when you tell us you're on Yarmouth Lane or near Yorktown Elementary on Race Track Road, the collector road that borders the section, we know exactly where we're headed.
