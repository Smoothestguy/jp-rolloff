That single fact shapes the work: a renovation in Mitchellville usually means updating a 1980s, '90s, or early-2000s kitchen, bath, or finished basement rather than tearing into a 1960s teardown. ## What fits in a 10-yard dumpster

The 10-yard is the smallest footprint we offer, measuring 12 ft long by 8 ft wide by 3.5 ft tall. It holds roughly three pickup-truck loads of debris and comes with a 1-ton (2,000 lb) weight allowance. In Mitchellville a typical 10-yard rental runs **$350 to $425, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no surprise tipping fees sprung on you after the load is weighed.

The low 3.5-foot walls are the quiet advantage. You can walk right up and drop debris over the side without a ramp or a heave, which matters when you are loading heavy, dense material like old bathroom tile, vanity stone, or yard soil. The 1-ton allowance is sized for exactly that kind of focused work — a single-room remodel or a one-area cleanout, not a whole-house gut. And because the container fits in a single car spot, it disappears onto a Mitchellville driveway without swallowing the whole approach. If your project turns out bigger than one room, we will point you to a 15 or 20-yard instead — but for the jobs below, the 10 is the right call.

## Which Mitchellville jobs suit the 10-yard

This is the size folks call the "garage cleanout," and around Mitchellville that is the call we field most. Here are the four jobs that land squarely in a 10-yard on these streets:

- **Garage cleanouts.** The larger Colonials out here came with two- and three-car garages, and after twenty or thirty years they fill up with paint, old lawn equipment, lumber scraps, broken furniture, and boxes that never got unpacked. Clearing a bay or two into a 10-yard handles it without overpaying for unused space, and the container parks right beside the garage door on these wide driveways.

- **Bathroom remodels.** Mitchellville's baths are largely 1980s-through-early-2000s vintage now, which puts them squarely in remodel territory. A single-bath gut — dated tile, the tub, vanity, drywall, and subfloor — is the textbook 10-yard job. That dense tile and plaster stays well inside the 1-ton allowance for one bathroom, and updating these original builder baths is one of the most common projects on the street.

- **Yard waste and trees.** The expansive yards that define this neighborhood — half-acre to a full acre, many lined with mature trees that have had decades to fill in — generate real volume when it is time to clear brush, take down a tree, or pull out old beds and sod. The low-walled 10 loads easily by hand, and because green debris is heavy once it is wet, the 1-ton allowance is sized for a single yard's worth.

- **Estate downsize.** Mitchellville is one of the wealthiest predominantly African-American communities in the country, with very high homeownership, and many of these homes have been held by the same family for decades. When it is time to downsize a longtime household — clearing furniture, accumulated goods, and the clutter of years — a 10-yard handles a measured, room-by-room cleanout without committing to a full whole-house clear-out.

If you are gut-renovating an entire kitchen, finishing a large basement from scratch, tearing off a roof, or modernizing a whole home at once, those run heavier and bulkier than a 10 is built for, and we will tell you so up front. The 10-yard earns its keep on the one-room, one-corner, one-weekend jobs that make up most of the work here.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and gate access

**Driveway placement is the default in Mitchellville, and the lots are built for it.** This neighborhood sits in Prince George's County's larger-lot residential zoning context, where lots commonly run from about a half-acre up to a full acre, with long, wide driveways to match. A 10-yard needs only about 12 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and a clear swing for the rear door, and on a Mitchellville driveway that is no contest — the container stages comfortably on private property with room to spare. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface. Large brick Colonials on roughly one-acre lots out here have sold in the $810,000-to-$865,000 range, and on properties like that, putting the dumpster in the street is rarely necessary.

**The City of Bowie permit rule.** Mitchellville falls within the City of Bowie's reach, and the city's rule is straightforward: a right-of-way permit is required **only** if the dumpster sits in a city street. A container placed on your private driveway does not require one. Because the lots here are so large and the driveways so generous, the overwhelming majority of our Mitchellville drops never touch the right-of-way and never trigger a permit.

**Gated communities and HOAs.** Here is where Mitchellville differs sharply from older, open-access Bowie subdivisions. Woodmore — the gated community centered on the Country Club at Woodmore, planned for 398 single-family homes on 799 acres and carrying a 20721 mailing address — is the area's signature development, and gated access means deliveries must be coordinated through the gate. Several area homeowner associations operate in and around Mitchellville and Woodmore, including sub-developments like Woodmore Meadows, Woodmore South, and the Kingsford development off Enterprise Road, plus nearby Lake Arbor. In an HOA or gated community, we will need an approved staging spot and gate clearance arranged ahead of the drop. None of that is a hurdle — it just takes a phone call before the truck rolls, and we will walk you through it.

**Drive time.** Mitchellville is about 25 minutes from our Beltsville yard, an easy run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway, with Enterprise Road (MD-193) and Central Avenue (MD-214) the practical landmarks for the last leg. We dispatch every morning, so same-day delivery is common when you call early, and the rest of the schedule fills inside 24 hours.
