## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 22 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds roughly nine pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material, and a typical Glenridge rental **includes delivery, pickup on request, a 14-day rental, and a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance — priced by material, so call for a quote**. Extra time runs $5 per additional day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — call to schedule.

Those four feet of wall height are the part people appreciate most. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, while the long 22-foot footprint swallows the volume of a genuine renovation. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but short of the oversized 30 that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret. It is even nicknamed the "whole-house cleanout" size for a reason.

## Why the 20-yard fits Glenridge specifically

Here is what makes Glenridge different from Bowie's newer subdivisions. Every home here was built in 1969, which means the original kitchens, baths, single-pane windows, and aging HVAC and roofing are all reaching the end of the line at roughly the same time. The tax records bear this out — the homes on Galaxy Lane run from about 1,583 to 2,063 square feet, modest-to-mid-size colonials where a single-room remodel might only need a 10 or 15-yard, but a whole-house cleanout, a multi-room gut, or an addition tear-out moves squarely into 20-yard territory.

And Glenridge has a particular wrinkle: many of these homes were expanded over the decades with rear additions — listings around here mention a 17x26 rec room addition, a sunroom addition, slider-to-patio family rooms. When one of those decades-old additions gets torn out or rebuilt, you are dealing with framing lumber, drywall, old roofing, and concrete all at once. That mixed, heavier debris stream is exactly what the 20-yard's 2-ton allowance and nine-load capacity were built to handle.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again on the G-named streets:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** Clearing an original 1969 Levitt colonial between owners, or after a longtime family has held it for decades, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-baths gut, or a run of rooms getting new flooring, drywall, and those long-overdue replacement windows, is the classic 20-yard job in a 55-year-old home on its second or third renovation.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Glenridge is a tiny, settled section, and homes here tend to stay in the same hands a long time. When one finally changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical Belair-era roof tear-off fits comfortably on these modest-footprint colonials, with the 2-ton allowance covering most single-layer asphalt jobs.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are taking a colonial down to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address on Galaxy or Gradys Walk and the scope, and we will steer you straight. We would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the Glenridge advantage

**Driveway placement.** This is where Glenridge has a built-in advantage. The Levitt colonials here were delivered with private driveways and attached or built-in garages as standard, so there is almost always a flat, code-compliant spot to stage a roll-off on your own property. The 20-yard needs roughly 22 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door — and it fits most Glenridge driveways with room left over. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Glenridge home has its own driveway, a street permit is the exception here, not the rule — most projects stage entirely on private property with no permit at all. That matters even more on these streets: Galaxy Lane and Gradys Walk are quiet interior residential streets rather than through roads, so driveway placement is both easier and neighborly. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning, typically up Route 197 (Collington Road) and in onto Gradys Walk, then Galaxy Lane. We know exactly how this little section is laid out, which means no wandering trucks and no guesswork on placement. Most Glenridge addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run. The section sits in ZIP 20715.
