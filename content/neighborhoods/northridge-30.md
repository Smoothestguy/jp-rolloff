## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about nine pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material: construction debris in the 30-yard starts at $665, with other materials priced separately (aggregates like concrete, brick, and dirt are only offered in the 10-, 15-, and 20-yard sizes). That price includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton (6,000 lb) weight allowance. Extra rental days run $5 per day, and any weight over the allowance is $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request — give us a call to schedule it.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, finished-basement build-out, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why Northridge jobs reach this size

Northridge is one of Bowie's newer neighborhoods. Where the town's postwar Levitt-era central communities date to the mid-century, nearly every Northridge home went up between 1992 and 2001 — a master-planned development of large contemporary, Colonial Revival, and Craftsman-style single-family houses, many with attached garages, alongside three-bedroom townhouses. The homes run roughly 1,408 to 4,060 square feet with three to five bedrooms, and that building stock is what drives the work the 30-yard was made for:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting one of Northridge's larger single-family homes to the studs — pulling a dated kitchen, taking out interior walls, or tearing a whole lower level back to bare framing — produces far more debris than most owners expect. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** Contractor tear-outs and build-outs throw off a continuous stream of fixtures, flooring, ceiling material, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** Whole-house clear-outs, estate and downsizing cleanouts, and the contents of a full lower level pile up fast. Nine truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** Turning over several of Northridge's three-bedroom townhouses at once — clearing old appliances, carpet, and tenant debris together — is exactly the volume the 30-yard handles in one drop.

The standout driver here is the scale of the renovations themselves. Northridge skews toward a high-value, high-income, owner-occupied base — a median sale price near $617,000 and owner-occupancy around 92 percent — which means projects tend to be sizable interior renovations, basement finishes, and additions rather than small repairs. A four- or five-bedroom contemporary getting a whole-floor remodel, a lower-level finish, or an addition throws off a remarkable amount of material. When a project escalates like that, the 30-yard is the size that absorbs it without a second trip. For a single room or a routine garage cleanout, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second haul.

## Placing a big box on Northridge's streets and lots

A 30-yard is a big box on a long frame, and the driver needs a longer, clearer run-up than the smaller sizes to back the trailer in straight. That is worth thinking through in Northridge, because the neighborhood was laid out the 1990s and early-2000s way — winding streets and cul-de-sacs like Northview Drive and Quill Point Drive rather than the older grid pattern in central Bowie. A cul-de-sac bulb or a curving frontage changes how the truck approaches and turns around, so where we come at the drop matters.

The good news is that Northridge's larger lots, wide private driveways, and attached garages usually give us a real driveway to stage on — which is exactly the plan we prefer for a box this size. That keeps the container off the street and out of the turning radius of your neighbors. The townhouses are the tighter case, where a shorter or shared driveway may mean fitting the box on a single-width pad or an HOA-approved common spot instead. Access through the neighborhood is open-street, so there is no gate to coordinate. Either way, tell us the lot type and the approach when you book — a cul-de-sac, a corner, low branches over the drive, or a shared curb cut — and we will plan the drop around it the first time.

## Permits and the real Northridge gatekeeper: your HOA

Placement also decides whether a permit even enters the picture. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Northridge's wide private driveways and attached-garage aprons almost always give us a place to set the box off the street, a city street permit ends up being the exception rather than the rule here.

The hurdle that actually matters in Northridge is the HOA, not the city. The neighborhood is governed by homeowners associations that maintain the shared spaces and parks — Northridge Park with its pond and loop trail, and the Northridge Swim and Tennis Club over on Quill Point Drive — and those associations may carry placement or staging guidelines for dumpsters. For a container this size, sitting on a driveway or common ground for the length of a demolition or remodel, the relevant approval is staging and architectural clearance from your association. A quick word with them before the drop — confirming where the box can sit and for how long — keeps everything clean and avoids a covenant headache mid-project. If the public street genuinely is your only workable spot, the city's review applies and we are glad to walk you through what is involved before your container shows up.
