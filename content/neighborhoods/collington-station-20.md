## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 20-yard for construction debris **starts at $620 (other materials are priced separately)**, and that price includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance. Keep it longer for just $5 a day, and any weight over the 2-ton allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

Those five feet of wall height matter more than people expect. The walls are low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but tall enough to hold the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that really only makes sense for ground-up construction or full structural demo. Around here it earns its nickname — the "whole-house cleanout" size — and it is the size most customers end up choosing and rarely regret.

## Why the 20-yard fits Collington Station specifically

Collington Station is an unusually uniform neighborhood. It went up as a single planned subdivision of three-level Colonial Revivals, most of them mid-1990s builds with brick siding and attached garages. Because they all hit the market within a few years of each other, they are all aging into the renovation-heavy stretch together — and the projects repeat from street to street. Roof tear-offs, basement finishing and cleanouts, and kitchen-and-bath gut remodels show up again and again on these blocks because the houses are essentially the same house, thirty-plus years on.

That full-basement, three-level layout is the other reason the 20-yard fits so well. A finished or cleared-out basement on one of these colonials produces real volume — old carpet, paneling, drywall, framing, and decades of stored belongings — and the 20-yard is sized to take that down to the slab in one container. Here are the jobs that come up most often:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's signature role. Clearing one of these colonials top to bottom — or readying it for sale after years of accumulation — fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use. Furniture, boxes, old carpet, and household debris all go in one drop.
- **Multi-room remodels.** Modernizing a 30-year-old colonial usually means hitting the kitchen and a bath or two in one push. The drywall, tile, cabinetry, and trim debris from a multi-room gut is exactly the volume a 20 was built to hold.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Many Collington Station homes have had the same owners since the neighborhood was new. When one of those longtime homes changes hands, the 20-yard handles a full clear-out in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these three-level colonials fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most jobs of that footprint.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a closet purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better and save you a little. If you are combining a gut renovation with a full basement clear-out and a roof all at once, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight. We would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the HOA

**Placement is the easy part in Collington Station.** Because these homes were built as attached-garage colonials with paved driveways, a 20-yard almost always stages driveway-side with room to spare — most lots here comfortably hold a two-car driveway, and a 20-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door. That driveway placement also keeps things simple on permits: the City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street, and driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Drop the box on your own driveway and there is no city permit to chase at all.

**Clear it with the HOA first.** Collington Station is governed by the Collington Station Homeowners' Association, listed on Jordon Pond Lane, and it is an active, professionally managed association that maintains common amenities like the clubhouse, pool, tennis courts, and playground. While a dumpster on your own driveway is straightforward, anything staged on or spilling onto common areas should be cleared with the association beforehand. We are happy to provide a service confirmation you can forward to the HOA, and if your only workable spot turns out to be the street itself, that right-of-way permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department — and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Getting the truck in.** Collington Station is laid out as a suburban subdivision of lane-style residential streets — names like Jordon Pond Lane and Johnstone Lane — rather than wide through-roads. They are quiet and residential, so we route the truck in carefully and set the box exactly where it needs to go on your driveway. The neighborhood sits in ZIP 20721, near the Oak Creek Golf Club along the southern edge of Bowie.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning. Most Collington Station addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run.
