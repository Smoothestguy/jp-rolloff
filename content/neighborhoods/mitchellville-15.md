## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly seven pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: construction debris in the 15-yard is **priced by material, so call for a quote** (other materials are priced separately, and aggregates are only available in the 10, 15, and 20). Every rental **includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance**. Extra days run $5/day, and weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a finished basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job start to end. That is why the 15 is best known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

## Which Mitchellville jobs suit the 15-yard

Mitchellville's housing stock is predominantly larger Colonial Revivals — often with front columns — plus some ranch-style homes, set in subdivisions of curving streets and expansive yards. Big brick Colonials on roughly one-acre lots have sold in the $810,000-to-$865,000 range. These are substantial, well-kept houses, and because the original construction dates to the 1980s, '90s, and early 2000s rather than the 1960s, the work tends to be updating dated-but-solid finishes rather than rescuing a worn-out shell. That points squarely at the one-room, one-system jobs the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one 1980s-or-1990s kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. This is the single most common reason a 15 lands on a Mitchellville driveway.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these homes fits comfortably, though the 1.5-ton allowance is easy to exceed on these roof footprints — any weight beyond it is billed at $105 per additional ton. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it — the larger Colonials here can carry more roof than the number suggests.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. In these homes that often means builder-grade fixtures from the original construction coming out together.
- **Basement cleanout.** Clearing out a finished basement — or the debris from re-finishing one — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles an estate or turnover cleanout when a long-held Mitchellville home changes hands.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house interior gut, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of one of these large Colonials — that may be a 20 or larger instead. We will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Placement, permits, and gated access

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here, and then some.** This is where Mitchellville differs sharply from the older Levitt sections of Bowie. Lots run roughly half an acre to a full acre, consistent with the county's larger-lot residential zoning, and the long, wide driveways that come with them give a 15-yard plenty of room to stage on private property. A 15-yard needs about 16 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door — and on these driveways that is rarely a question. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it tucks onto a driveway without dominating the front of the house.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites here.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Mitchellville's big lots and long driveways almost always allow on-property placement, a city street permit is the exception rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops here never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**Gates and HOAs are the real local wrinkle.** The signature development here is Woodmore — the gated, country-club community centered on the Country Club at Woodmore at 12320 Pleasant Prospect, with its sub-developments like Woodmore Meadows, Woodmore South, and the Kingsford development off Enterprise Road. Inside the gates, a delivery is not just a matter of pulling up to the driveway: access has to be coordinated through the gate and, in many cases, the HOA's approved staging spot. Multiple homeowner associations operate in and around Mitchellville and Woodmore, and they tend to care more about where a container sits than the open-access subdivisions elsewhere in Bowie do. None of that is a problem — it just needs a heads-up. Tell us your community when you call, give your gate or association our arrival window, and we will provide a service confirmation you can forward to the HOA if they want one.

**Drive time and routing.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. For routing into the Woodmore area, the practical landmarks are the arterials that frame it — Enterprise Road (MD-193) on the west and Central Avenue (MD-214) on the south, with Woodmore Road running east-west through the middle. Mitchellville carries the 20721 ZIP, served by the South Bowie/Mitchellville Post Office. Most jobs go out within 24 hours, and an early call may still make a same-day run.
