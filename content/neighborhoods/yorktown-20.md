Yorktown is one of the Levitt "Y-section" neighborhoods of Belair at Bowie, and you can find it the same way the mail carriers and the moving trucks do — by the street signs. Every road in the section begins with the letter Y: Yorktown Drive, Yarmouth Lane, Yellowstone Place, Youngwood Turn. That alliterative naming was William Levitt's way of giving each of his 21 lettered sections its own identity, and it still works today. When someone tells us they are off a Y-street near Race Track Road, we know exactly where we are headed. The houses behind those signs went up among Belair's later sections, roughly 1964 through the late 1960s, as part of the last detached single-family homes Levitt and Sons built in Bowie. That makes Yorktown's housing stock 55 to 60 years old now, and that single fact drives most of the dumpster work we do here. These are original Levitt homes coming due for their second or third real renovation, and the 20-yard roll-off is the size most of those projects land on.

We are JP Roll-Off Service, a family-owned hauler dispatched out of Beltsville, about 25 minutes from Yorktown by way of Route 197 or the BW Parkway. When you call, you get the family that owns the trucks — not a call center.

## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Yorktown rental runs **$450 to $525, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises added after the fact.

Those five feet of wall height are the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a genuine project. That balance is why the 20-yard sits in the sweet spot for so many homes in the Y-section: bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo. It is the size most customers end up choosing — and rarely regret.

## Why the 20-yard fits Yorktown specifically

Yorktown is overwhelmingly detached single-family housing — by the numbers, roughly 99.8% free-standing single-family homes, a higher share than nearly any neighborhood in the country. The homes themselves are mostly medium-to-large three-to-five-bedroom houses, with living areas running somewhere between about 1,500 and 2,800 square feet, and they are owner-occupied at one of the highest rates in the nation. That combination — sizable original homes, longtime owners, and a section that is now well into its sixth decade — is exactly what feeds the 20-yard.

Sixty years on, the original Levitt builds feel dated: standardized kitchens, original baths, layouts today's owners want opened up. When a Yorktown home turns over or a longtime owner finally commits to a real modernization, the work tends to be substantial but contained to one house. That is exactly the 20-yard's wheelhouse.

Here are the jobs that come up again and again on the Y-named streets:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** This is the 20-yard's best-known role — it is even nicknamed the "whole-house cleanout" size. Clearing an original Yorktown Levitt home between owners, or after years of accumulation in a house someone has held since the 1960s or 70s, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you will not use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-baths gut, or a run of bedrooms getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job in a Levitt home on its second or third renovation. These three-to-five-bedroom houses generate exactly the debris volume a 20 was built for.
- **Estate cleanouts.** Yorktown has held many of the same families for decades, with ownership rates near the top few percent nationally. When one of those longtime homes finally changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical Belair-era roof tear-off fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most single-layer asphalt jobs on these original homes.

If your project is smaller — a single bathroom, a garage purge, a few rooms of flooring — a 15-yard may serve you better. If you are taking a Yorktown home down to the studs and adding on, we will talk through whether a 30 makes more sense. Tell us the address and the scope and we will steer you straight; we would rather size it right than oversell you.

## Placement, permits, and the Yorktown advantage

**Driveway placement.** This is where Yorktown has a built-in advantage over Bowie's newer townhome and condo communities. Because the section is almost entirely free-standing single-family homes, nearly every property has its own private driveway, and a 20-yard fits most of these with room to spare. It needs roughly 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance and an open swing for the rear door — and on the typical Yorktown lot, the driveway is the natural staging spot. We will lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**No HOA sign-off.** Unlike the planned communities elsewhere in Bowie, the Yorktown at Belair area has no homeowners association governing your property. That means dumpster placement on your own driveway is between you and the city rules — there is no architectural-control board to ask and no association approval to wait on. You decide where it sits on your own pavement.

**The Bowie permit rule.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because nearly every Yorktown home has its own driveway, a city street permit is the exception here, not the rule — most Y-section projects stage entirely on private property with no permit at all. If the only practical place turns out to be the street, that permit goes through Bowie's Public Works Department, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Yorktown sits along Race Track Road — the main collector that borders the section and runs past Yorktown Elementary at 7301 Race Track Road — so we know exactly where these streets are and how to get a truck onto a Y-named cul-de-sac. Most Yorktown addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run. The section is in ZIP 20715.
