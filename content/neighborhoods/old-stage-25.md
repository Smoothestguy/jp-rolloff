## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 18 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly 10.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: in Old Stage a 25-yard is priced by what you're throwing away — call for a quote (other materials priced separately), and that includes delivery, a 14-day rental, and a 2-ton weight allowance, with pickup on request — just call to schedule. Extra days run $5/day and weight over the 2-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton.

The jump from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a second haul partway through — an addition, a full remodel, a big roof, or a heavy cleanout. At six feet, the walls are taller than the mid-size containers, so there is meaningfully more room behind them — load with the height in mind and use the rear swing door to walk the heaviest debris in low.

One piece of advice we give every Old Stage customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Eighteen feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. The good news in Old Stage is that the footprint is rarely the problem — the lots and driveways out here tend to be generous. Still, confirm the spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Old Stage jobs suit the 25-yard

The neighborhood's housing era is the story here. Old Stage homes were built from the 1980s through the 2000s, which means many are now 25 to 40 years old — squarely in the window where original kitchens, baths, roofs, and finishes start coming out. These are larger homes than Bowie's midcentury Levitt houses, often pricing in the $500,000 to $650,000 range, and bigger homes generate bigger renovation debris. These are the jobs the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** A bump-out, a rear addition, or a garage expansion on a split-level or Colonial throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a smaller box can take without a mid-project swap.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full modernization of a 1980s-or-90s Old Stage home — kitchen, baths, flooring, and drywall all coming out at once — is a textbook 25-yard job. When a larger house gets updated room by room or down to the studs, the debris adds up fast, and one large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** After two or three decades, the original roofs on these homes reach the end of the line. Bigger or multi-layer tear-offs run heavy, so plan for the 2-ton allowance — extra weight is billed at $105 per additional ton.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** Old Stage sits within minutes of Bowie Town Center, so a larger property turnover or a business clearout nearby that throws off material in bulk is exactly what the 25-yard handles in one go.

If your Old Stage project is on the smaller side — a single bathroom refresh, one room redone, or a routine garage clearout — a 20-yard or even a 15-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so. Remember, too, that routine household garbage, recycling, and yard waste are already handled by municipal pickup here, so a roll-off this size is for renovation debris and construction loads that blow past curbside service. The 25-yard pays off when the job is genuinely big.

## Local logistics: placement, parking, and permits

**Placement in Old Stage usually works in your favor.** Unlike Bowie's denser, tighter subdivisions, the Old Stage area tends toward larger parcels — some listings along the Old Stage Road corridor cite lots near 100 by 125 feet, and a few run to multiple acres. That generous frontage and those longer driveways mean an 18-foot container can almost always sit on your own property, rather than out in the street. For a homeowner running a major build, that is the easiest possible scenario: the box lands where the work is, the truck has room to set it and retrieve it, and you are not negotiating with anyone over a public spot. Before we dispatch, we will still confirm exactly where the container will sit and that the truck can reach it and swing the door clear.

**Coordinate with the Olde Stage HOA first.** The neighborhood is governed by the Olde Stage Homeowners Association, which sets bylaws and handles community maintenance. If your 25-yard is going on your own driveway, that is typically straightforward. But if placement would touch a common area or sit anywhere other than your driveway, HOA approval is usually the single thing that makes the drop go smoothly. A quick check with the association before delivery day is time well spent — and we are glad to work around whatever they approve.

**The city permit rule is straightforward — and usually in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster has to sit in a city street; the Public Works Department handles that engineering review and permitting for work in public rights-of-way. A container staged on private property or in an HOA-approved spot does **not** need a permit. Given Old Stage's roomy driveways, most drops here never touch the street, so the HOA — not a city permit — is the real placement gatekeeper. If your particular project does force the container into a city street, we will flag the permit requirement up front rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.

**Drive time.** From our Beltsville yard, Old Stage is about a 25-minute run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, and most addresses here are scheduled within 24 hours, with an early call sometimes making a same-day run — so when you are running a build on a deadline, the container shows up when the crew needs it.
