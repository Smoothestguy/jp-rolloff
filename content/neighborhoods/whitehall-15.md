## What fits in a 15-yard dumpster

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly four-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 15-yard for construction debris **starts at $595**, and other materials are priced separately (aggregates are available only in the 10, 15, and 20). That price **includes delivery, pickup on request, and a 14-day rental with a 1.5-ton allowance** — extra days run $5/day, and any weight over the allowance is billed at $105 per additional ton.

The four-foot wall height is the quiet advantage of this size. The sides are low enough to pitch debris over the rail without a ramp, which matters when you are carrying load after load out of a kitchen or up from a basement. But there is still enough volume to finish a real single-room job start to end. That is why the 15 is best known as the "single-room reno" size — the one to reach for when a 10-yard feels a touch tight but a 20 would leave you paying for air.

A very common Levitt-Bowie renovation is opening up an original galley kitchen, finishing an attic, or converting old carport or attached open space into living area — projects that generate framing, drywall, and old-finish debris that suit a 15 well. - **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these detached homes fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most of these mid-1960s roof footprints. - **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a late-1960s Levitt build, that often means dated fixtures and original wall material coming out together.

## Placement, permits, and the W-streets

**Driveway placement is the natural fit here.** Whitehall follows Belair's wayfinding quirk to the letter: every street in the section begins with W — William Lane and its neighbors — so the alliterative grid is the first thing our drivers confirm when they turn in, which cuts down on wrong-address runs that slow a delivery. These are established, non-gated Levitt streets with real private driveways, and for a one-room reno that is exactly what you want: the 15-yard stages on your own driveway rather than the street. A 15-yard needs about 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Whitehall driveways take that comfortably. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and because the 15 is shorter and lower than the bigger containers, it is the size that keeps your project on your own property.

**The golf-course lots are the real planning question.** Whitehall backs directly onto the public 18-hole Bowie Golf Club on MD 197, and lots that border the course can complicate where debris gets staged near the rear property line. If your home backs the course, the smart move is usually to keep the 15-yard up on the driveway and carry debris forward rather than working it out the back — and because the 15 is the lower, shorter container, it is the easiest size to tuck into a front driveway and load by hand. We will look at your lot before delivery and plan the drop around it.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Whitehall.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because these Levitt lots came with their own private driveways, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops in Whitehall never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day. Whitehall is referenced on the City of Bowie's civic association list; if your block runs a formal association with placement rules, give them a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway.
