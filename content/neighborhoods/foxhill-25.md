## What fits in a 25-yard dumpster

The 25-yard is our major-construction size. It measures 18 ft long by 8 ft wide by 6 ft tall and holds roughly 10.5 pickup-truck loads of debris. Pricing is per material: a 25-yard in Foxhill is **quoted by what you are throwing away rather than a flat sticker price**, and every quote **includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 2-ton allowance**. Need it longer? It is just **+$5/day**. Go over the included tonnage and excess weight runs **$105 per additional ton** — we weigh the haul and bill only the overage. Pickup is on request, so give us a call to schedule it when you are ready.

The jump up from a 20-yard is real volume, not just a couple of extra feet of steel. That capacity is what keeps a single container on site through a project that would otherwise force a second haul partway through — a whole-house remodel, a substantial addition, a large roof, or a bulk cleanout. The walls stand six feet here, taller than the lower mid-size boxes, so on a deep load you may want a wheelbarrow ramp — but there is meaningfully more room behind those walls than the mid-size containers offer.

The one piece of advice we give every customer on this size: **measure twice before booking.** Eighteen feet of straight container, plus clearance for the truck and the rear swing door, is a long footprint. Foxhill's lots were laid out with private driveways, which helps — but on a box this size it is still worth confirming the spot before delivery day rather than after.

## Which Foxhill jobs suit the 25-yard

Foxhill's housing stock is the story here. The F-section was one of the earliest pieces of Belair to go up, drawn from the single-family Levitt models that opened for sale in October 1960 — the Cape Cod, the Rancher, the Colonial, the Country Clubber, and the Manor House. Levitt built these assembly-line style on concrete slabs, finishing six to ten homes a day, and that slab-on-grade construction shapes the work we see here. With no basements to dig out, the big Foxhill jobs are interior gut/remodel work and roof and siding tear-offs rather than excavation. These are the projects the 25-yard is built for:

- **Home additions.** Bumping out a Rancher or adding onto a Colonial on an F-named street throws off framing lumber, old siding, and tear-out debris in the volume a 25-yard is meant to hold — more than a smaller box can take without a mid-project swap.
- **Whole-house remodels.** A full gut of an original slab-built Levitt home — kitchen, baths, flooring, and drywall all coming out at once — is a textbook 25-yard job. When a 1960s house gets modernized down to the studs, decades of dated finishes add up fast, and one large container beats two trips.
- **Large roofing tear-offs.** After six decades, original roofs reach the end of the line. Bigger or multi-layer tear-offs run heavy, and any weight beyond the included 2-ton allowance is just $105 per additional ton.
- **Commercial cleanouts.** When a larger property or a business near the section clears out in bulk, the 25-yard swallows furniture, fixtures, and accumulated material in one go.

If your Foxhill project is on the smaller side — a single-room redo, one bathroom, or a routine garage cleanout — a 10-, 15-, or 20-yard is usually the smarter spend, and we will tell you so. Most everyday Foxhill jobs fit a mid-size driveway container just fine. The 25-yard pays off when the work is genuinely big.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement, and Foxhill was built for it.** Most Belair lots were standardized at about 8,400 square feet — roughly a fifth of an acre — to keep pricing fair across the development, and every home came with a private driveway. Those modest, uniform lots mean the driveway is almost always the right call for staging a container, and on a 25-yard it lets you keep the full 18 feet on your own property. We still ask you to walk the driveway with a tape measure before delivery day — confirm the straight run, the overhead clearance, and room for the truck to swing the rear door — but the bones are here. We will scout the spot with you and lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The permit rule is in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster has to sit in a city street. A container placed entirely on your private driveway — or staged with HOA approval — does **not** need one. Given that nearly every Foxhill home has its own driveway, the vast majority of jobs here stage on-property with no permit at all. If your particular address forces the container into the street, we will flag the requirement up front rather than letting it surprise you on delivery day.

**Drive time.** Foxhill sits right by Foxhill Park — the 45-acre park around Woodward Pond at 5001 Collington Road, next to the historic Belair Mansion that anchors this whole part of Bowie. From our Beltsville yard, that is about a 25-minute run via Route 197 or the BW Parkway. We dispatch every morning, and most addresses are scheduled within 24 hours by next-day delivery — with an early call sometimes still making a same-day run, so when you are running a build on a deadline, the container shows up when the crew needs it.
