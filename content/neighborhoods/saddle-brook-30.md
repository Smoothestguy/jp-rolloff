## What the 30-yard holds

The 30-yard measures roughly 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet tall. That long, tall box swallows about fourteen pickup-truck loads of material, which is why it is the go-to for jobs that throw off a steady wall of debris rather than a few bags. Pricing is per material rather than one flat number: a 30-yard is **priced by what you are throwing away, so call for a quote**. Every rental includes delivery, pickup, and a 14-day rental with a 3-ton weight allowance. Extra days run $5 per day, and any weight over the 3-ton allowance is $105 per additional ton. Pickup is on request, so just call to schedule it when you are ready.

One honest word on that weight allowance. The 30-yard's volume fills up fastest with bulky, lighter material — framing lumber, drywall, old cabinetry, flooring, trim, furniture, and general construction debris. If your load leans heavy with concrete, brick, dirt, or tile, the cube fills slowly but the tonnage climbs fast, and a smaller bin or a dedicated heavy-debris container is usually the smarter call. When you phone in, tell us what is going in and we will steer you to the right size before the truck rolls.

## Why a Saddle Brook job reaches this size

Saddle Brook is not one of Bowie's older Levitt-era enclaves of ramblers and split-levels. It is a subdivision of larger single-family homes built largely between 1997 and 2001, running roughly 2,228 to 4,462 square feet with three to six bedrooms — full-size colonials with basements, not starter homes. The adjacent Saddlebrook West section is younger still, a run of New Traditional homes built mostly between 2001 and 2004, with brick fronts, Colonial Revival lines, and a fairly uniform look, ranging from about 2,053 to 5,108 square feet. When you renovate or clear out a house that size, the debris adds up fast — which is exactly where the 30-yard earns its keep:

- **Full demolition.** Gutting a full-size colonial or New Traditional home down to the studs throws off far more debris than most owners expect — drywall, dated cabinetry, flooring, trim, and finished-basement materials come out in a steady stream. The 30-yard keeps a demo crew moving without stopping to swap containers mid-job.
- **Commercial site work.** With the City of Bowie having acquired the adjacent former race track property — demolishing the grandstand and clubhouse, stripping the track surface, and working toward recreational space — and ongoing contractor turnovers across these larger homes, site work here throws off a continuous run of fixtures, framing, and packaging. One large box keeps the dumpster off the critical path.
- **Bulk waste hauls.** On these bigger lots, garage and full-basement cleanouts, estate clear-outs, and downsizing projects pile up fast — furniture, appliances, and the contents of an entire finished level all at once. Fourteen truck-loads of capacity means you load once and we haul once.
- **Multi-family cleanouts.** When several units or a whole property turn over at the same time — old appliances, carpet, mattresses, and accumulated debris together — that combined volume is precisely what the 30-yard was built to handle.

For a single-room remodel, a bathroom refresh, or a routine cleanout in Saddle Brook, a smaller container is almost always the better fit. The 30-yard shines when capacity, not budget, is the thing that would otherwise cost you a second trip.

## Placement: driveway staging is the norm here

Here is the good news for a 30-yard in Saddle Brook. Unlike Bowie's tight townhouse frontages, this neighborhood was built with wide private driveways and roomy lots, so the staging question is usually straightforward — most drops here go on the driveway, not the street. The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only when a dumpster sits in a city street; driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. With these homes, an off-street driveway spot almost always means no city permit at all.

The 30-yard is the longest box we run, and it needs a clear, straight run-up to set down cleanly. On most Saddle Brook driveways that is no trouble, but it is worth walking your approach before delivery — a parked second car, a low branch, or a tight turn off the street is the kind of thing we would rather know about in advance than discover with the trailer halfway up the drive. Tell us your street and where you want the box, and we will route the truck so it lands right the first time.

The other piece to think through is the HOA. Both Saddle Brook and Saddlebrook West are governed by active homeowners associations — Saddlebrook West manages common amenities including a pool, two tennis courts, and a playground, and Saddle Brook homes carry quarterly association fees that point to an HOA worth checking with before you schedule. If your container is going on your own driveway, that is generally the simplest path. If the only workable staging spot touches a shared drive or a common area, coordinate placement with the HOA first so there is no question about where the box can sit. A quick call to your association before delivery day usually settles it.

A little planning goes a long way. The Race Track Road corridor is the area's main spine — Saddlebrook East Park sits at 8311 Race Track Road, and the neighborhood's equestrian street names, like Running Horse Place and Race Horse Lane, nod to the old track next door. Because the approach runs through residential streets rather than wide arterials, telling us your exact street and where you would like the 30-yard staged helps us bring the truck and trailer in clean on the first pass.
