## Measure twice before you book

The 25-yard is a long box, and that length matters more than folks expect. Twenty feet of steel, plus the room our truck needs to set it down and lift it back up, calls for a clear, reasonably straight run. This is where Heather Hills's mid-century layout deserves a careful look before you commit.

These are quarter-acre Levitt lots — real driveway and front-yard room, but not unlimited. A driveway that easily swallows a 15-yard box can come up short for the big one. And because Heather Hills sits behind its own tight grid of "H" streets — every street in the section starts with the same letter, a Levitt convention that makes the street name the clearest sign of where a home sits — the residential roads here are narrow. Realistically, the right place for a 25-yard is your own driveway or quarter-acre lot, not tying up one of those streets. So measure your driveway length before you book, and tell us about any tight spots: a low oak branch, a gate, a second car you can't move. Give us those numbers and we'll tell you honestly whether the 25 fits on your driveway or whether you'd be better off staging it a different way. We'd rather have that conversation up front than show up with a box that won't sit right.

## Permits and placement in Heather Hills

Here's the part that catches people off guard, and for most of this neighborhood it's good news. The City of Bowie only requires a right-of-way permit when the dumpster sits in a city street. If the container stays on your driveway or an HOA-approved staging spot, no street permit is needed.

Because Heather Hills homes are detached single-family houses with private driveways on quarter-acre lots, we can usually keep the 25-yard off the street entirely — which means most homeowners here skip the permit step. That fits the neighborhood well, because the narrow "H" streets aren't where you want a 20-foot box parked anyway. If the only workable spot turns out to be the street, we'll let you know so you can pull the right-of-way permit before delivery day. There's also a Heather Hills Homeowners Association presence in the neighborhood, so if your staging plan touches any shared or common space, it's worth a quick check with them first. When in doubt, call us and we'll talk through where the box should go before it ever leaves the yard.

## A few things worth thinking through

- **Heavy materials add up quickly.** Roofing shingles, plaster, and old tile are dense. A large roofing tear-off on a 60-year-old Levitt home can approach or pass the 2-ton allowance, so tell us what you're loading and we'll flag whether you're likely to go over — extra weight is $105 per additional ton.
- **Whole-house gut jobs are the sweet spot.** Remodel debris — drywall, plaster, original cabinets, flooring and trim from a 1965 Cape Cod or Rancher — fills the volume of a 25-yard without usually maxing out the weight. That's the ideal load for this size.
- **Additions generate both.** A home addition throws off excavation spoils, framing waste, and packaging all at once. Keep heavy soil and concrete loads modest so the container stays under its 2-ton allowance and you avoid the $105-per-ton excess charge.
