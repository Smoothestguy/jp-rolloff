The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall, and it holds roughly four-and-a-half pickup-truck loads of debris. ## Which Idlewild jobs suit the 15-yard

Idlewild's homes are aging Levitt builds, and that drives a very particular kind of demand. The section was assembled from a small set of repeating models — the Cape Cod (later the Ardsley), the Rancher (the Buckingham), the Colonials (Cambridge and Gladstone), the Country Clubber (Framingham), and the Manor House. The models that sold in 1968 were essentially the same ones launched at the start of the decade, so the floor plans repeat up and down the street. Six decades on, that makes renovation scope here remarkably predictable — and most of it lands in the one-room, one-system range the 15-yard handles best:

- **Kitchen remodels.** Tearing out the cabinets, countertops, and flooring from one original Levitt kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — cabinets, drywall, old subfloor, and packaging, with a little room to spare. Because the kitchens repeat across the I-section, this is the single most common reason a 15 lands on an Idlewild driveway.
- **Roofing tear-offs.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these detached homes fits comfortably, with the 1.5-ton allowance covering most modest roofs. Shingles are heavy, so we will talk through the square footage with you before we drop it.
- **Two-bath gut jobs.** Pulling tile, tubs, vanities, and old plumbing from a pair of bathrooms is a classic 15-yard load — more debris than a 10 wants, not enough to justify a 20. On a ~1965 Levitt build, that often means older plaster and dated fixtures coming out together.
- **Basement cleanouts.** Clearing a finished or half-finished basement — or hauling the debris from finishing one — works well in the 15, since the lower walls make loading from a stairwell easier. The same size handles a move-out or downsizing cleanout when a longtime family home on Idlewild Drive changes hands.

If your project is bigger — a whole-house gut between owners, or a multi-room renovation that runs the length of the house — that may be a 20 or a 30 instead. We will tell you straight which size your job needs rather than upsell you into a container you cannot fill.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement is the easy part here.** Idlewild is the "I-section" — Levitt's signature alphabetical street-naming convention runs throughout Belair at Bowie, where every street in a section starts with the same letter, so addresses here cluster around Idlewild Drive. That single-letter quirk is one of the few things about the neighborhood that hasn't changed since Levitt laid it out. What also hasn't changed is the build standard: every Belair home was delivered with a paved driveway and a garage included as standard features. For a one-room reno that is exactly what you want — the dumpster stages on your own private driveway rather than the smaller yard or the street. Idlewild Drive alone carries roughly 90-plus addresses on uniform Levitt-era lots, the kind of mature suburban driveways where a 15-yard sits comfortably. The container needs about 14 feet of straight length plus a little clearance and an open swing for the rear door, and most Idlewild driveways take that with room to spare. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface.

**The Bowie permit rule, and why it rarely bites in Idlewild.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street — the city code prohibits obstructing streets, sidewalks, or alleys without permission and calls for permits and amber lighting on anything stored in a public street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not trigger that requirement. Because nearly every I-section home came with its own driveway, a city street permit is the exception here rather than the rule — most 15-yard drops never touch the right-of-way at all. If the only practical spot turns out to be the street, that permit goes through the city, and we will flag it the moment we look at your address rather than letting you find out on delivery day.

**A note on associations.** The original 1960s Levitt sections of Bowie, including Idlewild, generally do not carry the mandatory dues-and-architectural-control HOAs you find in Bowie's newer subdivisions — listings in these Belair sections routinely advertise "No HOA." In practice that usually means no design-review or staging-approval step before a dumpster goes on your own driveway. We say "usually" on purpose: if your block does run a formal association with placement rules, give them a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along.
