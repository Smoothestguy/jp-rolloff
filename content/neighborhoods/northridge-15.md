## Why the 15-yard fits a Northridge home

The 15-yard measures 14 ft long by 8 ft wide by 4 ft tall and holds about four and a half pickup-truck loads of debris. It carries a 1.5-ton (3,000 lb) weight allowance, and a typical Northridge rental runs **$425 to $485, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage** — no tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the haul.

We call this the "single-room reno" size, and it is the in-between container — bigger than a 10-yard quick cleanup, but still short and low enough to stay driveway-friendly. That matters in Northridge in a specific way. Because these are 1990s-and-newer homes on subdivision lots with attached garages and broad driveways, you almost always have your own paved space to stage the box. The 14-foot footprint sits comfortably on a Northridge driveway with room to spare, which is exactly why most placements here never need to touch the street.

So the 15 fits on two counts: it swallows a contained, one-room renovation in a contemporary single-family home, and its compact footprint stages easily on the kind of wide private driveway Northridge was built with. If you are gutting a whole level at once, finishing a large basement end-to-end, or running a full addition, you may want to step up to a 20 or larger. For a single defined project in a Northridge home, the 15 is the one we drop most often.

## Which Northridge projects suit the 15-yard

Northridge skews toward higher-value work. With a median sale price around $617,000, household incomes well above average, and owner-occupancy near 92 percent, this is a neighborhood of long-term owners who invest in their homes rather than patch them. And because the housing stock is now 25 to 30-plus years old, the projects coming up are first major renovations — original-finish kitchens, baths, and roofs reaching the end of their service life. A 15-yard is sized right for the jobs that hit one part of one of these homes at a time:

- **Kitchen remodel.** Tearing out the original 1990s cabinets, counters, flooring, and a wall of dated drywall from a Northridge kitchen fills a 15-yard about right — enough room for a full single-room gut without paying for space you won't use.
- **Roofing tear-off.** A single-layer asphalt tear-off on one of these contemporary single-family rooflines sits comfortably inside the 1.5-ton allowance. As the original 1992–2001 roofs age out across the neighborhood, this is one of the most common 15-yard calls we get out here.
- **Two-bath gut.** Pulling tile, vanities, tubs, and subfloor from a pair of bathrooms in a three-to-five-bedroom home is a classic 15-yard job — the right capacity for a multi-bath refresh without jumping to a larger box.
- **Basement cleanout.** Clearing out a lower level — old carpet, paneling, stored furniture, and accumulation — before a finish or a sale lands neatly in a 15. Given how many Northridge homes have full basements ripe for finishing, this is a frequent fit.

Because so many of these homes came out of the same early-2000s building era, we have a good sense of what one room's worth of demo weighs before we ever pull up. If you are not sure whether your project is a 15 or a 20, tell us the room and the scope. We have hauled enough of these contemporary-home renovations to point you the right way before you commit.

## Placement, parking, and the Bowie permit rule

**Your driveway is almost always the answer here.** This is what sets Northridge apart from Bowie's older postwar grid sections. Out here you have larger lots, attached garages, and wide private driveways — so unlike the tight-frontage neighborhoods closer to the center of town, there is genuine room to set a 14-foot container on your own property. We square the box on the driveway, lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface, and keep it clear of the garage and your neighbor's frontage.

**The Bowie permit rule works in your favor.** The City of Bowie requires a right-of-way permit only if the dumpster sits in a city street. Driveways and HOA-approved staging do not. Because Northridge placements stay on private driveways the vast majority of the time, most rentals here avoid the city permit entirely. In the rare case where your only workable spot truly puts the container in a city street, we will flag the right-of-way permit the moment we look at your address so there are no surprises.

**Coordinate with the HOA if your block has staging guidelines.** Northridge is governed by homeowners associations that maintain the shared spaces and parks, and some associations have placement or staging guidelines for equipment like roll-offs. If your section has rules about where and how long a container can sit, let us know — confirming an approved spot up front keeps the drop clean. Since the box almost always lives on your own driveway, this is usually a quick courtesy check rather than a hurdle.

**Truck access on winding streets.** Northridge is laid out along the curving streets and cul-de-sacs typical of 1990s and early-2000s subdivisions rather than Bowie's older straight grid. That pattern is relevant to delivery — our drivers plan the turnaround for a cul-de-sac or a winding stretch like Northview Drive or Quill Point Drive in advance. Access is open-street, with no gated entrances to clear, so a clear address and a known cross-street keep the delivery moving smoothly to your door near Northridge Park or the Swim & Tennis Club.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning by Route 197 or the BW Parkway. Most Northridge addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours, and an early call often makes the same-day run.
