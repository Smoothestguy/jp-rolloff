## What fits in a 20-yard dumpster

The 20-yard is the workhorse of the residential lineup. It measures 16 ft long by 8 ft wide by 5 ft tall and holds roughly six pickup-truck loads of debris. It comes with a 2-ton (4,000 lb) weight allowance, and in Bowie a typical 20-yard rental runs **$450 to $525, including delivery, pickup, and that tonnage**. No tipping-fee surprises tacked on after the fact.

Five feet of wall height is the part people underestimate. It is low enough that you can still walk debris up and toss it over the side without a ramp, but tall enough to swallow the volume of a real project. That balance is why it lands in the sweet spot for so many Bowie homes — bigger than a quick-cleanup 10 or 15-yard, but not the oversized 30-yard that mostly makes sense for new construction or full structural demo.

## Which Bowie jobs suit the 20-yard

Because the Belair homes are roughly 1,500 to 2,500 square feet and sit on concrete slabs with no basements, the debris from a renovation comes up fast and stays on one level — easy to load, and right in the 20-yard's range. The jobs we see it used for most around Bowie:

- **Whole-house cleanouts.** Clearing a Belair rancher between owners, or after years of accumulation, fills a 20-yard about right without paying for space you won't use.
- **Multi-room remodels.** A kitchen-and-two-baths gut, or a run of bedrooms getting new flooring and drywall, is the classic 20-yard job in a Levitt home.
- **Estate cleanouts.** When a longtime family home in one of the older sections changes hands, the 20-yard handles furniture, boxes, and household debris in a single container.
- **Mid-size roofing tear-offs.** A typical Belair roof tear-off fits comfortably, with the 2-ton allowance covering most single-layer asphalt jobs.

## A word on the Levitt homes and asbestos

This is the one thing that genuinely sets Bowie's older housing apart, and we would rather you hear it before the dumpster shows up. The original Levitt Belair homes are reported to contain asbestos-bearing materials — in the original 9x9 floor tiles, in joint compound, in transite ductwork cast into the slabs, and in asbestos siding. For ordinary cleanouts that is not a concern. But for **roof tear-offs, kitchen and bath gut remodels, and flooring removal in a 1960s Bowie home**, suspect materials may require special handling and cannot simply go in a general-debris roll-off.

We are not an abatement company, and this is not a diagnosis of your specific house. If your project might disturb any of those materials, the safe move is to have them tested or handled by a qualified pro first; once cleared, the clean construction debris is exactly what the 20-yard is for. We would rather have that conversation up front than leave you with a load we cannot take.

## Local logistics: placement, permits, and drive time

**Driveway placement.** Most Bowie jobs put the container right on the driveway, and the Belair lots generally have the room for it. A 20-yard needs roughly 16 feet of straight length plus a few feet of clearance, and an open swing for the rear door. We lay boards under the wheels on request to protect the surface. In the original Old Town Bowie neighborhood (ZIP 20715), where developer Ben Plumb laid out 500-plus small lots around the 1870s railroad junction, the lots are tighter and shaded by mature sycamores and oaks — we will scout placement with you so the truck clears the trees and the container fits the smaller footprint.

**The Bowie permit rule.** In Prince George's County and the City of Bowie, a dumpster sitting entirely on private property — your driveway, lawn, or yard — generally does **not** require a permit. A permit is only needed when the container has to occupy the public right-of-way, meaning the city street. So if it goes on your driveway or an HOA-approved staging spot, you are clear. If the only practical place is the street, you will need a Bowie right-of-way permit, and we will flag that when we look at your address.

**HOAs.** Bowie has a lot of active homeowners and civic associations — across the Belair sections, Fairwood, and beyond, all documented in the City of Bowie's official HOA listing — so placement and screening rules can vary block to block. Many require advance notice but rarely say no. Give your board a heads-up, and we can provide a service confirmation to forward along.

**Drive time.** We are about 25 minutes out in Beltsville and dispatch every morning. Early calls usually make the same-day run; most other Bowie addresses are scheduled inside 24 hours.
