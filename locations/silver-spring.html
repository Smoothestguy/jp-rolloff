Why Silver Spring

Older housing stock means a lot of demo.

Silver Spring has some of the highest renovation activity in Montgomery County. Pre-war bungalows in Woodside and Takoma-adjacent blocks, mid-century in Forest Glen and Kemp Mill, and constant condo turn-overs downtown. We deliver across all of it.

If you're inside the Beltway, expect tighter access — we've got the smaller-bodied trucks needed for narrow Silver Spring driveways and alleys, and our office handles the Montgomery County permit process when needed.

Silver Spring FAQ

Local questions.

Do I need a permit in Silver Spring?
Silver Spring is unincorporated Montgomery County — permits go through MCDPS. Right-of-way placement requires a Public Right-of-Way Permit; private driveways do not. We can walk you through it before delivery.
Will a 20-yard fit in a downtown driveway?
In downtown Silver Spring, often a 10 or 15-yard is the safer fit because of compact lot sizes. We'll confirm with a quick photo or aerial view of your address.
Can you handle pre-1978 lead-paint debris?
Yes — bagged and contained debris from RRP-compliant renovations is acceptable. We can't accept loose painted material from non-compliant projects. Ask us if you're not sure.
